Date: 2026-09-07

## Review execution

The review used a proportionate systematic mapping design with focused synthesis. It searched arXiv as a first-class recent source, accessible publisher and transport indexes, author repositories, an independent 2025 systematic node-model review, and two generations of backward/forward citation snowballing. Thirty-two records entered title/abstract screening; 31 entered and passed R5 full-text assessment. R7 added six eligible studies. The R8 corpus therefore contains 37 studies: 36 primary works and one secondary systematic review.

## Answers to Question A

### 1. Which comparisons and ablations are justified?

Use the four-rung ladder in `baseline_map.md`: matched homogeneous differentiable macro; structured aggregate movement/partial-FIFO queue; lane/storage-aware aggregate; and identity/order-preserving mesoscopic forward reference. The internal ablation chain is the causal core. External implementations or equations should be used only when physics and controls can be matched fairly. Differentiable hybrid comparison is deferred, not mandatory.

### 2. How do the three mesoscopic components relate to literature?

All are literature-backed adaptations and synthesis rather than isolated inventions. The ordered ledger combines multi-commodity semantics with the identity/order precedent of individual LTM while retaining divisible mass. The local zone combines multiresolution locality, lane-resolved state, and physical storage in a cumulative-rank representation that is not directly found in the corpus. The service operator combines generic node admissibility, continuous signal service, lane allocation, storage, and FIFO/order concepts.

### 3. Which works are most similar?

The nearest set is complementary: Wright for partial FIFO; COS for continuous signal service; Gong–Bliemer–Raadsen for lane allocation; de Souza for identity-preserving LTM; Wei for turn queues; Liu–Chang and Gao for lane blockage/physical queues; Andelfinger and Son for differentiable rich-state simulation; Seo for differentiable LTM; LIFT and the SUMO LTM alternative for LWR-consistent mesoscopic behavior; and LNCTM for continuous commodities.

### 4. What gap does the project target?

It targets the untested middle ground between continuous scalable differentiation and realized mesoscopic semantic order/local accessibility. The question is whether only selected structural discreteness can be retained while mass dynamics remain continuous, and whether doing so changes decisions usefully beyond established aggregate lane/movement models.

### 5. How does the literature support that the gap remains open?

The literature establishes every surrounding ingredient separately but, within this corpus, not their complete combination or its external gradient-usefulness test. This is evidence for a plausible open intersection, not proof that no equivalent work exists anywhere. Citation snowballing materially strengthened the conclusion by adding older analytic differentiable stochastic DNL and surrogate-DNL branches without finding exact continuous realized order.

## Conclusions changed from the initial targeted audit

1. Differentiable macro DNL is historically broader than recent AD/LTM work: smooth stochastic queue-distribution models were published in 2011 and extended into stochastic LTM networks.
2. Differentiability can also be obtained through a learned smooth DNL metamodel, making the distinction between simulator derivatives and surrogate derivatives essential.
3. Lane-state precedent is broader than the initial archive: endogenous macroscopic lane-flow equilibrium and older multiclass physical-queue DNL add relevant ancestry.
4. The recent SUMO discrete-time LTM preprint is distinct from the peer-reviewed event-based LIFT model, not a duplicate report.
5. The hybrid differentiable baseline remains relevant literature but is not part of the user-approved minimum experiment ladder.

## Remaining limitations

- This is not an exhaustive subscription-database export or dual-reviewer systematic review.
- Seven corpus records are preprints and require publication-status rechecking before submission.
- Citation indexes have uneven coverage for very recent papers.
- R8 recommends comparison logic, not implementation feasibility approval inside the simulator milestones.
- Question B—the task and experimental formulation—remains explicitly outside this review.
