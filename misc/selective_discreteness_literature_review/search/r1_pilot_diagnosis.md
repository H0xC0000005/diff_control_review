# Phase R1 search-pilot diagnosis

Date: 2026-09-07  
Status: Passed; no scope amendment required

## Result

The four-family search architecture reaches the intended literature neighborhood. Exact-title verification recovered every sentinel family already known to the audit. Concept searches independently recovered the principal differentiable-simulation, turn-level queue, lane-group blockage, individual-tracking LTM, and mesoscopic signal-optimization families.

## Precision finding

Generic arXiv/web queries containing `signal`, `network`, or `traffic` are noisy because those words occur in unrelated physics, communications, and computing work. The execution rule is therefore:

- use road-traffic qualifiers such as `vehicle`, `intersection`, `dynamic network loading`, `traffic signal`, `LTM`, or `CTM`;
- use exact methodological phrases for narrow families;
- exclude controller-only ML papers unless simulator differentiation or relevant traffic-state modeling is central;
- use exact-title and citation expansion to recover older foundational work.

## Recall finding

Sentinel recall is satisfactory across the combined process, not necessarily within each individual query. This is expected because foundational LTM/node papers do not use modern “differentiable simulation” terminology, while recent differentiable papers do not necessarily use “mesoscopic.”

Recovered sentinel groups:

- LTM and generic node foundation: Yperman; Tampère et al.;
- FIFO/signal foundation: Wright et al.; Han et al.; COS;
- structured queue/lane models: Raadsen et al.; Wei et al.; Liu and Chang;
- mesoscopic models: de Souza et al.; DTALite; MESCOP;
- differentiable simulators: Andelfinger; Son et al.; Seo.

## Material new candidates from the pilot

- *Network Traffic Control Based on a Mesoscopic Dynamic Flow Model* (2016): mesoscopic horizontal-queue model embedded in green/offset optimization;
- *Optimization of Actuated Traffic Signal Plans Using a Mesoscopic Traffic Simulation* (2020): continuation of the MESCOP task family;
- *Structure-free model-based predictive signal control: A sensitivity analysis on a corridor with spillback* (2023): finite-storage macroscopic predictive-control comparator;
- *Traffic Signal Optimization for Oversaturated Urban Networks: Queue Growth Equalization* (2015): spillback-aware signal-task comparator;
- *Simulating link-level interrupted flow traffic dynamics and the comparison between different models for urban road networks* (2026): peer-reviewed LIFT method, distinct from the earlier arXiv record.

These candidates do not invalidate the review question, but they further narrow any contribution based merely on queue/spillback-aware signal optimization.

## Decision

Proceed to Phase R2 corpus construction. No institutional retrieval request is currently necessary because primary metadata and several open manuscripts are available for the closest papers. Full-text access will be reassessed during R5/R6.
