The most material recovery is Osorio, Flötteröd, and Bierlaire (2011), which establishes a smooth stochastic differentiable DNL mapping with queue distributions and spillback. Its descendants add stochastic link-boundary dependence and a stochastic network LTM. These papers correct an underdeveloped part of the initial map: differentiable macro traffic modeling predates modern AD implementations and includes analytic probabilistic formulations.

Shiomi et al. (2015) adds endogenous macroscopic lane-flow equilibrium, Bliemer (2007) adds continuous multiclass queues and spillback, and Song et al. (2018) adds a deliberately smooth Kriging DNL surrogate. None retains exact realized semantic order in continuous traffic mass or evaluates externally transferred control gradients.

Later papers refined stochastic node/link distributions, metamodel resolution, or movement exit capacity but did not alter the target identity/order/differentiation intersection. The approved stop rule is therefore met for this proportionate review.
