## Comparison evidence and limits

The [baseline matrix](../../synthesis/baseline_realization_matrix.md#L6) fixes a common physical task and prohibits privileged state. Its representation table separates homogeneous macro, movement queues, lane/storage aggregate and selective order, while R1/R2 are forward references. Its [fairness rules](../../synthesis/baseline_realization_matrix.md#L48) prohibit held-out blockage fitting and separate representation from optimizer comparisons.

The [adoption matrix](../../synthesis/literature_adoption_matrix.md#L34) still requires one principal B1 and a defensible B2 allocation/storage composition. The [formal pair definition](../../synthesis/experiment_formulation_formal.tex#L186) matches aggregate quantities but does not itself show equality of every rival's admitted history. Those two observations support the proposed UR-G4 scope. They do not establish that every aggregate rival can infer order, or that any particular pair is indistinguishable.

The example of different movement arrival histories is a logical counterexample to the claim that equal current totals alone prove indistinguishability. It is not a constructed V1/V3 fixture. Under loading from empty, the later pair study must check the preparation histories actually admitted to each chosen rival. If those histories reconstruct relevant order, say so; do not suppress them. If a proposed realization explicitly stores exact ordered lane/access state, its baseline role must be made explicit instead of calling it an order-free rival.

Responsive allocation does not change the agreed fairness objective. It clarifies which quantities can be matched as inputs and which are model outputs. Match common exogenous assumptions; do not enforce identical endogenous lane shares or realized access merely to preserve an older fixed-intent description. A model-specific choice law may legitimately differ, but its capability and assumptions must be reported. No weaker B2 or extra baseline is selected here.

[P9/P10](../../synthesis/project_impact_matrix.md#L24) retain external perturbation evidence before full optimization and a common effective-service-to-evaluator mapping. They do not provide that mapping. Its timing, population, source-wait measurement, seeds and mismatch policy remain UR-C6-Q1 work. No software/API availability or present license/version claim is newly verified in this pass.

## Clearance and derivative evidence

[D2](../../synthesis/experiment_formulation.md#L34) states a constant candidate allocation during the control/demand-release interval, followed by the same fixed clearance policy. The proposal reads that fixed continuation as independent of the candidate allocation. Under that retained default, there is no direct candidate-service change after the handoff, but the state carried into clearance still depends on the earlier allocation. That sensitivity cannot be detached at the handoff. This reading is made explicit for review; the numerical continuation is not selected here, and D2 retains its default status.

[D3](../../synthesis/experiment_formulation.md#L40) and the [formal objective](../../synthesis/experiment_formulation_formal.tex#L60) count source plus network mass through completion. [D7](../../synthesis/experiment_formulation.md#L64) leaves numerical values for later calibration/freeze. This pass therefore proposes a conditional common-horizon strategy without inventing a horizon, clearance cutoff, perturbation size or numerical tolerance.

Candidate A and Candidate B under UR-G5 are ways to compute the same complete objective under the same intervention, rather than two different traffic objectives. When the common horizon covers complete clearance in the relevant local neighborhood, extending an empty trajectory adds zero time. A completion-based implementation still needs a sound derivative of its selected dynamics and timing; a changing integer stop index is not that derivation. No assertion is made that a finite cap guarantees clearance or that the two implementations automatically agree numerically.

The [formal derivative contract](../../synthesis/experiment_formulation_formal.tex#L77) and [recursion](../../synthesis/experiment_formulation_formal.tex#L109) condition the derivative on feasible physical allocations, fixed scenarios and stable signatures. The [M1 differentiation contract](../../../../context_snapshots/diff_minimal_meso/docs/milestones/milestone_1_ordered_route_mass_ledger.md#L142) and [M2 G6](../../../../context_snapshots/diff_minimal_meso/temp_content/milestone_2_per_lane_interaction_zone/phase_B_revision.md#L34) preserve continuous mass/timing derivatives while treating labels and structural changes separately. Continuous responsive allocation shares, transferred masses and resulting eligibility/history effects must therefore remain in the chosen path; the actual local response remains to be derived after the laws are specified.

- [objectives.py](../../../../context_snapshots/diff_minimal_meso/src/diff_minimal_meso/objectives.py#L22) sums source availability and link occupancy over the supplied horizon; it does not establish complete urban clearance or supply lane/access laws.

Its numeric simplex assumptions and tolerances cannot be treated as a frozen urban experiment contract.
- [Objective tests](../../../../context_snapshots/diff_minimal_meso/tests/test_objectives.py) and [gradient tests](../../../../context_snapshots/diff_minimal_meso/tests/test_gradients.py) were inspected, not executed. They are accepted macro checks, not new evidence for this proposal.

Internal derivative correctness and external usefulness remain separate under the [accepted evidence sequence](../../synthesis/experiment_formulation.md#L70). Boundary cases stay in forward reporting. No smoothing, surrogate gradient or zero-response remedy is adopted.
