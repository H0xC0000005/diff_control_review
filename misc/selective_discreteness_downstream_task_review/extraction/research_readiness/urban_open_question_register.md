## How much should this review establish?

### UR-G0 — what makes the methodology ready to explain and test?

We need a reader to understand what the model predicts without inventing a traffic rule. That is different from proving that its predictions are accurate or its gradients improve decisions.

**Candidate A — a complete model with focused checks.** State the traffic laws and where they apply, explain why their flows are feasible, and use neutral/coupled examples to check the joined model. Establish a unique result or a stated selection rule where needed. This is a practical foundation for later testing; its conclusions remain limited to the admitted traffic family.

**Candidate B — add a general mathematical guarantee.** Also prove existence, uniqueness or stated selection, feasibility and derivative regularity across the full admitted family. This offers stronger assurance, but may become a substantial proof program or require a narrower domain. It still cannot establish real-world accuracy or useful control directions by itself.

I provisionally favor A, adding a specific proof where the chosen law needs it. Neither a broad theorem nor a formal question template should obscure the traffic explanation.

## Traffic world and behavior: what reaches the junction and who can move?

### UR-G1 — how do we obtain the queues present when control begins?

The same approach count can mean traffic waiting at the stop line or still traveling toward it. That difference changes who can use extra service immediately. Urban names initial queues, but their construction still needs connecting to the existing empty-start model.

**Candidate A — prescribe the queued state directly.** Describe traffic already on physical links, including route/lane composition and enough history to establish readiness. This can target difficult queue patterns, but adds a consistency problem: arbitrary occupancies do not provide valid propagation histories. M1 deferred this capability rather than rejecting it scientifically. It remains deferred after feedback 3.

**Candidate B — create the queue by loading from empty.** Use a common preparation period so traffic and travel histories form together. This reuses the accepted origin and may supply every required case. Its limit is the range of queues the chosen preparation can produce.

The user accepts B for development and early validation. Consider A only after later experiment evidence establishes a need and a new decision authorizes it. Preparation is fixed while the candidate allocation varies; otherwise the control question and its gradient change. Its relation to complete trip-time accounting must be explicit. This chooses the present initialization approach without claiming every eventual queue stratum is already covered or closing all of UR-G1.

### UR-G2 — what does an average service allocation let a shared queue do?

Half the budget for left turns and half for through traffic does not say when a front left-turn block releases traffic behind it. A capacity limit alone also does not decide how movements share one lane or downstream room.

**Candidate A — a coupled continuous-service law.** Service is available at allocated average rates, with order, access and shared physical resources determining its use. This preserves the accepted urban simplification. It still needs a defensible rule for obstruction and unused service; it cannot promise every red/green effect.

**Candidate B — explicit service opportunities in time.** Give the same budget a declared timing pattern, so a front block waits for its movement's opportunity. This makes timing-related obstruction explicit, but adds an assumption that the accepted averaged task omitted. Its adoption would need that change agreed and applied consistently across comparisons.

Feedback 4 explicitly selects A's effective average-service meaning for this stage; B's active phase clock is deferred. Q3's broad choice is settled. The traffic law must still say when departures free room and how movements share resources. Physical lane capacity, allocated movement service and realized discharge remain distinct; M0 retains its valid common-exposure domain. Phase-duration sensitivities are not inherently zero: switching-time effects can matter. Average service simplifies the current model without guaranteeing nonzero realized-flow or objective gradients.

### UR-G3 — how much of the real lane order should determine behavior?

Exact metadata can be stored without being used exactly. We must choose what order actually constrains access and service. P8 left this open for M3; the present authorization allows the methodological choice to be resolved in this review, with implementation remaining separate.

**Candidate A — follow the selected lane order exactly.** Traffic passes only as permitted by the physical-access law and its actual eligible order. This gives a clear obstruction reference, at the cost of more detailed state and changing active traffic fronts. Exact order is useful only if the physical access and service meanings are sound.

**Candidate B — permit declared local mixing.** Preserve order between mass groups while relaxing some order within them. This may reduce complexity and tests whether coarse order suffices. It may also let a turner pass a blocker; a small mixing region does not guarantee a small objective or gradient error. Feedback 3 defers this direction for the current development path. Its possible future use would need a new decision and explicit conservation, reconstruction and bias evidence; it is not declared impossible to implement.

**Candidate C — organize the traffic model around passage times.** Describe when continuous ordered streams enter, become eligible and leave, rather than letting bin removal organize service. This may clarify timing, but it still needs a concrete geometry/access/resource law and can be difficult to formulate. It could implement either A or B, so it is a separate modeling direction, not a third information level or a ready model.

The agreed direction retains exact incoming processing order and realized lane order, without an obligatory relaxed-order study. Allocation and downstream dynamics determine actual lane occupation. Feedback 4 separates logical completion of assignment from physical zone entry: assigned mass may wait upstream. This does not create one global discharge order across independent lanes or delete within-lane sequence. Screen C only where it supplies a concrete compatible exact-order law. The waiting-target commitment choice is recorded below.

### UR-G3-Q1 — when does upstream waiting traffic become committed to a lane?

**Candidate A — preserve independent access while processing in order.** Keep the blocked package waiting and consider later traffic in a stable sequence, admitting it only where physical access is independently possible. This avoids making one destination close unrelated lanes. M2 uses this idea for precommitted independent lanes. Free room alone cannot justify bypassing a real shared-queue obstruction, so a bay extension still needs an access law.

**Candidate B — one physical feeder queue imposes strict front blocking.** Admit only a prefix: a blocked front prevents later entry. This is coherent where later traffic cannot reach another lane before the front moves. It can also create artificial cross-lane blocking if imposed solely by ledger order where paths really are independently usable. It differs from merely retaining an exact incoming record.

**Candidate C — commit at logical allocation, then wait upstream for admission (former default; superseded for new unadmitted mass by feedback 10).** Responsive allocation assigns lane targets/shares in incoming order. The resulting waiting pieces retain those targets until physical entry. Their waiting records are views of upstream mass, not extra physical lane buffers. This reads the user's staged proposal consistently with the existing fixed-commitment rule. The concrete proposal must establish compatibility with that seam. Its limitation is that waiting pieces cannot revise their choice as lane conditions change.

**Candidate D — keep upstream targets provisional until admission (deferred).** Desired shares could respond again while mass waits; actual entry would fix realized lane membership. This allows more pre-entry adaptation. It also requires rules for reconstructing waiting order, priority and access without turning metadata changes into free physical movement, and explicit reconsideration of the inherited precommitment meaning. It is not selected or required for the present path. Actual resident traffic would not be reassigned under this direction.

Retain C as the default rather than reopen fixed commitment without a reason. This is a documented reading of the staged proposal, not approval of a particular allocation/access equation. An already desired lane can be reused only while valid under the chosen policy; the shortcut cannot invent or silently freeze a provisional target.
