## Candidate definitions and their practical limits

`G.internal` gives the zone a common feeder section and two branch sections. Actual section occupancy is physical; a bay target can be carried while the mass remains in the feeder. This explicitly extends the simplest upstream-only pending-view interpretation. It requires current-section and committed-target meanings to be distinct, but no retargeting or microscopic lane-change rule. Storage belongs to disjoint physical sections, while labels/grids remain views. `G.mouth` avoids that internal ownership extension only on its restricted single-feeder layout, with the refinement-domain change recorded.

The internal prototype proposes assignment at first physical eligibility for entry into the refined feeder, before room is necessarily available. Existing valid commitments are reused. This choice location is a proposal, not an inherited fixed rule. Internal sections do not expand the global route sequence or advance its position at the bay mouth. A boundary-aligned comparison must hold that physical choice location/information fixed or disclose the additional behavioral change; matching road lengths alone is not equivalence.

The original `L.queue` prototype used an affine pressure cost with queued/waiting mass plus the new allocation. The amendment restores progressive load accounting across source packets and selects ideal queue-load balancing for the first version. Its old travel-time/storage normalization is not silently adopted for arbitrary heterogeneous lane classes. The PDF keeps those equations as opening prototypes and updates their decision status. Unselected `L.work` assumes service knowledge; its zero-service extension is not required now. No action-domain restriction or epsilon is introduced.

For identical adjacent packets with the same route state/legal alternatives and no intervening event, exact progressive balancing can be grouped without changing final lane totals. Heterogeneous movement order must not be erased. Earlier assigned waiting load remains in the later packet's queue estimate; it is not extra physical mass or storage. Per-lane order/route identity is maintained alongside the movement grid. These are existing conservation/order obligations, not new user questions.

The earlier packet treated target chronology as a required choice. Feedback 6 corrects that premise for one homogeneous source block at an accessible fork: `A.joint` may return lane amounts together without inventing target precedence. The strict single-target feeder-prefix equation remains a restricted case for genuinely precommitted ordered targets, not a rule to impose by arbitrary concatenation on new splits. Homogeneity preserves persistent route state, movement, legal alternatives and applicable commitments. No distinct source blocks are mixed, no later block bypasses a blocked earlier block, and realized lanes retain exact order.

The equal-scale static example admits bay/other additions 0.3/0.5 into storage 0.5 each from initial 0.2/0, leaving 0.2 physically upstream. Fixed intended totals 0.4/0.6 would retain pending target masses 0.1/0.1. A parent block with fixed pending shares and single-target admission children is a possible conservative representation; it is not an approved M2 ingress replacement. Dynamic partial admission, arbitrary-packet invariance and assignment-location compatibility still require formulation. The user example establishes a behavioral requirement, not every part of that dynamic law.

The service comparison first considers already-ready resident mass and nonbinding receiving. Its two exact local laws have explicit time/budget consumption and partial prefixes. Travel/eligibility profiles must be composed when arrivals become eligible within an interval; the mathematical note states a causal clock construction. New admissions do not receive same-section service in that same update. Both zero-rate heads and downstream-blocked heads can have valid zero local responses.

The local clock also needs explicit reconciliation with the inherited boundary-time cumulative-history interpolation. Different within-step departure profiles cannot be inserted into a supposedly unchanged backbone without checking sending/eligibility consistency. This packet does not claim that the new local section network and time profiles already reproduce the accepted macro implementation.

Feedback 7 selects green-weighted receiving across inbound legs, equal within a leg, with feasible demand caps and reuse. A leg receives its weight once, rather than once per lane. The relevant movement green remains a service bound and is not multiplied into allocated discharge again. This settles the receiving-share principle in the present discussion; the full mixed-lane operator is not opened here. Source/receiving and elapsed-rate compatibility remain later construction obligations, not an additional open policy question now.

## What this discussion does not select

No lane permission is expanded, no current-node route mapping changed, no general nonempty initializer added, no local mixing activated, and no actual phase clock, cycle-length objective, regularization or numeric cutoff is introduced. Fixed routes, continuous mass, per-lane local order, causal propagation and complete TST remain. Original-series access through a rival's declared macro conversion remains allowed. Particular rivals, exact observation protocols and richer-evaluator mappings are separate concrete questions; they are not silently resolved by these B3 candidate definitions.

The numerical examples are hand-auditable illustrations. They are not traffic experiments, calibration results, gradient-validation evidence or approved numerical defaults. The candidate PDF derives local responses, but empirical/useful-gradient claims still need the accepted separate evidence.
