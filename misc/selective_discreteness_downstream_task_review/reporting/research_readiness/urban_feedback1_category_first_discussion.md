Historical response. Feedback 2 subsequently replaces the question-statement template used below with short conceptual explanations. The category-led arrangement remains accepted. Read the [rewritten current proposal](../../temp_content/research_readiness/phase_B_categories_1_2_principle_proposal.md) and [current review plan](../../temp_content/research_readiness/phase_A_proposal.md) for the live discussion.

**Yes to both suggestions.** We can make the logical categories the main units of discussion, and begin each with its core traffic question, behavioral meaning and principles. The detailed question register can serve as a completeness check behind that discussion. It should not require you to reconstruct an unfamiliar procedure from isolated technical choices.

There is one distinction to preserve: **agreeing a category's principles and completing its model-specific answers are different achievements.** The categories are separate intellectual topics, but their final answers are not all independent. A fair comparison needs actual predictors; a derivative needs the actual traffic laws; precise equation credit needs the equations that were selected.

## 1. What can be settled category by category?

| Category and core question | What we can discuss and agree at the principle level | What needs the model to be specified |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Traffic world:** What traffic is represented, where can it be, and what makes it advance or wait? | The physical interpretation of mass, routes, lanes, initial traffic and service; which assumptions are retained and which need a decision | Compatible initial histories, exact physical accounts and the timing of coupled transfers |
| **2. Traffic behavior:** What lets a turner reach the bay, what obstructs it, and how can service remove that obstruction? | Which traffic distinctions the model should represent, how retained or relaxed order is interpreted, and what each candidate mechanism is trying to explain | Concrete access, admission, discharge, storage and residual-state rules |
| **3. Fair comparison:** What must a simpler model be allowed to know and explain before retained order can receive credit? | Common task/action/objective, legitimate observations and inference, strong simpler rivals, separation of information and optimizer comparisons | Selected B1/B2 equations, whether a particular pair is indistinguishable to each rival, independent reference coverage and the common external mapping |
| **4. Sensitivity:** Which consequences of a service reallocation must be included before its gradient represents complete TST? | Physical control meaning, what remains fixed, complete accounting, conditional derivative claims and separate usefulness evidence | Local derivatives of the selected access/service laws, their exceptional cases and the joined clearance sensitivity |
| **5. Explanation and credit:** What should a reader understand about the traffic model and its scientific sources? | Traffic-first exposition, familiar notation, adjacent source/adaptation credit and inspectable supporting material | Exact equation-level credit and the final coherent report |

Categories 1 and 2 are best treated as **one shared traffic discussion**, with two distinct questions. Assumptions describe the traffic world; behavioral laws describe what happens in that world. A proposed access law can reveal a missing assumption, while a chosen assumption can rule out a law. We can work through that relationship using one road example, without forcing artificial independence.

Categories 3, 4 and 5 can each have a focused principle discussion. Their applied conclusions consume the specified traffic model, so they cannot all be declared fully complete in advance. For example, we can agree now that a rival may use lawful arrival-history inference; whether it can distinguish a particular order pair remains open until its state and law are defined. Likewise, deciding that all clearance time belongs in the objective does not yet derive the sensitivity of an unspecified access rule.

This permits modular discussion and substantial agreement. It avoids treating an unfinished dependent answer as a closed category.

## 2. How principles should lead to detailed answers

Take the familiar case: a left-turn bay has spare room, while a turner is behind through traffic on the approach. Begin with the traffic meaning: does spare room establish access? What physical obstruction does the model represent? What would changing through service do to that obstruction? What information would distinguish this queue from one with the same movement totals but a different order?

We then compare the viable modeling ideas at that level, explaining what each preserves, predicts and leaves unresolved. An exact-order proposal and a bounded order-relaxation proposal need different behavioral commitments. Conservation alone cannot select between them, because both can account for all mass. Neither policy is selected by this example.

Once the user accepts the relevant principles, I can work out consequences that actually follow: consistent notation, the necessary state/flow definitions, an exact transcription of an approved law, its conservation identities and its derivative calculations. These become a concrete, reviewable specification. A detail that still admits scientifically different answers remains a decision. For example, selecting an order relaxation, deciding when discharge opens receiving space, or admitting extra information to a rival cannot be inferred merely from a broad agreement to model blockage fairly.

Each category's discussion can therefore stay focused on a small number of core choices. The result should state the agreed traffic meaning, the remaining genuine choices and which detailed questions have become consequences. Accepted principles remain binding inputs to the later specification; they do not silently approve every compatible formula.

## 3. Recommended discussion arrangement

Use a **category-led, principles-first discussion**, followed by category-led specification. Start with a short reminder of the already accepted task and common requirements. Then discuss the shared traffic world and behavior, the comparison principles, the sensitivity principles, and the explanation requirements as coherent topics. Keep one physical example available throughout.

Close and consolidate the shared scientific contracts before formally settling dependent component questions. If the principle discussion leaves a global scientific choice ambiguous, make that specific choice explicit before treating the global contract as closed. A broad slogan is not sufficient closure. This preserves the selected global-assumptions/interfaces-first decision and the repository's global-before-local requirement.

After that consolidation, prepare the detailed specifications in coherent blocks: the physical model, the concrete comparison/reference realizations, the actual sensitivities, and the final report. Collect source/adaptation records while each law is specified so attribution does not require restarting the traffic discussion. Remaining returns to a category should be deliberate: show the exact dependency or newly exposed choice and reopen only what it affects.

This entails a small number of planned returns from principles to their application. Fully completing every category in one visit would require its inputs to be settled already. The aim is to reduce fragmented context switching while retaining honest dependencies.

The existing S2–S7 sequence remains a map of required work and acceptance dependencies. It need not dictate the user's conversation agenda. Similarly, the UR question IDs remain useful for checking that nothing was missed; the reader-facing discussion starts with the core question and traffic behavior, with IDs attached afterward.

## 4. Smallest viable arrangements and tradeoffs

**Problem statement.** Which discussion arrangement gives coherent category closure while respecting the dependencies needed for a complete urban methodology?

**Assumed model-chain function.** The arrangement governs how shared assumptions, physical laws, comparisons and sensitivities are understood and approved. It changes the review presentation and grouping; it does not select traffic behavior.

**Core gap.** The earlier inventory organizes decisions precisely but exposes many technical consequences before their traffic purpose is familiar. Logical categories improve understanding, yet their model-specific results have dependencies that a fully independent closure rule could conceal.

**Candidate A — category-led principles, then explicit specification (accepted discussion format).** Discuss each core question through behavior, competing modeling ideas and accepted constraints. Consolidate the shared global decisions, then complete the detailed category/component specifications under them. Record which answers follow directly and which still require a scientific choice.

- **Pros:** matches the requested logical learning order; reduces fragmented context switching; gives later details a clear justification; retains a complete audit trail and global-first scientific control.
- **Cons:** some categories need a later application visit; a principle agreement must be distinguished from a fully specified method.
- **Dependencies/consequences:** all shared global contracts close before formal component-local resolution. New non-equivalent choices return to their owner; they are not silently inferred.

**Candidate B — complete each category in full before moving to the next.** Prepare its complete method, equations and evidence specification together, importing explicitly settled requirements from other categories. Follow the dependency order, normally combining categories 1–2 before concrete comparisons, sensitivity and reporting.

- **Pros:** each completed block is tangible and self-contained; a reader can keep one category in view through its detailed consequences.
- **Cons:** this is feasible only with sufficiently complete inputs. If shared comparison or derivative requirements are still open, an apparently finished physical block can need revision. It can also demand technical detail before its conceptual choices are understood.
- **Dependencies/consequences:** shared global requirements still need prior closure; finalizing a category cannot override them. Where an essential input is open, the category remains open rather than receiving a misleading completion label.

**Candidate C — use the technical dependency list as the discussion agenda.** Retain the earlier explicit global-question order followed by the component-question order as the main reader-facing sequence.

- **Pros:** makes ownership, prerequisite decisions and implementation handoffs easy to track; supports systematic completeness checks.
- **Cons:** has the context-switching and prerequisite-knowledge burden identified in the feedback; the reader must assemble the traffic story from technical items.
- **Dependencies/consequences:** the same scientific obligations remain. This order can continue as an internal check without governing the discussion presentation.

**Recommendation and acceptance.** A was recommended and is now accepted. B and C remain the considered, unselected dialogue alternatives; their useful features do not replace A. The accepted format retains the physical task, traffic invariants, evidence hierarchy and global-first scientific precedence.
