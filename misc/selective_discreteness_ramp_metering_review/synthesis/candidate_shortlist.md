## Core decision

**Problem statement:** does the current evidence justify further work toward a diagnostic ramp task, or should it be deferred?

**Assumed model-chain function:** ramp release affects merge admission and local/aggregate state, which affects time or throughput. Selective state must change a scientifically justified part of that chain beyond the aggregate comparator.

**Core gap:** the aggregate control task is plausible, but the necessary in-scope selective-information contrast is unverified. Availability of node interfaces or a successful controller does not fill that gap.

### A — plain aggregate ramp/merge control

Use one controlled ramp, an aggregate merge and finite queue accounting. RM0001/RM0002 provide task lineage; a strong aggregate model and conventional feedback belong among comparisons.

**Pros:** smallest physical task; clear action and queue accounting; strong first-order precedents.

**Cons:** no demonstrated need for order/access refinement beyond aggregates; may primarily test optimizer or merge implementation.

**Dependency/consequence:** retain as a possible reference case, not the accepted second scientific task. No full formulation is warranted solely by the present evidence.

### B — bounded local merge-access mechanism audit

Before formulation, investigate one finite local merge/acceleration-zone candidate in which fixed lane/route permissions or realized access might distinguish aggregate-matched states. Define why that information can influence discharge without microscopic trajectories, new speed dynamics or an added capacity-drop law. Compare against a capable lane/storage aggregate model. Require a source-backed mechanism and independent reference concept.

**Pros:** directly targets the unresolved project question; may retain the shared backbone and planned M3 ownership; a negative result remains informative.

**Cons:** diagnosticity and compatibility are not yet proven; may expose a need for lateral dynamics or additional geometry.

**Dependency/consequence:** continuation is a narrow feasibility search, not RM4 task formulation. Any added exit/lane restriction must have a reason beyond manufacturing a contrast. Stop if the necessary mechanism violates strict compatibility or remains unsupported after this bounded check.

### C — defer ramp metering as a parallel task

Close with aggregate feasibility acknowledged but insufficient diagnostic evidence. Revisit only when a specific compatible mechanism or M3 design supplies a stronger starting point.

**Pros:** honors minimality and prevents an application-driven expansion; preserves effort for the already accepted TSC task.

**Cons:** may defer a viable local mechanism that the small corpus missed; establishes no universal impossibility.

**Dependency/consequence:** does not require project changes and does not turn the aggregate task into an accepted benchmark by default.

**Provisional recommendation:** do not promote A or begin RM4 now. B is the smallest meaningful continuation if the user wants to resolve the remaining possibility; C is defensible if minimal effort is the overriding priority. The current evidence supports neither an unconditional yes nor a universal no.
