# RM3-B: local mechanism and added value beyond TSC

Date: 2026-09-14. Evidence supplement; provisional at the follow-up user checkpoint.

## Finding

The evidence now supports a more discriminating conclusion than the initial single-ramp screen. There are credible first-order ways to represent additional freeway phenomena, including node-level capacity reduction and route-composition memory. They weaken blanket objections to compatibility, but also strengthen aggregate alternatives to selective order. The strongest remaining project-relevant candidate is an upstream ramp controlling arrivals into a downstream route-sensitive restriction. Its possible new contribution is testing **indirect control of a local mechanism through arrival history**, rather than direct allocation of that mechanism's service capacity. That is a proposed experiment logic, not a result demonstrated by the retrieved papers.

We still do not have sufficient grounds either to accept a fully formulated second task or to declare all ramp tasks infeasible. The evidence does justify rejecting some specific adoption paths under the current scope and focusing the remaining decision on one concrete candidate.

## New primary evidence

**RM0009 — Salehi, Somers and Seibold, _Off-Ramp Coupling Conditions Devoid of Spurious Blocking and Re-Routing_ (2017 preprint).** The authors show that full FIFO can overblock through traffic at a congested exit, whereas naive non-FIFO splitting can violate intended route totals. Their FIFOQ construction adds minimal queue-composition memory; §2 explicitly allows triangular fundamental diagrams. This supports a shared first-order framework and a strong aggregate-memory alternative. Their vertical queue is not a spatially resolved finite exit pocket or an exact-order ledger; it cannot simply stand in for either. Inspected local PDF pp. 1–6, including §§2–4. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.00529).

**RM0010 — Jin, Gan and Lebacque, _A Kinematic Wave Theory of Capacity Drop_ (2013 preprint; related 2015 journal paper).** The inspected model retains continuous link fundamental diagrams and introduces a discontinuous boundary flux at a lane drop. Its drop magnitude is exogenous. This directly qualifies the earlier impression that capacity-drop modeling necessarily requires an independently evolving speed or hysteresis state. But it is a new boundary law, not an effect automatically generated by the current ledger, and the demonstrated lane-drop formulation is not itself an approved ramp-merge operator. Inspected §2 and local PDF pp. 4–6. [Inspected preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.2660).

**RM0011 — Laval and Daganzo, _Multi-Lane Hybrid Traffic Flow Model: Quantifying the Impacts of Lane-Changing Maneuvers on Traffic Flow_ (2004 working paper).** The mechanism combines kinematic-wave lanes with bounded-acceleration moving particles and lane-changing demand. This provides a concrete physical reason why some freeway effects require more than route labels and mass order. Direct adoption conflicts with the current prohibition on microscopic motion and integer physical transfers; this does not prove all representations of the phenomenon require those features. Inspected §§1–3 through the official repository PDF. [Repository](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/8w70q261).

Publisher discovery also identified _Traffic Control of Urban Corridor Systems with Weaving Effects_ (2012, DOI 10.1016/j.trc.2012.06.005), whose abstract describes flow-ratio-dependent operational capacity and equilibrium-constrained control. This is only a screening lead: neither a lane-local state mechanism nor project-compatible gradients were established from its abstract. [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0968090X12000873).

## Why the last candidate may add value

The closed TSC suite optimizes local effective service: control changes the service capacity of movement groups at the focal intersection. A ramp meter upstream of a route-sensitive restriction would change admissions and the arrival history reaching a restriction whose local service/supply is held fixed. If the combined stream's route/order and accessibility affect later discharge, this tests whether retained information is useful under an indirect intervention with propagation delay.

This is reviewer inference from the architecture and source mechanisms. A longer link or a different traffic label alone would not make the question distinct. The candidate must demonstrate that the action-to-state dependency and the attainable richer-reference contrast differ materially from merely replaying V3. General robustness under a second geometry is supplementary value, not an automatic reason to build a second task.

Minimum conceptual construction: one controlled on-ramp, a mainline, and one downstream off-ramp with fixed receiving restriction. Keep exit destinations fixed; do not optimize routes or add a second controller. Begin with enough separation to avoid assuming a microscopic weaving zone. The extra exit is justified because it creates distinct downstream service eligibility that the single common-destination merge lacks. This is a candidate geometry for approval, not a frozen scenario or an assertion that arbitrary fixed lane commitments reproduce real freeway behavior.

The baseline challenge is stronger than homogeneous FIFO versus the ledger. Use a route-aware/composition-memory comparator and a lane/storage aggregate comparator, with matched physical storage. FIFOQ is a methodological warning and possible comparison ingredient, not permission to replace physical space with a vertical queue. Its original limitations must remain visible. An order contrast that disappears under a credible aggregate-memory baseline would support sufficiency, not failure of the experiment.

## Conditions required before promotion

1. Show the entry/merge and downstream access operators can preserve conservation, route identity and the shared backbone under an M3-consistent service resolution. Do not silently model physical weaving as a metadata permutation.
2. Define what is matched in a scenario pair and where information differs: dynamic route totals alone are not proof of a need for finer order. Give the aggregate-memory and lane/storage competitors the information their definitions permit.
3. Establish a source-backed or independently justified service contrast and a plausible evaluator mapping. No effect sizes or useful gradients have been verified in this pass.
4. Explain the distinct inference relative to V1–V3: control of arrivals into fixed downstream service, rather than direct local service allocation. If this reduces to a redundant TSC example, do not promote it.
5. Resolve these conceptual conditions before detailed controller, numerical geometry or calibration design. If an essential condition requires a backbone/scope change, return for the separate compatibility decision already required by the user.

## Evidence against specific candidates versus a global no

There is affirmative evidence that direct bounded-acceleration particle models exceed scope, and that adding an exogenous capacity-drop boundary law does not by itself demonstrate the value of retained order. There is also affirmative evidence that route-sensitive off-ramp coupling can be handled within first-order theory with aggregate memory. Together these findings support a narrow continuation, not a universal infeasibility verdict.

The review remains open. No project or TSC-suite change follows. The proposed ramp-plus-exit case would be a separately reviewed task; it does not revive the excluded TSC V5/I1 experiment under another name.
