## 1. Distinguish an effective cap from a green fraction

M.A is an explicit ideal continuous admission cap: availability, permission and
approach receiving bound a work-conserving entrance transfer. It is conservative
and causal, with no physical startup or pulse timing in the contract.

For plentiful waiting and constant receiving below fully open service, a fixed
green duty cycle serves only during green. Averaging its nominal fully open
permission first, then taking the receiving minimum, produces a different
operator. The discrepancy can persist as cycle length tends to zero. The PDF
gives all regimes and a maximum local gap. This disproves universal equivalence
of these two admission formulas under the stated local inputs; it does not
disprove M.A as an idealized task or certify a traffic-system performance gap.

H01's continuum signal rule applies its split to effective receiving before
demand limitation. It is therefore a substantive candidate B adaptation, not
a proof of M.A equivalence. Its first-order signal analysis lacks our endogenous
bounded-acceleration restrictions. No-spillback count results and spillback
limitations cannot be imported as joined reference/gradient guarantees.

An effective rate controller could adjust physical timing using receiving or
release measurements. That would be an actuator realization contract, not
proof that an open-loop fixed duty fraction implements M.A. Shared high-level
open-loop rates and feedback inside a physical realization must be distinguished.

## 2. Exact bookkeeping is not eventwise adequacy

The selected deterministic cumulative-floor rule remains precise. Progress
carries across samples and controls. Its terminal remainder is bounded below
one threshold unit, independent of the time mesh at fixed cumulative endpoint.
All remainder mass has already transferred and contributes to population/TST.

The discrepancy is a descriptor representation, not integerized flow. Very
small positive transfer can create no new descriptor or one depending on the
declared public progress. A small count perturbation near a threshold can change
the record set. Count convergence alone supplies neither an unconditional
record-set equivalence nor an impact/gradient bound. Away from thresholds, a
known error margin can preserve counts at finitely many declared samples;
this conditional margin is not adopted as a new acceptance tolerance.

A01 offers floors and reports its tests using Poisson generation. Similar
macroscopic results do not prove eventwise or differentiated equivalence.
The latter would change stochastic/knowledge/gradient contracts and does not
guarantee an event for every subthreshold increment. It is not adopted or
used as a tail repair. No forced final birth, counter reset or smaller threshold
is authorized by this audit.

## 3. Why the finding is a scope/adoption gate

Current approvals selected a source/reference route and conservative reset;
they did not certify literal pulse operation. The new operator result explains
why treating pulse equivalence as already established is invalid for the entire
finite-receiving task. It leaves a defensible model-level rate reference, or
requires an explicit actuator adaptation if a physical-signal bridge is required
before continuing. The facade presents A/B/C with their strongest supported
forms, pros/cons and dependencies.

A accepts that limited reference-qualification scope while preserving all
admitted small-transfer cases and the complete objective. It cannot turn an
unqualified physical bridge into an experiment-only formality or claim a final
research-ready reference. B changes the service operator; C introduces finite
pulse and launch qualification. Neither automatically fixes the descriptor
remainder or permits a shortcut in exact joined-motion qualification.
