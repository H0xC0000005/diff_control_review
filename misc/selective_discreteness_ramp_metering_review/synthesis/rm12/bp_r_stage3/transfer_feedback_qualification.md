## 4. Sending obstruction and what is actually refuted

Consider the finite ramp approach initially jammed on its first 3 m and
empty on its remaining 27 m, with closed upstream admission and free merge
receiving. This is an admissible nonuniform initial field, not certified as
the product of a specific prior control. Its initial mass and native sampled
sending mass are both 9/20. The first physical arrival at the merge is at
9/10 s. Capacity-limited service until the public clock is at most 9/140.

**Refuted:** requiring the native coarse cell's entire accepted 9/20 mass
to cross its exit during this interval while also requiring the same richer
KW field's causal port flow. No temporal interpolation can deliver that mass.
This is not a refutation of native coarse CTM, which evolves a different
internal approximation. Nor is it a ban on qualified finite numerics.

**Not uniquely fixed:** treating the desired mass as a total ceiling yields
9/140 in the single-exit completion; treating mass divided by interval as
an additional held rate ceiling yields 9/200. Both have admissible waves and
exact conservative ending fields. They differ by 27/1400 mass and can alter
E1 eligibility. No record/count inconsistency is hidden by either calculation.

At the source CFL, total physical outgoing mass cannot exceed the native
total sending ceiling: maximum speed prevents new upstream-port admissions
from traversing the entire section before the next clock, and discharge is
bounded by lane capacity. This supports the total-mass interpretation without
adding a rate throttle. It does **not** automatically enforce each oriented
choice budget under partial blocking; those individual budgets and shared
donor availability still need an explicit multi-target completion.
