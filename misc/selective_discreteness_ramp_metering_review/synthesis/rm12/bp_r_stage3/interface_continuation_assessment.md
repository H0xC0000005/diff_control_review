## The semantic progress

The reference represents a slow entrant occupying space and restricting
following traffic while it accelerates. A compact model can instead carry
spatial queues and calculate a reduced service rate. We now have a published
dynamic example of that second approach, including a compatible priority
operator. The outstanding issue is what its service calculation means: a
phenomenological queue-triggered drop and an average of accelerating disturbances
are different approximations. They are not merely two data structures for an
otherwise identical traffic law.

A controller can obtain different travel-time predictions from either model.
Before attributing that difference to omitted disturbance information, the
comparison must identify ordinary constitutive error. Otherwise a detailed
model could appear necessary because its rival uses a service penalty in a
case with no disturbance to represent.

## I1 — binding the count, node and event interfaces

The following interface identities are qualified at formulation level:

1. A ramp admission credits the approach, not the merge. Node demands are the
   independently propagated approach/mainline/lateral requests. The selected
   N.A operator spends one shared receiving budget. Non-ramp subdivision uses
   already budgeted requests; no donor requests its full allowance twice.
2. One cumulative count ledger owns each directed transfer. A lateral source
   transfer already goes to the next longitudinal section. One paired debit
   and credit update the continuous field; descriptor creation adds no mass.
3. Only an actual accepted transfer advances its descriptor-generation counter.
   Permission and desired lateral demand do not create constraints. Each stream
   has public initial threshold progress and the source sampling convention.
4. In a source-free lane segment, a constraint's cumulative-count value remains
   constant along its path while relative passing is prohibited. Junction and
   lateral-source events require conservative count anchoring/reindexing; the
   homogeneous-segment identity is not a global multilane theorem. Node count
   increments, path values and cell inventories must agree. Removing a
   nonbinding descriptor changes no mass.
5. At a simultaneous node/birth/path event, the eventual reference solver must
   produce the same conservative boundary solution without jitter, double
   service or an extra assumed capacity penalty. The sources do not prove
   that every proposed operator-splitting order accomplishes this.

Items 1–4 make the interface reviewable. Item 5 remains a joined reference
qualification obligation, not a reason to demand simulator development now.
The source's general cumulative-count method is a route, not an implementation
certificate for this adapted boundary. No birth-time or coincidence law is
silently adopted by writing these interfaces.

## I2 — analytic clearance and the objective tail

On a finite directed spatial mesh without backward transfers, a unit of mass
can cross only finitely many interfaces. Finite population therefore bounds
total descriptor-generating transfer mass and descriptor count. This prevents
unbounded births from recirculation within that declared topology. It does not
prove eventual exit.

On a level road with a positive lower bound on the source acceleration up to
free speed, a constraint recovers in bounded time **after downstream traffic
allows it to accelerate freely**. Congestion can delay that opportunity. No
unconditional recovery duration or universal draining rate follows.

The PDF supplies a sufficient tail certificate: if the joined law establishes
a uniform contraction of remaining inventory over a declared time window, its
remaining TST is bounded by a geometric sum. Those constants must be justified
from the eventual physical law; they are not fitted to hide a stalled queue.
Finite population, finite descriptors and positive permission alone do not
establish the contraction. Clearance remains partly qualified.

This narrows the obligation: prove eventual draining or certify the integral
tail. It does not add a terminal penalty, alter TST or require numerical execution
before a methodology task can be formed.

## I3 — a concrete independently evolving compact law

The RM0010 candidate stores its own densities and waiting/approach inventories.
Its source boundary, transcribed in the PDF, uses those demands, ordinary
receiving and one fixed drop parameter. Applying the selected M.A wrapper,
non-ramp grouping and per-lane receiver is a declared task adaptation, not a
source theorem about this multilane merge. Node bounds and paired conservation
survive the transcription. It does not need future reference trajectories.

**New static qualification result:** the native capacity-drop trigger can
produce additional loss even when the selected reference has no active moving
constraint. The hand example in the evidence/PDF establishes this local defect
relative to the reference, not a failure of all macroscopic models. The zero-drop
parameter limit passes; the whole inactive-disturbance regime does not follow.
Its numerical scheme and lane-drop uniqueness theorem must not be represented
as a theorem for the new multilane node.

A mechanism-oriented mean closure remains viable. Embedding it into spatial
conservation gives a dynamic compact model, even if its service relation is
quasi-steady. That is a substantive approximation: the spatial inventories keep
history, but local service is inferred from present own-model summaries, rather
than explicitly evolving every already admitted disturbance. It must specify
actual entry statistics, insertion-speed convention, coupled receiving solution
and zero-entry/nonbinding limit. Native source geometry and acceleration
assumptions cannot be transferred silently. No arbitrary exponential recovery
term, private history or per-control calibration is authorized.
