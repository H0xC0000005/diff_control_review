## 3. A CFL-compatible field projection counterexample

Use a level 30 m cell, one second, u=30 m/s, w=5 m/s, jam density 0.15
equivalents/m, initial uniform free density 0.01, free exit and sufficient
upstream demand. Two old records start at position/count zero with desired
speeds 5 and 15 m/s. This satisfies the ordinary u*dt=dx CFL equality. These
are admissible local initial records, not a constructed E1/meter witness.

The previously proved coincident local fan gives congested density 0.075 behind
the 5 m/s path and vacuum ahead; the faster record separates through vacuum.
At one second the record positions are 5 and 15 m. Initial mass 0.3 exits;
accepted entry is 0.375 and the remaining mass is 0.375 in [0,5]. Both old
count anchors remain zero. No internal source, integer population or hidden
second service deduction is used.

Uniform averaging instead gives density 0.0125 over all 30 m. The resulting
local counts at the two positions are 0.3125 and 0.1875. It invents 0.125 mass
in their empty gap while retaining cell total and boundary counts. Relabeling
changes old source-free histories; moving positions changes the selected
motion. This refutes preservation of both anchors and positions by this one
projection, not every numerical CTM treatment or the complete source model.

## 4. Candidate F.A has a constructive local solution

Take completed cell-boundary counts, existing record positions and their
completed own count anchors in the same local gauge. Sort all distinct field
locations. Coincident positions must have equal count; their distinct kinetic
records remain in the inventory. Between consecutive locations, the count
drop must lie between zero and jam density times interval length.

If these compatibility conditions hold, linearly interpolate cumulative count
between locations. The negative slope is a nonnegative density no greater than
jam density. Count drops telescope to the unchanged cell mass. Every retained
count is matched exactly; equal adjacent counts imply vacuum. No new history,
arbitrary spacing or integer traffic is required. With only cell boundaries
this reduces to the ordinary uniform cell reconstruction. The worked example
reproduces the fan's congested and empty pieces exactly.

This is an interface/reconstruction result, not an existence theorem for the
next moving-boundary fluxes. Incompatible anchors must trigger diagnosis of
the upstream field/motion/node update, not clipping, moving labels or mass
repair. Ordinary averaging still occurs within anchor intervals, so exact
anchor preservation is not exact continuum evolution. Source evidence does
not establish the coupled approximation's convergence or derivative quality.
The interpolation intervals are not automatically smaller native CTM cells
to which the same one-second update may be applied. A downstream numerical
flux treatment must satisfy its own CFL/stability conditions or resolve the
interior waves appropriately. The example's parent-cell CFL check does not
certify such a flux treatment.

F.A is recommended but not adopted. F.B retains numerical field projection
with exact metadata and would require an explicit joint compatibility/motion
error contract; it is not automatically incompatible with G1. For one
projection of a field bounded by jam density, the absolute count discrepancy
is at most jam density times cell length. This local bound tends to zero with
cell refinement, but neither proves convergence of successive endogenous
updates nor controls one-sided density traces or derivatives. K.A stays fixed
under either. This is Type 2 field/count and comparison scope, separated from
reviewer-owned interpolation proof and transcription.

## 5. Clearance and complete TST remain conditional

Earlier finite-birth arguments bound descriptor births under finite mass,
finite forward-directed topology and positive descriptor threshold. They do
not show when records become harmless or when all traffic clears. A finite
population bound is not a clearance theorem.

Primary ordinary-CTM stability results have different node maps or restricted
domains. They support checking eventual free-flow behavior, not transferring
global clearance to this exact-record multilane task. Even asymptotic zero
mass needs a sufficiently fast tail for finite TST. A one-cell free-flow
recurrence has a summable geometric tail for positive progress; the PDF gives
the formula. This is a conditional tail calculation after entering that regime,
not a simulated clearing run or proof that the joined task reaches it.
