\section*{1. Averaging permission and limiting receiving need not commute}
Let $s>0$ be fully open entrance service (vehicle equivalents/s), $R\ge0$
constant receiving (same units), and $\eta\in[0,1]$ a fixed green fraction.
Assume enough waiting mass to saturate entrance demand throughout this diagnostic.
Let $b(t)\in\{0,1\}$ be a periodic signal with cycle $c>0$ and green duration
$\eta c$. Its nominal rate is $r=\eta s$. The current ideal cap gives
\begin{equation}
q_A=\min\{\eta s,R\}.
\end{equation}
With instantaneous first-order release and no startup loss, the pulse gives
\begin{equation}
q_P(t)=\min\{s b(t),R\},\qquad
\overline q_P=\frac1c\int_0^c q_P(t)\,dt=\eta\min\{s,R\}.
\end{equation}
Hence the discrepancy is
\begin{equation}
q_A-\overline q_P=
\begin{cases}
(1-\eta)R,&0\le R\le\eta s,\\
\eta(s-R),&\eta s<R<s,\\
0,&R\ge s.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
It is positive for $0<\eta<1$ and $0<R<s$, with maximum
\begin{equation}
\max_{R\ge0}(q_A-\overline q_P)=\eta(1-\eta)s
\quad\text{at }R=\eta s.
\end{equation}
For $s=1$, $R=0.4$ and $\eta=0.5$, $q_A=0.4$ but
$\overline q_P=0.2$ vehicle equivalents/s. Over one diagnostic ten-second
cycle, they admit four and two equivalents respectively. These are uncalibrated
local inputs, not a traffic simulation. The averaged discrepancy is independent
of $c$; decreasing cycle length does not repair the wrong operator.

With finite bin duration $\Delta t$, available mass $B$ and receiving mass
$\mathsf R$, M.A is
\begin{equation}
A_A=\min\{B,r\Delta t,\mathsf R\}.
\end{equation}
Pair its debit/credit: $W^+=W+I-A_A$, $n_{\rm app}^+=n_{\rm app}+A_A$
before other transfers. Conservativity does not prove pulse equivalence.

\section*{2. What the inspected continuum-signal source actually averages}
For the controlled incoming road in Han et al.'s author preprint,
$D(t)$ is demand (veh/s), $C$ its capacity, and $S(t)$ target supply.
Its equations 2.7--2.8 distinguish
\begin{equation}
q_{\rm on/off}(t)=\min\{D(t),b(t)\min(C,S(t))\},\qquad
q_{\rm cont}(t)=\min\{D(t),\eta\min(C,S(t))\}.
\end{equation}
This is not $\min\{D(t),\eta C,S(t)\}$. Its continuum signal scales
effective receiving, not merely nominal capacity before receiving.
The preceding section's saturated example matches the distinction. This is
source evidence for candidate B's family, not a verified full ramp adaptation.

Under the source's no-spillback hypotheses, its Theorem 4.4 bounds the
on/off--continuum cumulative count difference by
\begin{equation}
\left|N^{c}(t,x)-N^0(t,x)\right|
\le \eta(1-\eta)c\min(C_1,C_3).
\end{equation}
Both counts use that source's link, boundary and signal conditions. Its
spillback sections give distinct limitations; its first-order signal model
does not include our endogenous bounded-acceleration merge descriptors.
This bound therefore proves neither M.A equivalence nor descriptor/gradient
equivalence. No error tolerance or guaranteed study regime is inferred.

\section*{3. The deterministic birth remainder is independent of time refinement}
For a directed generating stream, let $H(t)$ be cumulative actual transferred
mass, $m_*>0$ the fixed descriptor threshold, and $K(t_k)$ the cumulative
generated count at sample $t_k$ (with declared public initial progress).
For zero initial progress,
\begin{equation}
K(t_k)=\lfloor H(t_k)/m_*\rfloor,\qquad
e(t_k)=H(t_k)-m_*K(t_k),\quad 0\le e(t_k)<m_*.
\end{equation}
At a fixed terminal $H(T)$, refining the sampled time mesh leaves
$\lfloor H(T)/m_*\rfloor$ and $e(T)$ unchanged. At $H(T)=0.4m_*$,
no record is generated. The transferred $0.4m_*$ is still in the field,
exits and complete inventory accounting. No physical fractional vehicle is
asserted to lack a real blockage.

\section*{4. A small count error is not automatically a small event error}
For $0<\epsilon<m_*/2$, a local threshold diagnostic gives
\begin{equation}
H_-=m_*-\epsilon,\quad H_+=m_*+\epsilon,\qquad
\lfloor H_-/m_*\rfloor=0,\quad\lfloor H_+/m_*\rfloor=1.
\end{equation}
The difference $2\epsilon$ can be arbitrarily small while the record count
differs. This disproves an unconditional inference from small flow/count
error to identical descriptor sets. It supplies no lower bound on TST error:
a generated record may be nonbinding or its effect may be small.

For a finite declared sample set, one possible conditional statement is
\begin{equation}
\max_k|\widetilde H(t_k)-H(t_k)|<\gamma,\qquad
\min_k\operatorname{dist}(H(t_k),m_*\mathbb Z)>\gamma
\ \Longrightarrow\ 
\lfloor\widetilde H(t_k)/m_*\rfloor=\lfloor H(t_k)/m_*\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Here $\gamma>0$ is a hypothetical mass margin, not an adopted tolerance or
an exclusion of threshold cases. Equal sampled counts still do not imply
equal speeds, effects, intermediate timing or derivatives.

For completeness, the source's reported Poisson alternative at mean increment
$\lambda=\Delta H/m_*$ has
\begin{equation}
\Pr(B=0)=e^{-\lambda}>0,\qquad \mathbb E[B]=\lambda.
\end{equation}
It does not guarantee a record for every positive increment. No randomness,
new seed contract or control-dependent event differentiation is adopted.
