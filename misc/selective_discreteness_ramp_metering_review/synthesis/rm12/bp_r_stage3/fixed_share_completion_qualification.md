## 2. Static port completion advanced

Use fixed original movement weights and active unspent claims. At one local
wave state, every donor's outgoing rates share its physical sending; every
target's inputs share receiving. The ramp/mainline target uses the approved
hierarchical priority and unused-share rule; an effective mainline cap is an
algebraic partition of that same receiving, not a second independent resource.

Two explicit static constructions allocate weighted increments, stopping
all tied binding rows/columns/port limits simultaneously. Their water level
has inverse-time units and is not a traffic execution clock. At least one
remaining port stops each allocation pass, so the finite calculation ends.
Each construction yields unique flows for its declared capped-priority
sharing rules, satisfies joint feasibility and is invariant when a strictly
restricted donor's physical demand rises to capacity: its unused row limit
was not setting the answer. It does not maximize arbitrary network throughput
or import FIFO coupling. This is original analytical transcription, not a
claim that the general published node theorem already covers our model.

The constructions differ by a per-movement orientation cap. It has a native
sampled source lineage but is not implied by P.A's receiving weights. This
is Q.J.F.D, kept Type2 rather than resolved as local implementation glue.
