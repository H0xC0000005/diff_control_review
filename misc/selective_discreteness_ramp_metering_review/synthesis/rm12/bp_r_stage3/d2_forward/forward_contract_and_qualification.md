## Static D2/N.A/P.A completion and boundary consistency

Fix the current active ports and their positive original weights. The earlier
weighted-increment construction now runs without oriented capacity ceilings.
Each donor's total is bounded by its physical sending. Each receiver's total
is bounded by its own receiving. For the ramp target, reserve the approved
priority share subject to ramp availability, allocate the mainline subgroup
against the remainder, then return unused receiving to eligible ramp demand.
These are partitions of one resource, not separate capacities.

There are finitely many active ports. Each allocation pass freezes every port
incident to a tied saturated resource; at least one freezes. This gives one
deterministic finite static allocation, with nonnegative rates and no donor
overdraw, while preserving the unchanged integrated request ceilings during
subsequent physical integration. Its allocation variable is not elapsed time.

**New source-boundary qualification.** If a donor's accepted total is strictly
below its current physical demand, that row resource is slack throughout the
monotone allocation. Replacing the demand by capacity leaves the calculation
unchanged when weights and active requests are held fixed. For the ramp, a
strictly receiving-restricted award implies the ramp availability is above its
priority share; raising it does not change the mainline resource. Likewise,
raising a receiver supply that was not fully used cannot change the result:
that resource is slack. At a slack ramp target, the ramp demand is fully served
and the effective mainline bound is also slack.

This establishes the relevant stationary-boundary demand/supply consistency
of our declared static map. It complements the physical KW boundary waves:
restricted donors develop congestion without a priority bonus, and unfilled
receivers can have free inlet states without unlocking a different allocation.
It is not a general network existence, throughput-optimality or full-FIFO theorem.
The frozen active set excludes amount exhaustion, request refresh, moving-record
contacts and node crossings; those remain physical events of the joined model.

Exact checks cover 5,832 two-donor/two-recipient/ramp cases, including zero
sending/supply, asymmetric original weights, two ramp priorities and exhausted
fork ports. They include 8,292 strict donor/ramp demand lifts and 3,716 slack
receiver supply lifts. The analytic slack-resource argument, not enumeration
alone, is the general static result.

## The field-refresh gap and its constructive diagnostic

Mass and all selected count anchors define an admissible reconstruction, not a
unique physical profile. On a 30-m donor, move the same 0.45-eq jammed block
from the last 3 m to the first 3 m. With the same sampled request data, a
blocked through receiver and an ample lateral receiver, D2 accepts respectively
9/80 eq and 9/140 eq over one second. Original lane-change allowance is 9/80 eq
in both. The two profiles have the same fixed-end counts, and no donor record
distinguishes them. Anchor-only averaging maps both to density 3/200 eq/m.

The early case exhausts its allowance at 7/40 s. Its completed donor is jammed
on the last 9/4 m, mass 27/80 eq; its target holds 9/80 eq. The late case first
arrives at 9/10 s; its completed donor is critical on [12,30] m, mass 27/70 eq;
its target holds 9/140 eq. The PDF verifies the release/stop contacts and paired
profile integrals. At the terminal instant no positive mass has exited the
diagnostic target, so the one-step total inventory integral is not a control
benefit result.

These are admissible field/request inputs. No matched reachable prehistory is
proved; no unknown-initialization/full-history ambiguity claim is revived.
This is not proof against macro physics: a spatially richer first-order model
can distinguish the pair. It refutes exact equivalence of the mass-and-anchor
projection, not numerical usefulness at a declared grid/refinement level.

## E1 and the next input: what can be proved without a new solver

Conditional on an admissible completed own solution, E1 changes neither its
population nor its count field. Every coincident newborn shares the same count
at the declared location while keeping its distinct signature. If an old
record is at that location, its compatible current count agrees as well.
The previous own-field local fan supplies an initial continuation for held
local speeds without inventing rank spacing.

For two source-free anchors at ordered positions, their count difference is
the mass between them. Jam-bounded density puts a lower bound on their
separation whenever that count difference is positive. They cannot collide
with unequal counts in an admissible field. Equal-count anchors enclose vacuum;
their local coincidence need not force compression or label changes. This
does not prove every later joint contact has been resolved.

For the prior joint certificate, the A-section completed mass is 39/100 eq,
the old anchor is at 13/3 m with count zero, and both newborns attach to count
39/100 at the inlet. The count gauge includes the initial 9/100 eq as well as
3/10 eq actual entry. The mass between old and new anchors is 39/100, and their
4.333-m gap exceeds the 2.6-m jam-bound minimum. Own ahead queue speed is 10/3
m/s; the supplied birth speeds obey the same initial traffic cap. Hence the
completed E1 state is a compatible next input, without adding population.

**Limit:** input/reset compatibility is weaker than an independently completed
second interval, arbitrary contact continuation or indefinite model evolution.
Retaining the completed profile makes that continuation a defined qualification
target; it does not certify it by inspection. Arbitrarily projected profiles
would introduce an additional state transformation first.
