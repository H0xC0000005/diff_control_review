## Result in plain language

A finite merging lane gives traffic limited space in which to find access to the
mainline. That is a genuine mechanism, and the source audit identifies actual
sites rather than inventing extra lanes or a favorable synthetic case. However,
public geometry and traffic's observed longitudinal location can already enter
a spatial aggregate model. Finer local position or gap alignment may matter,
but this review has not established that such information remains unresolved
after the **full admitted aggregate history**, or that it changes useful common
metering-rate actions under **all-stream TST through clearance**.

Therefore **do not admit C to detailed formulation as a positive answer to the
current research question on this evidence**. This is insufficient task-level
support, alongside several conditional information negatives. It is not a proof
that finite-lane mechanisms are irrelevant or that every aggregate spatial model
is sufficient.

## What is new compared with the closed A review

C0 examined **finite longitudinal merging opportunity**, not another recovery
law. It found a genuine two-lane physical antecedent, checked position-sensitive
behavior and its transfer limits, and upgraded a previously excerpt-only
aggregate rate-control reference to accessible selected full methods. It also
audited the closest metering comparison against the unchanged actuator/sensing
contract. These are substantive C findings, even though the final admission is
negative.

The particular temptation C0 rejects is: “traffic closer to the lane end must
behave differently, therefore its position is hidden and useful to control.”
There are three separate obligations: whether that behavior is supported for
the task, whether the controller can already infer the relevant position, and
whether the remaining uncertainty changes a useful rate decision. Physical
position dependence alone does not satisfy the latter two.

The empirical position evidence is site-dependent rather than a universal urgency
law. The direct local-information/control lead changes sensing and timing.
These source facts are documented, with their limitations, in the linked audit;
they do not authorize either a new end-of-lane penalty or a timing-control task.

## Exactly what remains unachieved

1. **A joined genuine task contract:** initial traffic knowledge, observation
   epoch/support, finite receiving/merging dynamics and common feasible rate
   schedules, supported rather than selected to guarantee hiddenness.
2. **Residual information after history:** more than an equal-total snapshot;
   all admitted mass, completed flow, actual supported speed, ramp and control
   histories must be consistent. Known complete physical initialization with
   exact deterministic public dynamics cannot become newly unknown merely by
   adding geometry. Aggregate-only initialization is a different premise and
   is not universally excluded.
3. **Useful rate-action relevance:** a sound difference in useful perturbations,
   policy ranking or improvement under complete TST. More local detail or
   different service predictions alone does not establish this.
4. **A task-specific useful gradient:** the general rate → release → travel →
   merge/receiving → exits → TST chain is identifiable conceptually. The
   finite-lane law and its event sensitivities are unbound; correctness and
   independent usefulness are unestablished. C0 did not require experiments.

These obligations need not be proved by a single literature paper. A defensible
analytic adaptation could bridge them, but none was established within this
bounded source/static pass. Access-limited leads are retained as coverage limits,
not counted as refuted mechanisms or evidence of universal absence.
