# RM8.20 — Open-loop local merge findings and model-family gate

Date: 2026-10-01. Status: **Closed 2026-10-01; RM8-UG1.A approved.**

## Result and limits

The approved open-loop local-ramp task has a physically credible family to
investigate further. A compact local merge-state formulation is a **conditional
admission recommendation**, not a positive answer to the hidden-information or
useful-gradient questions. Strong aggregate history/memory remains a competing
explanation. The full [status matrix](../synthesis/rm8_task_gradient_reference_screen.md)
separates the established mechanism from the missing research bridges.

The closest direct field predecessor is
[Cassidy and Rudjanakanoknad's isolated-merge report](https://escholarship.org/content/qt30s46939/qt30s46939.pdf),
which also contains aggregate-observability counterevidence. The
[hybrid merge theory](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/f/3571/files/2023/03/merges.pdf)
and [continuous-lane source audit](../extraction/rm8_source_studies/continuous_lane_family.md)
supply alternative mechanism structures, with unapproved adaptation needs.
Read [the information map](../synthesis/rm8_predecessor_and_information_map.md)
for the scalar/group distinction and the strong-history test.

What is still missing: a physically valid local-state closure, a realizable
identical-history pair, a different useful metering action under complete
accounting, verified derivative semantics and independent one-step usefulness.
No source identified in this bounded pass supplies that whole chain. This is
not a literature-wide nonexistence claim.

## RM8-UG1 — which family should enter detailed analytic formulation?

**Classification and ownership:** Type 2 review-global; user owns adoption.
Dependencies: accepted RM7 action/accounting/scope contracts and RM8-G1–G4
findings. RM8-G5 is open. Choices here do not reopen the already approved
open-loop decision unless explicitly stated.

**Problem statement:** Should the next review formulate a compact local-lane
merge mechanism now, test aggregate/history closure first, or reopen the task
around fine gap-aligned releases?

**Assumed model-chain function:** A meter rate changes ramp arrivals. Receiving
and local mainline redistribution determine realized merging and service; those
flows change ramp/mainline trajectories and total time. The retained state must
predict how the same feasible rate perturbation changes that chain.

**Core gap:** Operational mechanism support does not identify an extra state
that is both unavailable to the strong aggregate observer and consequential
for the preferred action. Source models also differ in priority, acceleration,
actuation and nonsmooth events. A model family and its adaptation cannot be
selected solely from the fact that lanes matter physically.

### Candidate A — compact local merge-state analytic review (recommended)

Keep the approved single-ramp open-loop task. Begin from a shoulder-lane/rest
partition as a **candidate** local mechanism, checked against the continuous-lane
and hybrid-source structures. Review conservative transfer, finite shared
receiving and realized ramp access before accepting a closed law. Derive or
reject a compact sufficient state; do not assume a full per-lane model.

The next bounded segment should first settle the precise observer and test
whether aggregate memory or an equivalent scalar already resolves the effect.
Only if an information gap survives, seek an exact feasible history/action
certificate and inspect a piecewise sensitivity path. Stop for a new event/
stochastic requirement or an unsupported source adaptation. A sufficiency or
negative result is an acceptable closure; no simulation is implied.

**Pros:** Directly investigates the information-to-decision question using the
source-supported local mechanism. Keeps the common rate action and allows a
minimal sufficient state to emerge. Provides a concrete route to an analytic
certificate without assuming microscopic identity or project compatibility.

**Cons:** The proposed compression may not close, or its state may be recoverable
from admitted history. Action ranking can remain unchanged despite forward
lane effects. Nonsmooth receiving/acceleration may prevent the desired simple
gradient path; evidence of external usefulness will still be later work.

**Dependencies and scientific consequence:** The next segment must distinguish
published laws from new adaptations, preserve full accounting, and give
aggregate/history and scalar sufficiency genuine chances to win. Approval
admits a review family; it does not approve new traffic equations or prove that
lanes are necessary. The multiramp extension remains deferred.

### Candidate B — aggregate/history closure audit before richer formulation

Keep the same traffic task and action, but make the next segment solely a
sufficiency/identifiability audit of road-total memory, admitted speed dynamics,
separate ramp/mainline flows and available aggregate merge closures. Specify
what outcome and intervention response each closure can reproduce, and seek a
source-backed inadequacy before constructing a lane-state law. If no inadequacy
is established, close at a bounded unresolved/sufficient-aggregate finding.

**Pros:** Gives the strongest simple explanation priority and avoids creating
an information effect through an imposed lane-service penalty. Can end with a
valuable aggregate-sufficiency result and a sharply scoped remaining gap.

**Cons:** Fitting aggregate observations alone cannot certify intervention
sufficiency. Published closures may not provide an identifiable exact physical
model. Without a richer causal candidate the audit can stop at an unresolved
explanation rather than answer the hidden-information question.

**Dependencies and scientific consequence:** Requires a precise observer and
matched interventions; absence of a discovered counterexample is not a universal
sufficiency proof. This ordering prioritizes comparator closure rather than
formulating its richer alternative in parallel; no representation is adopted.

### Candidate C — reopen toward gap-aligned release timing

Use the microscopic gap-detection predecessors to define a common fine-release
parameterization, declared sensor/history and local acceleration/gap reference.
Review whether different hidden gaps change useful release timing under
complete accounting. Ordinary infrastructure metering is possible; CAV control
need not be assumed. Event/expected sensitivity needs its own admission.

**Pros:** Has a direct nearby information-aware control predecessor and a clear
semantic connection between a receiving gap and a release decision. May expose
consequential information that mean-rate controls cannot use.

**Cons:** Changes the approved action resolution and potentially observation
interface. Source benefits depend on release feedback, driver behavior and
lane-demarcation assumptions; continuous-rate derivatives do not transfer.
Event/stochastic derivatives and stronger reference qualification are required.

**Dependencies and scientific consequence:** Explicitly reopen RMD-G1/G3/G5
before execution and separate timing benefits from altered lane permissions or
extra sensing. This is RM8-UG1.C, **not** RMT-R1.C, the deferred multi-ramp task.

**Provisional recommendation: Candidate A.** The evidence is enough to justify
an analytic local-mechanism attempt, with history/scalar sufficiency as an early
filter. It is not enough to assert that the mechanism is hidden or that its
gradients are useful. Candidate B remains a defensible more conservative
ordering; C is a scientifically different branch requiring scope discussion.
