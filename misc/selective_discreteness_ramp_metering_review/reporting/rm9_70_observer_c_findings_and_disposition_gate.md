## Main finding

C describes traffic occupying the cell at the observation instant, using the
approved representative equilibrium-speed closure. It supports population-based
accounting, but **does not calculate TST or replace the all-stream time objective**.
The observer is idealized; receiving-limited physical speeds are not validated.

With exact total and C speed, the lane split is identifiable within most known
FD branch combinations. However, knowing a branch locally is not the same as
identifying the full state globally: mixed/congested snapshot roots can remain.
The audit explicitly retains this distinction rather than assuming the free-flow
inverse applies everywhere. Actual completed flow/counts provide further
constraints and do not replace the speed channel.

The stronger obstruction concerns **history**. If the complete state was known
or uniquely reconstructible at any earlier common epoch, deterministic public
dynamics determine every later state and response to any common future schedule.
Later queues, saturated receivers or an ambiguous present snapshot do not undo
that knowledge. A state distinction lost before control acts also cannot change
the later action ranking when the complete poststate and remaining inputs agree.

This excludes a hidden-information advantage in those conditional domains.
It does not exclude every aggregate-only, preloaded congested initialization.
For that remaining domain, this pass found no source-grounded concrete task
demonstrating full historical equality and different useful actions. Geometry,
admissible initial knowledge, complete coupled histories and all-stream clearance
still need binding. Four narrow additional searches and selected primary passages
are evidence scoping, not an exhaustive literature search or observability solver.

## What the sources add, and what they do not

Bounded acceleration can create merge-associated voids and reduce discharge.
The inspected merge paper even studies a site without an acceleration lane.
That is physical mechanism evidence; ramp queue dependent entry speed is not
hidden if the queue and its speed rule are public. No exact-history lane-split
or useful-gradient claim follows. See
[Laval, Cassidy and Daganzo's merge model](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/f/3571/files/2023/03/merges.pdf).

A credible rival is to change the aggregate law before adding state.
Srivastava's Chapter 3 changes congested demand with a jam-demand parameter while
retaining density conservation and supply. Its task is queue discharge; the
separate Chapter 4 lane-drop model is not already a complete ramp formulation.
This provides a minimal-law competitor to investigate, not a justified copy into
our task. See [the UCI dissertation, selected Chapters 3 and 4](https://escholarship.org/content/qt7dx478g4/qt7dx478g4.pdf).

Joint metering/lane-change feedback and gap-informed release studies concern
different actuators or information/action contracts. They motivate alternatives,
but cannot establish our unchanged open-loop rate-only claim. Source versions,
selected locations and access limitations are in the
[access record](../archive/metadata/rm9_observer_c_source_access_record.md).

## RM9-UG5 — disposition and the next review scope

**Classification / owner:** Type 2, user; governs G1/G3/G5 and dependent task work.

**Problem statement.** Should we accept the bounded current-task result as
“no established hidden-information/action advantage, with conditional aggregate
sufficiency and an unresolved uncertain-initial-state domain,” and move to a
physically motivated family, or keep investigating that residual domain?

**Assumed model-chain function.** A common metering schedule changes actual
release, finite ramp storage and merge entry, then longitudinal/lateral traffic
and all-stream TST. A successful task needs relevant state that the complete
aggregate observer/history cannot recover; a richer forward model alone is
insufficient. Strong aggregate competitors may infer the state or use a better
public discharge law. Only a surviving action distinction can motivate the
subsequent gradient-usefulness study.

**Core gap.** The current packet supplies physical lane interactions and
conditional inverses, but no genuine matched full-history/action certificate.
Choosing an unbound initial history or new law to force that result would change
the methodology. The adopted C observer also cannot silently be reused as an
equilibrium closure for an acceleration model whose speed is dynamic.

### A — accept the scoped result; plan a minimum recovery/acceleration comparison

A and C share the same evidence-first/minimum-required-representation philosophy.
A begins with non-equilibrium discharge/recovery; C begins with finite physical
merge opportunities. The [semantic comparison](../synthesis/rm9/rm9_ug5_a_c_semantic_comparison.md)
records their strongest aggregate rivals, observation consequences and the
user's preference for mixed search rather than deep pursuit of B. Changing
physics does not by itself evade complete-state deterministic sufficiency.

Accept the limited current-task finding, with the uncertain-start domain retained
as unresolved rather than disproved. Authorize a targeted next-stage **proposal**
comparing (i) unchanged CTM, (ii) a source-supported modified aggregate discharge
law and (iii) extra recovery/readiness state only if the physical evidence and
information test require it. Retain single-ramp common open-loop rate control,
two mainline lanes by default and all-stream TST. First specify which real merge
and initial-history setting supports the mechanism. Revisit speed semantics
explicitly where non-equilibrium dynamics require it; keep all methods' sensing
matched and the full history intact. The proposal must state actual differences
in demand, state, geometry and observer before admission. No service multiplier
or extra state is predetermined.

**Pros:** physical evidence identifies an omission in elementary first-order
discharge; the aggregate-demand rival guards against claiming necessity merely
from richer physics. Preserves the controller question and avoids manufactured
parameter/history restrictions. Adds state only when justified.

**Cons:** changes G2 and potentially G1; a modified aggregate law or history may
still explain the effect completely. Source tasks need ramp-specific transfer;
neither positivity nor a new gradient advantage is promised.

**Dependencies / consequence:** explicit RM9 scoped disposition and a successor
planning boundary, not automatic implementation. C approval remains valid for
this task; a changed observer needs its own admission. X3 remains Deferred until
a separately approved proposal opens the required part. No RM10 opened now.

### C — accept the scoped result; plan an actual finite acceleration-lane task

C asks which spatial opportunities and constraints matter; it is not an
alternative research philosophy to A. Known distance-to-end is geometry, not
hidden information. A position effect already captured by adequately observed
aggregate cells would be spatial-model improvement, not a hidden-state result.

Start from a real finite ramp acceleration lane with a distributed merge region.
Review whether position/remaining distance, end urgency or usable merge space is
decision-relevant beyond exact aggregate histories and known geometry. Compare a
longitudinally resolved aggregate CTM before retaining lane/position metadata;
mere spatial refinement is not evidence of irreducible hidden information.
Keep rate-only metering/TST initially; do not introduce gap-timed actions silently.

**Pros:** genuine physical geometry supplies a reason to examine merge position
and urgency; remains recognizably ramp-rate control. Clear spatial aggregate
competitor can separate resolution error from missing information.

**Cons:** changes geometry and merge/lateral laws; the strong observer may reveal
the additional distribution once cell supports are matched. More cells can
reproduce the effect without selective metadata. Source mechanism/formulation
transfer remains to review; not necessarily fewer changes than A.

**Dependencies / consequence:** scoped RM9 disposition plus a new planning
boundary, with G1/G2 reviewed explicitly. X2 remains Deferred until admitted.
No arbitrary extra mainline lane or engineered merge geometry is authorized.
