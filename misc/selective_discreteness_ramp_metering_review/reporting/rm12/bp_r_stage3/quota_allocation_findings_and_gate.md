## What is now settled by T.A

Suppose a lane has requested 0.05 equivalents of lane-changing movement and
0.15 of through movement for the present second. Through receiving is closed;
the adjacent lane is available. Traffic ready at the exit can change lanes
at the physical allocation rate, using the 0.05 allowance. Once that amount
has changed lanes, this movement stops for the rest of the step. The remaining
0.15 stays on the donor lane; it is not silently reassigned to the available
lane. Requests are reconsidered at the next regular update.

Using the source's quarter-share orientation, an exact example reaches the
lane-change allowance after about 0.311 s. Its
resulting waves leave exactly 0.15 equivalents queued at the end. This closes
a genuine allowance-depletion case beyond the earlier late-arrival example.
It also shows why repeatedly applying a choice fraction without tracking the
allowance can count more lane-changing demand than was sampled. All mass
remains continuous. Effect creation still waits for the regular E1 clock.

## The newly exposed distinction

Consider the same ramp and two mainline lanes under both completions. The
receiving lane already contains a slow effect with a queue behind it and
empty road ahead. Its own field permits 0.30 equivalents/s at the entrance.
The approved fixed ramp share awards 0.10 to the ramp, leaving 0.20 for
mainline through traffic and traffic changing from the adjacent lane.

At the regular update, their sampled requests have proportions about **55:45**.
If those proportions remain the competition weights, the non-ramp service
stays about **0.111 through / 0.089 lane-changing** equivalents/s.

Both donor streams then queue. In a first-order traffic field, a queued
stream's *sending demand* can rise to lane capacity. Sending demand means
what it could send if receiving were available; it is not its actual accepted
flow. If IT instead reads those new demands, the compatible wave solution
gives **0.160 through / 0.040 lane-changing**.

Both solutions use the same 0.20 non-ramp receiving, respect the approved
amount allowances and have admissible donor queues. Ramp priority, total
receiving and the old effect's motion are unchanged. The difference is which
information determines the non-ramp share. It can change the next E1 births:
with a previous lane-change counter of 0.94, the first solution crosses one
threshold during this step and the second does not.

This is a **modeling-choice diagnostic**, not a hidden-information/control
witness: the two completions use different allocation interpretations.
It does not establish fine-state necessity or useful gradients.

## The now-closed question: should queue formation change service proportions?

**Problem statement — Q.J.F.P, Type 2 / user.** Within a regular interval,
do IT's incoming-demand proportions remain tied to its sampled requests,
or follow a source-consistent continuous wave-junction completion?

**Assumed model-chain function.** Physical donor availability and recipient
receiving set feasible service; the allocation selects who uses it. Accepted
movements then change lane fields, consume their T.A allowances and determine
completed E1 counters. This question affects the common forward/comparison law.

**Core gap.** Keeping sampled proportions and recomputing proportions after
queues form are not equivalent. A naive direct point-demand substitution
changes its own answer when its output creates a queue. A wave-junction
completion can settle that feedback locally, but its result differs from
holding the original sampled proportions. Neither total-mass bookkeeping
nor conservation selects between them.

**Smallest scope reopened:** the temporal meaning of IT's incoming-demand
proportions, including N.A's non-ramp subgroup. N.A's fixed ramp share,
unused-share reallocation, grouping and single receiving resource stay
closed. T.A, M.A, G1, E1 and K.A remain approved.

### P.A — preserve the sampled proportions as competition weights

Keep the original sampled request ratios while those claims remain active.
Own traffic still determines physically available sending and receiving.
If a stream cannot use its share or its amount is fulfilled, it does not
claim impossible service; the remaining claims need a qualified redistribution
using the same frozen weights. A request weight is not an absolute rate cap.
No new geometric priority parameter is added.

**Pros:** follows the regular sampled task most directly; preserves its
original IT proportions without letting a newly formed queue alter its own
priority. Fixed-weight allocation has a primary node-model basis. The actual
coupled example is conservative and consistent with its own field.

**Cons:** treating sampled demand ratios as interval-local priorities is an
explicit adaptation. It is not identical to a continuously refreshed
demand-proportional rule. Shared donors serving multiple targets still need
qualified allocation when claims become unavailable or depleted; separate
target awards cannot spend the same donor traffic twice.

**Dependencies/consequences:** selecting P.A fixes these weights, not a full
solver or field family. Qualify capped redistribution/shared-donor service,
remaining encounters/crossings and reset under that contract. Reopen this
choice if preserving it would require a changed FIFO or source geometry.

### P.B — retain the proportional source rule through a wave-junction completion

Let the allocation and newly created donor queues be solved together, using
the source's oriented demand rule and admissible traffic waves. The boundary
calculation must return the same accepted flows after its own queues appear.
This is more than recomputing a point formula once. A derived two-lane
merge/diverge case supplies a unique local flux before allowance depletion.

**Pros:** has a direct source-to-continuum rationale and reads the evolving
traffic demand. It adds neither a new priority parameter nor a held source
rate cap. The local construction resolves queue/flow feedback rather than
assuming externally supplied histories.

**Cons:** differs from the sampled finite-step proportions. General
multi-target, quota-depletion and ramp-group completion is not already proved
by the reviewed junction theorems. Importing a generic FIFO junction would
change the source IT behavior and is not justified here.

**Dependencies/consequences:** selecting P.B opens qualification of the
oriented source-consistent wave-node completion, including allowance events.
The present result is local, with one unrestricted parallel recipient and
pending claims. It does not automatically adopt a continuous field everywhere
or change the recovery/birth clock.

**Recommendation subsequently approved: P.A**, because the approved task keeps regular
sampled requests and T.A preserves their desired amounts. P.A makes their
relative competition explicit without introducing another absolute rate
ceiling. P.B is a legitimate source-family alternative with a newly established
local positive; it is not refuted or treated as an incorrect CTM rule.
