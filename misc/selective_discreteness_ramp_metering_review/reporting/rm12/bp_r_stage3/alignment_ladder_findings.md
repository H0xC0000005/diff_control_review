Date: 2026-10-08. The user opened the conditional ladder with autonomous
progression. All three routes were examined. **No step establishes the full
bridge for the currently approved metering task.** Step 1 has a concrete
negative result; steps 2 and 3 have useful support and local constructions,

the traffic mechanism is impossible.

The traffic meaning is straightforward. A slow merging effect can leave a
queue behind it and empty road ahead. A suitable macroscopic field can retain
both. The remaining difficulty is making that same field determine the
effects' motion and all lane transfers, including situations where its own
queues change what the transfer rules see. Keeping positions and counts
consistent at one instant is necessary, but does not settle that feedback.

## The strongest positive result

Consider two slow effects in one 30 m section. One has a queue at the upstream
end and moves at 5 m/s. The other has a separate queue farther ahead and moves
at 10 m/s. The roads between and ahead of their queues are empty. We can
advance both queues and effects together, conserve continuous mass and keep
both exact count labels. Their positions need not coincide with cell edges.
This extends the earlier one-instant reconstruction result to an explicit
local evolution. It remains a source-free local certificate, not a reached
metering scenario or the complete sourced model.

There is also a useful numerical alternative within step 2: **retain fixed
cells but reconstruct the discontinuity inside a relevant cell**, and derive
fluxes from that reconstruction. A published conservative method couples this
idea to restriction motion. Its single-restriction setting does not certify
our multiple-record adaptation. It is a promising A-family alternative,
rather than evidence that fixed cells must be discarded.

[Conservative field/motion method](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/36595)

## Why changing representation did not finish the job

Suppose two incoming streams request 0.2 and 0.4 equivalents/s, while their
shared receiving road offers 0.3. A demand-proportional rule awards 0.1 and
0.2. Both incoming streams then queue. On a continuous wave field, the
traffic immediately next to the junction is now congested; its sending
demand is different. Simply reapplying the same proportional formula to those
new traces can award a different split to the same physical junction.

This illustrates **junction consistency**: representing a queue more precisely
must not leave the transfer rule with incompatible answers before and after
its own update. The calculation refutes a particular direct continuous
transplant of proportional merging. It does **not** refute the source's finite
CTM step, prove every allowed lane-transfer configuration fails, or establish
that the example is reached by our public scenario. The approved fixed ramp
priority is a different rule; the concern is the remaining IT transfer lift.
Continuous link-model literature explicitly distinguishes these cases.

[Continuous link model and junction consistency, section 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.7080)

Likewise, a count-field approximation can be accurate while its one-sided
traffic trace—the quantity used for braking—still needs separate
qualification. A proved continuum result for one differently parameterized
moving bottleneck cannot certify our multiple, zero-passing, sampled-recovery
and lane-transfer task by substitution.

## Next core problem and brief continuation plan

**Core question: can accepted lane transfers and the moving restrictions
advance the same traffic field consistently, while preserving the existing
transfer and timing rules?** Step 2 shows that queues, empty gaps and
restriction motion can agree locally. We now need to establish how traffic
entering that field changes it, and how those changes determine further
entry and motion.

For example, through traffic and traffic changing from an adjacent lane both
try to enter the same next lane section. A slow restriction already there can
create a queue that reduces the space available for entry. The accepted
transfers must respect that space, and their resulting traffic must be the
traffic used to move the restriction. This feedback is the specific missing
piece to complete.

The user agrees to continue from step 2's partial result. The brief plan is:

1. **Specify how the existing transfer rules read the richer field.** Establish
   which requests are sampled at the regular update, which receiving/motion
   quantities respond during the interval, and where transferred traffic
   enters. First preserve the source's sampled transfer meaning; justify any
   equivalent transcription explicitly. Purpose: prevent a representation
   change from silently changing traffic allocation or timing.
2. **Close one actual transfer-with-restriction case.** Start with through and
   adjacent-lane traffic entering a section containing an old restriction.
   Derive accepted flows, queues, restriction motion and next counts from
   that same field, covering available and constrained receiving. Purpose:
   establish the missing feedback beyond the earlier source-free example.
3. **Extend the construction to the approved joined setting.** Include the
   ramp's fixed priority and shared receiving, multiple restrictions and
   contacts, section crossings, and births after completed transfers at the
   regular update. Purpose: ensure the local solution remains consistent
   when the selected components interact, with every transfer counted once.
4. **Assess whether the forward alignment gap is closed.** Produce one
   qualified construction with its source basis, applicability conditions
   and analytical checks. If a piece fails or remains unsupported, identify
   that piece and any necessary methodological choice precisely. Purpose:
   return a supported bridge or a bounded diagnosis for discussion/adoption.

Success means a determinate shared forward update that conserves mass,
respects available sending/receiving and retains the required positions,
counts and birth/recovery timing. A qualified finite numerical construction
