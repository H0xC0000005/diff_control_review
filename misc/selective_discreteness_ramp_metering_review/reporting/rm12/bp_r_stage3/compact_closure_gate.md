## Read first — the traffic story and the two modeling philosophies

Both options build a compact competitor for the same ramp-control task. Traffic
and queues evolve spatially, the upstream meter changes admission to the ramp,
and complete system time charges every stream's waiting and travel. The reference
explicitly represents moving restrictions from slow insertions. The compact
competitor needs a simpler way to predict how much traffic the merge serves.
Both candidates can retain lane information. Their distinction is how they
explain the service loss, not a macro-versus-micro or lane-blind-versus-lane-aware
classification.

**A begins with the physical cause and averages its effect.** Slow entering
traffic accelerates and disturbs surrounding flow. Instead of keeping each
disturbance's position and speed, A proposes estimating the mean service loss
from compact own-model insertion/traffic summaries. Its initial quasi-steady
version uses the mean appropriate to current conditions as current service.
The research intent is to assess how well that physical explanation can be
compressed. Mean-capacity source support does not supply a qualified transient
port automatically. A still changes the kinetic description through an explicit
approximation; it is not an exact state-only rewrite of the reference.

**B begins with an aggregate flow pattern and models it directly.** The native
RM0010 rule lowers the service ceiling when total merging demand exceeds ordinary
receiving. The drop magnitude is fixed site calibration. Its queues/densities
evolve independently, but the service rule does not calculate a particular
entrant's acceleration or remaining disturbance lifetime. The research intent
is to assess whether this established compact response model already predicts
the consequences of meter changes adequately. A useful phenomenological model
need not reproduce every microscopic cause, and this choice is scientifically
legitimate within its declared predictive claim.

**One shared traffic event makes the distinction visible.** Suppose a tighter
upstream command eventually reduces actual merge entries, after propagation
down the ramp. The reference still contains any earlier slow entrants that
remain restrictive. A's estimate follows the new insertion/traffic summaries
and may recover service too early if they omit those remaining disturbances.
B's penalty follows its demand-versus-receiving trigger: it can persist while
that inequality holds or disappear when it stops holding. Neither response is
guaranteed to match the actual disturbance recovery. This is an explanatory
thought example, not a measured outcome or a demonstrated task trajectory.

**The difference in what we can learn matters more than procedural order.** If
A works, the tested mean representation may be sufficient for the intended
response; if it fails, missing recovery memory is one diagnosis to investigate,
alongside closure/parameter errors. Individual identity is not proved necessary.
If B works, it supports the usefulness of that compact predictive family. If B
fails, a mismatched trigger or drop magnitude can explain failure without proving
that omitted vehicle detail caused it. Accurate travel-time prediction alone
does not validate useful gradients for either model.

A is now the primary review line because it directly continues the existing
mechanism-oriented review. B has a concrete published dynamic boundary and
provides a companion view of the control problem. Neither is presumed inferior,
a subset of the other, or a guaranteed stronger predictor. The local hand check
establishes a potential B mismatch; reachability in the final task remains to
qualify. Both families can ultimately coexist in a study. Their review routing
is selected, but their concrete task laws remain unqualified and neither
resolves the core questions by its existence.

**Problem statement.** How can the mechanism-oriented mean closure (primary A)
and the published congestion-triggered capacity-drop law (companion B) each be
qualified against the chosen moving-disturbance task, and what conclusions can
each support? The eventual adoption question concerns concrete service
approximations and their claims, not whether CTM can evolve densities over time
or whether evidence preparation must wait for an exclusive choice.
The static mismatch is a local admissible-state result, not a complete reachable
traffic witness. Final regime/geometry qualification must establish where it
applies; it is not a reason to refute the source model universally.

**Assumed model-chain function.** Every model propagates its own traffic under
the same upstream permission, storage, finite population and node priority.
Its service closure determines actual downstream transfers and hence complete
exit history/TST. It receives public initialization and forcing, without later
reference-state corrections. The compact model must be a credible independent
rival before the study can infer the value of richer disturbance state.

**Core gap.** RM0010 supplies a concrete dynamic law, but its queue-triggered
penalty can remain active when the reference has no active obstruction. It is
therefore not a drop-in mechanism-matched reduction. Existing mean-service work
offers another route, but using its local equilibrium calculation during rate
changes introduces a quasi-steady approximation. Choosing either route affects
the meaning of later discrepancies. A local adapter cannot decide that meaning.
No candidate below is represented as an already qualified complete task.

Semantic clarification: "retain a penalty" means the native trigger can still
apply a reduced capacity in the current state. No separate penalty timer or
additional recovery-memory variable is asserted. A moving obstruction is a
slow entrant/transfer currently restricting followers; ordinary downstream
crowding can remain after such restrictions are absent. The local example
compares different service explanations, not observed evidence that detailed
state is necessary.

## Q.A — primary qualification of a quasi-steady mechanism-oriented compact model

Here **quasi-steady** means estimating the local mean service that would prevail
if the current insertion/traffic conditions persisted long enough to settle,
and using that estimate at the current update. Continuous traffic inventories
and spatial queues still evolve in CTM. The approximation concerns adjustment
of the local disturbance pattern; it does not mean every vehicle accelerates
instantly, and it has no connection to pseudo-convex optimization. After actual
ramp entries decrease, earlier slow entrants may still be restricting traffic;
using the newly estimated mean immediately can miss some of that transition.
This is a potential approximation error, not an established result for this task.

Preserve the current physical reference and inactive-equivalence requirement.
Use spatial lane/approach conservation and the selected admission/priority.
Propose evaluating the source-family mean disturbance/service relation from the
compact model's own present insertion and traffic summaries at each update.
Retain spatial queue history; do not inject a new recovery lag or individual
descriptor history merely to make the approximation more successful.

This is the selected primary review route for a **quasi-steady local service
approximation**. Its concrete source-to-task map is not yet adopted, and the
stationary source is not declared a validated transient model. Qualification must
bind the source map, its valid regimes, own entry-speed/statistic convention,
coupled solution and zero-entry/nonbinding limit. If that cannot be done without
changing geometry, acceleration or another closed contract, return with the
smallest concrete amendment; do not silently adopt one. Unknown predictive
accuracy can later be tested; an undefined or inconsistent map cannot.

**Pros:** directly averages the mechanism being studied; preserves the existing
inactive control and makes additional state easier to interpret; adds a stated
equilibrium approximation rather than an invented memory term. Failure or
sufficiency of this first approximation can guide subsequent richer summaries.

**Cons:** may miss intervention-time recovery because present summaries need
not encode previously admitted disturbances. The multilane source map and
nonlinear-acceleration transfer still require qualification. This selection
does not guarantee that Stage 3 immediately closes or that compact models fail.

**Dependencies/consequence:** G1, reference A, M.A and N.A stay closed. The
selected routing authorizes qualification; it does not approve an unspecified
source port, impose a weak baseline as sufficient comparison, or open experiments.
RM0010 is reviewed now as the companion, with its mismatch assessed separately.

## Q.B — companion qualification of the published phenomenological dynamic closure

Use the concrete RM0010 boundary and its own density evolution, with the common
M.A/N.A wrappers. Its drop magnitude is fixed across controls under a declared
calibration/held-out protocol. Keep all waiting in TST and retain the source's
ordinary-supply trigger; do not make the trigger self-referential by testing an
already reduced supply.

Companion review does not require an inactive-criterion waiver: inspect and
report the mismatch, qualify reachable regimes, and distinguish diagnostic use
from certification as a mechanism-matched reduction. The earlier proposal to
use B as the sole first rival despite that failed limit is not adopted. If a
later definitive comparison needs that acceptance transfer, **explicitly reopen
only the compact-model inactive admission criterion** and explain the narrower
claim before adoption. The reference's unobstructed limit, mass/storage and
action/knowledge fairness remain unchanged. Keep the matched KW limit control
too. No discrepancy is automatically attributed to missing identity, age or
ordering, and no unreviewed activation patch erases the counterexample.

**Pros:** starts from a fully transcribed published dynamic merge boundary;
does not require constructing a new kinetic moment update; gives compact
congestion/queue state a serious role rather than assuming it is inadequate.

**Cons:** the capacity penalty is phenomenological and fixed; it is not the same
moving-obstruction mechanism. The known inactive error makes causal attribution
weaker. This model alone cannot justify finer-state necessity, and its source
well-posedness result does not prove the adapted multilane task well-posed.

**Dependencies/consequence:** companion qualification is authorized and does
not depend on A failing. Any later adoption that relaxes comparison acceptance
needs its own concrete decision. The reference, remaining node/event and
clearance obligations stay unchanged. Per-intervention fitting and comparison
against only a deliberately weak constant-capacity model remain excluded.
