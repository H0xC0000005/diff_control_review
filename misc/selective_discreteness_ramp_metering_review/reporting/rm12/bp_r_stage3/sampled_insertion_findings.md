## What your choice means in traffic terms

During an interval, the model predicts traffic using the restrictions that
already exist. At its end, actual merge transfers determine the new records.
Each starts at its declared entry location with the donor's sampled speed and
the mainline's updated traffic-count value. It can constrain following traffic
from that update onward.

This is the selected timestep model's account of a slow entrant. A restriction
record describes a traffic effect; it is not an extra package of vehicle mass.
The entire accepted transfer remains in the conserved traffic population even
when it produces no new record or several records. No information is averaged
or discarded merely because the list becomes large.

You preferred this regular-update interpretation to immediate activation.
Immediate activation would remove the sampled delay, but it would still need
consistent insertion and coupled continuation. It does not analytically close
those obligations merely by supplying an earlier timestamp.

## What the static review establishes

The birth/reset interface now specifies its own inputs. It preserves completed
traffic counts, populations and exit history; initializes every restriction from
own-model data; and leaves existing restrictions in the preceding interval's
traffic calculation. This supplies conservative, causal synchronization at the
declared sampled level. It does not prove that the sampled level reproduces
continuous-time activation exactly.

The label attached to a newborn says which cumulative traffic-count value is
present at its starting location. If several records start at the same place
and time, they attach to the same field value. We cannot assign each an artificial
extra vehicle of spacing: that would require two different count values at one
point, or silently add population. Different speeds and motion parameters remain
separate records, even when their initial count labels agree.

## Why simultaneous restrictions do not force an approximation

Suppose two different entry streams produce new restrictions at the same merge
location at one update. They may have different initial speeds. Both can be
retained and anchored consistently, but the traffic calculation must account
for their combined effect.

One inspected solver advances traffic by treating at most two nearby restrictions
together. Its own paper reports difficulties with larger coincidences. That is
a limit of that construction, not a proof that the traffic model cannot retain
the exact records. [Daganzo/Laval moving-boundary method](https://escholarship.org/content/qt1hp588xx/qt1hp588xx.pdf).

The journal's related variational literature offers a wider analytic route.
Here, “variational” means finding the traffic counts allowed by all wave and
restriction conditions together. It retains each path rather than replacing
several paths with an average. Published results support prescribed moving
restrictions with compatible road-boundary data.
[Daganzo's well-posedness analysis](https://www.aimspress.com/aimspress-data/nhm/2006/4/PDF/1556-1801_2006_4_601.pdf).

That is positive support for continuing the exact construction. It is not a
completed solver for our task: our paths must themselves respond to traffic,
and lane transfers and the ramp node must agree with that same solution.
No new solver, position perturbation, event-resolved birth clock or recovery
approximation is adopted by this audit.
