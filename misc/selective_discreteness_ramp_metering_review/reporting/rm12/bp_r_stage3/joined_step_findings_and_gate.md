## What the timing and accounting now mean

During an interval, old restrictions move with their own traffic. All traffic
trying to enter the same lane section uses one receiving resource. The ramp
and mainline keep the approved fixed priority, with unused shares reallocated.
After actual transfers finish, the field is updated and new E1 records are
attached to that completed state. Newborn records influence the next interval.
Changing lanes advances traffic to the next section once, without a second
longitudinal move or any extra mass assigned to a restriction record.

If the available receiving changes inside the interval, its allocation must
follow that history. For example, only ramp traffic is waiting during an early
service opportunity; only mainline traffic is waiting during a later, smaller
one. Dividing the interval's total capacity between the two afterward could
give mainline traffic part of an opportunity it missed. The approved priority
can be applied to the successive opportunities without changing the priority
rule. This is a conditional node calculation, not a reached metering example.

The traffic-count label of an old restriction also uses its own lane field.
At a section crossing it is expressed in the receiving section's count
coordinates, using the actual crossing time. Traffic entering that section is
counted once. These rules close the bookkeeping requirements; naming them does
not prove that every endogenous wave/node interaction has a solution.

## Why ordinary cell averaging leaves another gap

Imagine two restrictions initially at the same point, one moving at 5 m/s and
one at 15 m/s. The slower one holds back faster traffic. The faster record
moves into the empty space ahead. They can share the same count label because
there is no traffic between them. The records are descriptions of traffic's
restrictions, not two extra vehicles.

In a one-second, 30 m cell example, the traffic field has 0.375 vehicle-equivalent
mass behind the slow record, now at 5 m. The fast record is at 15 m, with empty
road between them. Replacing that field by its uniform cell average keeps the
total mass, but places 0.125 equivalents in the supposedly empty 10 m gap.
The field and the two retained count labels now disagree.

This matters because the next motion update reads the traffic field. Retaining
exact labels while silently rewriting the traffic they describe cannot be
called an exact field/count coupling. Changing those labels to match the
average would instead change their no-passing history. The example isolates
this projection problem; it does not prove all CTM schemes fail, establish a
reached E1/meter case, or demand an exact solution of the continuum equations.

There is a constructive repair: reconstruct the traffic between the already
retained count locations. If two locations carry the same count, the interval
between them stays empty. Between other locations, use the average density
consistent with their count difference. Cell totals, storage bounds and all
distinct records can be preserved. This is still first-order macroscopic
traffic with continuous mass; it adds no individual vehicle dynamics.

## The next decision: how the field and exact counts share an update

**Problem statement (S3-G2-Q.J.F):** after a numerical field update, must the
field preserve the exact traffic counts at retained restriction positions, or
may that compatibility have a declared numerical error?

**Model-chain function:** the reconstructed lane field supplies the next own
motion and receiving calculation. Count labels, positions and continuous mass
must describe a declared common state before E1 births are attached.

**Core gap:** exact record retention is approved, but ordinary numerical field
error is allowed. Those approvals do not specify whether a projection may
change the cumulative mass at a retained record. The example shows that total
mass conservation alone cannot decide this. This affects the common reference
and comparison contract, even though the component families stay the same.

### A — preserve the retained count locations in the field (recommended)

Use cell-boundary counts and each existing restriction's position/count as
reconstruction locations. Keep their counts unchanged and average only between
those locations. Equal counts create an empty interval. Coincident compatible
locations share a field location while all distinct kinetic records remain.
Reject incompatible candidate data rather than clipping or relabeling them.

**Pros:** conserves mass and preserves the information already approved;
resolves the demonstrated contradiction; keeps ordinary numerical averaging
where it does not contradict retained counts. With no interior records, the
local reconstruction becomes ordinary uniform cell averaging.

**Cons:** this coupling is a review-derived adaptation, not the published
complete multilane recipe. The flux solver must generate compatible counts
and positions. Local reconstruction alone does not prove arbitrary coupled
motion, node interactions, clearance or convergence.

**Dependencies and consequences:** freeze exact field/count compatibility as
the shared interface, then qualify its endogenous motion/flux continuation.
M.A, N.A, E1 and K.A stay fixed. This changes the numerical field reconstruction
and its reference/comparison contract, not the first-order traffic physics or
the retained information inventory. No storage-cost argument is involved.

### B — retain ordinary cell averaging with an explicit compatibility-error contract

Keep all restriction records and their positions/kinetics, while allowing the
numerical field to differ from their exact count anchors. Specify how that
discrepancy enters own motion and how it is bounded or vanishes on refinement.
Do not silently replace exact record histories by labels from the new average.

**Pros:** retains a source-style cell projection and accepts that a numerical
field need not be an exact continuum solution. A qualified error contract could
be legitimate; the local counterexample does not rule that out. For one
projection of a bounded-density field, count error can be bounded by cell
length times jam density, so that local bound shrinks with cell refinement.

**Cons:** no such joint field/count/motion error contract is established here.
The single-projection bound, cell-total conservation and prescribed-path count
bounds do not establish convergence of the repeatedly coupled motion/field
update. An unqualified discrepancy may alter motion and obscure a fair
reduction comparison.

**Dependencies and consequences:** explicitly declare the allowed discrepancy
at Q, then qualify the coupled approximation before using it as the reference.
K.A remains the selected timing law; this does not select the earlier K.C
single-speed law. No records may be discarded or merged lossily.

**Provisional recommendation: A.** A concrete compatible reconstruction now
exists and uses existing information. B remains viable if a joint numerical
error qualification can be supplied. Neither field interface is adopted.
This pause concerns information consistency, not another permission to do
ordinary synchronization or to continue reading papers.
