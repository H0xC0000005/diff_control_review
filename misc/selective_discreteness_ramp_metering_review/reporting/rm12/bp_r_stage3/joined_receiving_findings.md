## The traffic meaning of the receiving result

Imagine an already active slow entrant moving away from the merge. Following
traffic can enter behind it, but the entrant determines how tightly that traffic
can discharge. When the entrant accelerates, the merge does not immediately
receive the benefit: the less restrictive traffic state travels back to the
merge as a wave.

In a simple triangular-FD diagnostic, an entrant travels at 5 m/s and changes
to 15 m/s at second 10. With a backward-wave speed of 5 m/s, the change reaches
the merge at second 20. At second 15, the saturated merge still receives 0.375
vehicle equivalents/s, although substituting the entrant's current speed would
give 0.5625. The latter shortcut lets traffic in too early. This is a
prescribed-path hand calculation, not a trajectory produced by an optimized
meter or by the journal's acceleration parameters.

There is a second, complementary effect. If little traffic has entered for a
while, space remains behind the restriction. A later burst can initially use
that space faster than the restriction's saturated following flow. In the
constant-speed diagnostic, an entrance left unused for ten seconds can then
admit at ordinary road capacity until second 24. Applying a permanent reduced
cap from the beginning would hold traffic unnecessarily.

Thus the required information is **the own traffic field and its wave/count
history**, including how much receiving was actually used. This agrees with
the selected exact-state route; it does not justify an extra scalar penalty,
a permanent capacity discount or a new actuator. The
[derivation and examples](../../../synthesis/rm12/bp_r_stage3/joined_receiving_math.pdf)
separate saturated flow from actual admission.

A spatial aggregate model with suitable wave or memory state can also retain
these effects. This result identifies a receiving obligation; it does not prove
that every restriction record is necessary or that aggregate competitors fail.
