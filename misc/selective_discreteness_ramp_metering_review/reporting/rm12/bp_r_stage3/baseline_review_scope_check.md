# Where the baseline review stands, and how much detail it still needs

Date: 2026-10-09. Discussion and scope assessment only. No model adoption,
acceptance amendment, new review execution or Stage 4 opening.

The traffic-model backbone is recognizable. A rate command admits traffic to
the ramp; the ramp transports and queues that traffic; actual merging can create
slow moving restrictions; those restrictions and the lane traffic jointly
determine service; service determines exits and complete system time. The main
unfinished issue is whether these functions form one autonomous, consistently
repeatable model. Listing the modules alone cannot settle that issue.

However, there is a real risk of spending too long qualifying individual
interactions before assessing the whole task. We have a constructive joint
one-step example, but not a qualified complete evolution or a full-system-time
response to metering. More elaborate local examples do not automatically close
either gap. The next work should be measured against a bounded whole-model
closure target, rather than the number of local certificates accumulated.

This is a recommendation about emphasis and closure discipline, not approval to
omit an existing obligation. If the proposed bound would change the admitted
state scope or acceptance criterion, that change needs an explicit decision.

## What the current construction can establish

The review began with compact competitors for the moving-restriction reference.
Present-entry mean service and a demand-triggered capacity-drop rule did not
automatically supply the required transient recovery description. The user then
approved keeping every distinct restriction and its needed own-model history,
before attempting reduction. State size and processing cost are not rejection
criteria.

The current primary construction therefore aims first to establish a sound
full-information chain. It can anchor subsequent comparisons, but completing it
alone would not establish either compact sufficiency or the necessity of exact
restriction retention. Its final reference/comparator role also needs to be
stated when the study contract is bound. Calling it compact would obscure the
approved change in scope.

Three conclusions must remain separate: a specified independent forward model;
a methodologically credible competing representation under matched conditions;
and demonstrated predictive or gradient performance. This methodology review
can qualify the first two and plan evidence for the third. It does not conduct
the experiment or presume a superiority result.

## Four remaining milestones, with a reason to stop at each

**1. Consolidate the complete forward-model backbone.** Put the state and the
whole update in one readable contract: what the ramp and lane fields contain;
what restriction records and histories contain; how actual transfers are
accepted; when effects are created and moved; and how the resulting state feeds
the next regular step. Separate published laws, approved adaptations and still
conditional proposals. Bind the own-field representation at its declared model
level; comparing every possible numerical realization is unnecessary.

The current sender-side D choice belongs here. Merge priority decides who gets
receiving space; D decides whether a donor's remaining lane-change request can
use sending capacity left unused by a blocked through movement. This changes
accepted traffic and possibly later restriction creation. It is a forward-law
choice, not just a coding detail. D1 and D2 remain unselected; this scope check
does not resolve them.

Stop when there is one complete candidate update with explicit dependencies
and limitations, or a precisely identified coupling that cannot yet be supplied
without a new unsupported assumption. Another local example is useful only if
it closes one of those named dependencies.

**2. Qualify autonomous evolution and complete accounting.** Establish that the
candidate uses its own state and accepted flows, conserves mass, respects
sending/receiving/storage, and gives consistent restriction attachment and motion
through the interactions admitted by the task. The next step must remain defined
after a request is exhausted or effects meet or cross a boundary. Explain how
finite offered traffic drains, or how a convergent remaining-time tail is
accounted for. Check the no-restriction limit against the chosen ordinary
traffic model.

This needs a defensible composition and continuation argument, supported by
targeted analytic checks and the relevant source results. A missing next-state
rule for an included encounter cannot be deferred as engineering. Conversely,
implementing a general solver, tuning its mesh, benchmarking it, or proving a
theorem for arbitrary road networks is not the review target. Exact retention
of information does not require an exact numerical solution of every possible
traffic wave.

Stop with a qualified forward/accounting chain for the declared task, or a
bounded evidence-gap/failure report. Do not silently restrict the task to the
successful one-step example to obtain a pass.

**3. Qualify the credible competing model and fair comparison.** After the
exact-chain qualification, return to the deferred reduction question. Specify
the strongest relevant source-grounded aggregate, memory or lane-aware
competitor: its state, public initialization projection, own evolution and
service law. It must predict future transfers independently rather than
receiving them from the detailed model. The reviewed mean-service and
capacity-drop families supply candidates and known qualification limits;
neither is automatically admitted or universally refuted.

Keep geometry, offered traffic, physical parameter access, upstream rate
commands and complete-time accounting comparable. A lane-aggregate model already
retains lane information. A meaningful baseline need not be lane blind, and
failure of one mean formula does not establish failure of all aggregate models.
No compression budget or requirement that the richer model win is added.

Stop with a concrete credible comparison contract, a supported sufficient
reduction, or a precise reason that the proposed competitor still lacks a
qualified law. This is where the compact-baseline question is answered at the
methodological level; empirical competitiveness remains later evidence.

**4. Audit the control question and close the formed task.** Establish whether
the same admissible rate changes can affect external exit timing and complete
all-stream system time in the declared task. Merely moving waiting traffic from
the mainline to the ramp is insufficient. Use a naturally admissible analytic
case or an adequately qualified source result; do not create a case solely to
defeat the competitor. Include upstream waiting and clearance, and inspect
whether the setting makes control irrelevant or trivially dominant for a
structural reason.

Then state exactly what the comparison can test: which omitted information
might change the response, what a sufficient compact model would mean, and what
is not yet evidenced. An unresolved performance difference can be a legitimate
future study question; it must not be presented as already proven. Close Stage 3
with a coherent task/comparison, a scoped negative, or an explicit partial result.
Detailed differentiation, gradient correctness and one-step usefulness belong
to the separately opened Stage 4, not to an indefinite expansion of this step.

## Practical depth check

The recent gates are justified when they change physical predictions or make
the next state undefined. Their existence does not justify an unbounded campaign
to certify every solver case before showing the whole contract and its control
purpose. The recommended emphasis is a whole-model closure inventory before
further local derivations: for each remaining gap, identify the affected module
or interface, why it prevents one of the four milestones above, and what minimum
evidence would close it. Follow only those gaps.

This follows the existing roadmap's order: exact construction before reduction,
task/control/comparison qualification in Stage 3, and gradient-study readiness
in Stage 4. It proposes no new physical mechanism, baseline advantage, topology,
actuator or approximation. An amendment to the current encounter/continuation
acceptance obligation, if needed, must be discussed explicitly rather than
inferred from this summary.
