## The traffic meaning of the new finding

Imagine plenty of waiting ramp traffic. A fully open entrance could serve one
vehicle equivalent per second, but congestion leaves receiving space for only
0.4 per second. Give a physical signal half green time. It admits 0.4 during
green and zero during red: its average is 0.2. The current model instead grants
a continuous permission of 0.5 and admits the available 0.4 throughout.

The difference is when the receiving limitation acts. A continuously enforced
rate target can use all receiving below its target. A fixed green schedule
cannot use receiving during red. Shortening the cycle does not remove this
particular discrepancy while green fraction and receiving stay unchanged.
This is a local operator diagnostic, not a simulated scenario or a full-TST
comparison. An actuator that adjusts its timing to deliver an effective rate
is another interpretation; its physical realization has not been qualified.

The nearest [continuum-signal analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.3461)
provides conditional count-error results, but its average-service rule also
differs from our absolute rate cap. Its theorems do not cover the present
accelerating merge restrictions. Nothing here proves rate modeling inherently
unsound or requires replacing it with binary signals.

There is a second distinction at restriction generation. In the selected
source rule, a final 0.4 equivalent transferred from zero counter progress
creates no new record. The traffic is fully accounted for; the rule simply
has no new discrete disturbance at that scale. Refining time does not remove
that terminal remainder. The source permits cumulative-floor generation and
reports tests using Poisson generation with similar macroscopic results;
that is not evidence of identical individual-event or gradient behavior.
[Supplied journal](../../../archive/user_supplied/rm12_bp_r_stage3/Lane-changing%20in%20traffic%20streams.pdf), Section 2.2.
We cannot interpret fractional fluid mass as a missing physical vehicle, or
claim every such fraction must create its own blockage.

## The decision: what reference are we qualifying?

**Problem statement:** should the study continue with the current idealized
effective-rate reference, or amend the actuator to qualify physical signal
averaging/timing first?

**Model-chain function:** the selected entrance rule determines admission,
ramp queues and speeds, later merging and restriction generation, and ultimately
exit timing and total system time. Every future comparison must share its
meaning and control interface.

**Core gap:** the current formula and the inspected signal literature do not
establish the proposed pulse-to-rate bridge throughout the admitted finite-
receiving domain. Small count errors also do not automatically preserve floor-
generated record sets near a threshold. Exact retention after birth cannot
repair either upstream modeling difference.

### A — keep the idealized effective-rate task

Study a regulator that continuously permits a bounded admission rate, with
actual admission limited by availability and receiving. Keep the selected
source birth law and E1 timing. Continue qualifying the exact model under
those explicit semantics; physical pulse equivalence remains unestablished.

**Pros:** preserves the approved task and deterministic first study; supplies
a clear common action; needs no new traffic law or discarded information.
It permits answering which retained information matters within this qualified
model family, if the remaining model/control chain can be established.

**Cons:** eventual results are conditional on that reference. They cannot
establish fidelity to literal red/green launches, to every isolated entrant,
or useful physical-controller gradients. Threshold-induced effects alone are
insufficient evidence of a genuine retained-information advantage.

**Dependency/consequence:** accepts the explicitly limited qualification scope
at Q; it does not reselect M.A or certify a complete reference. Keep small-transfer
cases and all waiting in the review. No criterion is silently waived and no
positive full-chain result is presumed.

### B — qualify a supply-scaled continuum signal rule

Keep a continuous control, but let a green fraction scale the entrance's
effective service after receiving limits it. This represents average available
green service rather than the current absolute rate permission. For the example
above, its saturated average is 0.2.

**Pros:** follows the inspected signal-junction formulation; addresses the
specific operator discrepancy while retaining continuous controls and mass.

**Cons:** changes admission and therefore queues, entry speeds and control
responses. Existing convergence results have regime restrictions and do not
establish the accelerating-merge or threshold-event bridge. It is still an
average model, not exact individual launches.

**Dependency/consequence:** explicitly reopen the actuator/action mapping
within M.A/G1 and its shared comparison contract. Qualify this adaptation before
joining; meter location, N.A and E1 need not change automatically.

### C — qualify finite signal pulses

Retain when permission is green and red in a declared fixed pulse family, and
qualify its actual release response to waiting, receiving and any startup law.
Traffic quantity can remain continuous. A binary fluid permission alone does
not reproduce one-vehicle detector operation.

**Pros:** represents the timing lost by averaging and gives a direct framework
for testing signal/receiving interactions. Physical signal operation is supported
by [FHWA guidance](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/ramp_mgmt_handbook/manual/manual/5_1.htm), Section 5.3.6.

**Cons:** requires more actuator/launch and timing qualification; changing
timing can change event sets and later gradient semantics. It does not itself
fix the descriptor remainder or prove a complete control model.

**Dependency/consequence:** reopen the common actuator/action contract before
dependent joining. Use no integer mass rounding, assumed birth per green,
unapproved acceleration rule or automatic change to E1.

**User decision: A approved, 2026-10-08.** It preserves the explicitly selected rate
task and lets us first establish its exact forward/control chain. B and C are
methodologically legitimate alternatives if physical signal interpretation is
required now; their differences concern behavior, not engineering cost.
