## BPI-G3 — accept the result and choose the next bounded question

**Decision:** the user selects candidate A after the semantic clarification.
BP-R's claim/direction is approved; candidates B/C remain unselected alternatives.
The [Stage 2 protocol](../../../rm12_50_bp_r_stage2_protocol.md) transcribes
the selected bounded work. The question/candidate reasoning below is retained.

**Problem statement:** after this evidence-limited domain audit, should the next
pass explicitly change the comparison claim to BP-R, seek a concrete missing
operational contract, or park the branch?

**Assumed model-chain function:** the information contract governs what both
controllers can know. Dynamics and common rates govern service and exits;
complete TST governs usefulness. The research claim governs whether the study
must establish unrecoverable information or relative fidelity of a reduction.

**Core gap:** there is not yet an admissible full knowledge contract for the
original claim. No complete physical rate/TST task has become proven merely
because its controller uses detector aggregates. Accepting missing evidence as
the bounded outcome does not approve changing the claim.

### Candidate A — close Stage 1 and open BP-R comparison admission

Accept Stage 1's mixed evidence-limited disposition. Explicitly select the
relative representation/control-response claim for Stage 2: can a qualified
compact aggregate/memory model preserve the intervention response of one
source-supported richer local merge family? Use shared declared initialization,
public forcing and common open-loop rates; do not require initialization to be
hidden. First check genuine rate-to-entry-to-exit/TST leverage, then the strongest
plausible reduction. Reuse the existing physical/service and compact-model
frontier; recovery is a candidate, not the preset winner. Stop if source transfer
needs a new law/actuator, compact sufficiency survives, or useful rate leverage
cannot be supported. No numerical experiment is part of admission.

**Pros:** targets the representation question without inventing unavailable
history; reuses reviewed physics and strong competitors; avoids deepening an
unsupported inverse problem. A compact-sufficiency finding is informative.

**Cons:** changes the proof target. A positive would be relative to qualified
reductions, not proof that lane information is fundamentally unrecoverable.
No richer-state necessity, positive control effect or gradient result is assured.

**Dependency/consequence:** user approval now explicitly adopts BP-R's claim.
Original information findings remain intact; physical task changes retain their
own gate. Stage 2's scope must name its family/rivals and admission obligations.

### Candidate B — repair the original operational-contract gap first

Retain the original hidden-information claim. Obtain/inspect the exact v1.09
specification and its relevant site interface/initialization/logging documents,
or an equivalently complete primary setting, then transcribe all accessible
records and physical knowledge. A specification that initializes program
counters alone would not resolve initial traffic knowledge. Only a supported
contract justifies a later full-history/action analysis. Close this bounded
repair if the critical fields remain unknown; do not reopen elementary roots
or broaden to an indefinite all-site search.

**Pros:** preserves the stronger original claim and directly addresses its
remaining prerequisite. Additional accessible records could fairly establish
sufficiency as well as support an unresolved domain.

**Cons:** no concrete complete document has been retrieved; another ordinary
literature sweep has no demonstrated yield. Interface documentation may still
fail to bind operational initial knowledge; ambiguity and useful rates remain
separate downstream obligations.

**Dependency/consequence:** a targeted primary-document lead is needed to justify
fresh work beyond this completed frontier. No assumed cold start, record deletion,
private driver forcing or privileged fine initialization is acceptable.

### Candidate C — accept Stage 1 and park this continuation

Preserve the operational lead and the precise knowledge-contract gap as future
reopen conditions. Open neither a new observer law nor a relative comparison.

**Pros:** prevents a speculative claim/task change and retains a useful bounded
negative-admission record for colleague discussion.

**Cons:** leaves the freeway research task and gradient-study plan unfinished.

**Dependency/consequence:** a later reopening requires a newly justified source
contract or explicit task/claim choice, not simply another mechanism layer.

**Provisional recommendation: A**, if the aim remains to find a defensible
freeway representation/gradient study. The missing full-history contract makes
further original-route work poorly targeted without a concrete new lead. BP-R
can inspect model adequacy under explicit common knowledge, while preserving
the separate rate-leverage and accounting filters. This is a planning
recommendation, not automatic adoption or a promised positive result.
