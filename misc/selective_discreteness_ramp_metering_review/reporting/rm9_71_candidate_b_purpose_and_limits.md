## Existing evidence versus the new obligation

| Aspect | Already established | What B would have to add |

| Deterministic propagation | A uniquely known complete state and public inputs determine later states and action responses | Nothing overturns this; that domain remains excluded |

“Initial aggregate information” includes the approved speed channel and the
declared face/ramp information, not just a total-density snapshot. A hypothetical
controller taking over an already occupied motorway illustrates incomplete
initial knowledge, but is not an admitted task or evidence. Its observation-start
epoch and lack of earlier complete knowledge must be independently defensible;
no already available observations may be discarded. Known inputs cannot be
made private to manufacture ambiguity.

This is new work only if it moves from conditional lemmas and snapshot structure
to a bound, defensible initial-information domain and a whole-history/action
analysis. Searching for more papers saying merges are complex is not enough.
Literature supports the physical/information setting; reviewer analytic reasoning
must establish the adapted model's identification and action claims separately.

## What can reasonably be expected

There is a mathematical reason not to dismiss B outright: snapshot roots can
exist, and deterministic evolution alone does not identify an unknown start.
There is no established evidence that these roots survive the full history,
remain distinct when actions act, or require different useful actions. Thus B
has a reason to be considered, but no demonstrated positive yield or probability.
Methodological uncertainty alone is not a reason for unlimited further search.

Use three sequential evidence gates if B is selected:

1. Find and transcribe a genuine initial-information domain while retaining C,
   all admitted history and public laws/inputs. If unsupported, stop with that
   evidence gap; do not construct a favorable initialization instead.
2. In that domain, analyze complete-history identification. Unique reconstruction
   strengthens a scoped negative result. Multiple compatible initial states are
   insufficient if they converge to the same decision-time complete state.
3. For a surviving decision-time distinction, compare the same feasible schedules
   and all-stream TST. Different state or forward behavior without different
   useful actions is a partial, action-negative result. A supported useful-action
   distinction is a candidate positive task, not yet proof of correct/useful
   gradients or cross-fidelity validity.

The existing literature packet does not yet supply the domain/witness required
for steps 2–3. A bounded review can promise an explicit feasibility/evidence
disposition, not necessarily a positive task or a stronger mathematical negative
theorem. A failure at step 1 leaves the current scoped result intact.
