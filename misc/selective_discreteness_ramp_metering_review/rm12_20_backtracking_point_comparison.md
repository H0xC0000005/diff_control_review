## Problem statement, model-chain function and core gap

**Problem:** which earlier methodological contract should be reconsidered before
another substantive ramp review, so the next pass addresses a diagnosed obstacle
rather than merely adds another physical mechanism?

**Model-chain function:** source task and controller information define the
initialization/observation contract; traffic dynamics translate common feasible
meter actions into merge service and cumulative exits; complete TST supplies
decision relevance; the comparison claim determines what retained state and
gradients must establish. Backtracking can change different links in this chain.

**Core gap:** recovery is source-supported physics, but there is no evidence
that it alone avoids the previous information/compact-closure obstruction. The
previous L proposal addressed control leverage without sufficiently justifying
why the physical/service link, rather than information, action or claim, should
be the first backtracking position. The comparison was authorized; subsequent
discussion expresses the BP-I-first preference and asks whether BP-R is a sound
fallback. This records that planning sequence without adopting its future task/
claim contracts or executing a substantive successor.

## Is recovery responsible for, or protected from, systematic failure?

**Neither a universal recovery failure nor immunity is established.** Separate:

- **Complete-state/history sufficiency:** a known complete state with exact
  deterministic public laws/inputs fixes future responses. Adding recovery
  cannot make that already known state hidden. Some simpler recovery subclasses
  retain only observed/reconstructible aggregate coordinates. This is the most
  established information obstruction; it is not specific to merge recovery.
- **Adequate compact representation:** RM0003's density/congestion-memory model
  shows that recovery need not require lane identity in that family. Richer
  transient recovery can still challenge particular closures. No exact universal
  reduction for every recovery model has been proved.
- **Useful control leverage:** physical discharge can vary, so recovery need not
  inherit the fixed-service result in which metering merely relocates waiting.
  Source intervention/control evidence supports this possibility, not our exact
  complete-TST benefit or a hidden-information gradient certificate.

Thus the current recovery proposal is logically defensible as a **task/control
admission question**, but insufficiently justified as the default escape from
the strongest diagnosed obstacle. Physics is not made artificial just because
a compact model captures it. Likewise, useful metering does not establish a need
for fine information. Source statements and inference limits are in the
[selected primary-source recheck](extraction/rm12/backtracking_source_recheck.md).

## BP-M — backtrack the physical merge/service contract

**Position:** RM8 family admission / RMD-G2–G4, before the elementary RM9 service
construction. Keep the local single-ramp rate/TST task; admit a source-grounded
dynamic discharge-recovery family rather than assume fixed effective service.
This is the location of the earlier L proposal.

**Question and resulting chain:** can two feasible common rate schedules alter
actual entry, recovery and exit timing enough to outweigh all holding costs?
Produce one finite-storage task-admission record or scoped rejection. Transcribe
source domains and adaptation assumptions; do not splice favorable fragments
from distinct models.

**Justification:** RM8-P01's hybrid merge mechanism and field comparison provide
physical/rate evidence. RM0003 supplies a compact dynamic alternative and
conditional control evidence. This directly targets a missing task/control
transfer, not another generic assertion that merging is complex.

**Pros:** preserves the action, objective and local scope; reuses reviewed
mechanisms; makes queue-relocation and horizon-accounting errors explicit;
can produce a useful ramp task even if a compact aggregate model suffices.

**Cons:** does not remove known-state/full-history sufficiency; strong aggregate
recovery can still be adequate. Even a positive task-admission result may leave
the core information question negative. RM10 already screened recovery for that
stronger question, so repeating the broad physical search is unjustified.

**Dependencies/consequences and stop:** physical-law or finite-storage/source
transfer needs explicit adoption. Reject/gate unsupported complete accounting.
A positive is a control-task result, not hiddenness or minimum resolution.
Recommended when control leverage itself is the desired immediate product;
lower priority as a cure for the information obstruction.

## BP-I — backtrack the operational information and initialization contract

**Position:** RM7 RMD-G3 / RM8 declared strong observer, before RM9's idealized
instantaneous cell-mass/section-speed benchmark. Keep the local rate decision and
strict information question, but first ground what a documented controller can
actually know from its available sensing, logs and initialization.

**Question and resulting chain:** does one genuine deployed/operational rate-meter
setting justify an information domain outside the known-complete-state case?
Transcribe detector locations, raw versus reported records, spatial/temporal
support, ramp observations, available history, initial state knowledge and public
inputs. Then determine whether current sufficiency results apply. Ordinary point
flow/occupancy/speed must not automatically be treated as exact cell inventory.
Do not assert hiddenness solely because devices measure different quantities.

**Justification:** RM9's measurement audit and FHWA distinguish point/event and
section quantities. RM8-P02 identifies an existing rate-meter setting and its
aggregate detector/control context. These provide concrete source leads for
grounding a different task contract; they do not yet establish non-observability
or the desired history/action certificate.

**Pros:** directly targets the strongest demonstrated obstruction; preserves
the intended hidden-information question and can retain rate/TST; lets genuine
operational evidence determine the state/measurement boundary instead of selecting
a mechanism to make the old idealized observer fail.

**Cons:** real sensing does not guarantee ambiguity after all available history;
source initialization/log availability may be undocumented. State estimation
becomes explicit. Detail unobserved by both controllers cannot be supplied as a
fine-state oracle to only the richer model. Positive physical uncertainty alone
does not prove that a mesoscopic representation can exploit it.

**Dependencies/consequences and stop:** this is a Type 2 task/observer amendment,
not a correction to the properly closed RM9 observer choice. Preserve every
actually available observation, common raw data, public forcing and history;
no artificial cold start, discarded event records or added measurement noise.
Reference fine truth is for evaluation, not privileged controller initialization.
If a genuine initial-information domain is unsupported or reconstruction removes
the residual, report that outcome and stop.

**Difference from earlier RM9-UG5.B:** B retained the elementary law and strong
observer, then pursued uncertain-start/full-history identification within that
fixed family. BP-I instead reselects the operational information contract from
source evidence first; it does not reopen B's depth-first classification. Its
bounded first output is an information-domain admission/rejection, not an unlimited
inverse-problem campaign. If it merely reproduces B's premises, stop and identify
that overlap rather than relabeling B as new work.

## BP-U — backtrack the meter actuator and release-pattern contract

**Position:** RMD-G1 rate/fixed-shape release boundary. Retain a local merge and
complete TST, but ask whether the source task calls for timing releases relative
to available gaps. This is the earlier unselected T direction.

**Question and resulting chain:** do operationally feasible timing degrees of
freedom let a meter act on local merge opportunities that the present permission-
rate mapping averages away? First compare the actual mappings: a sufficiently
resolved rate schedule may already encode the relevant patterns. Only then admit
a distinct timing task if necessary, with common safety/action/sensing semantics.

**Justification:** RM8-P02 offers a direct gap/class-conditioned release concept
at a real meter. It connects local detail to a concrete decision rather than
assuming fine traffic variation must affect a mean-rate choice.

**Pros:** provides a particularly direct information-to-action mechanism; keeps
the infrastructure meter and local freeway setting; can clarify whether the old
actuator, rather than recovery physics, suppressed useful distinctions.

**Cons:** inadequate rate leverage is currently a hypothesis, not an established
theorem. Source comparisons change sensing and timing together and include driver/
demarcation assumptions. Event/safety sensitivity is harder. This does not evade
known-complete-state sufficiency or prove additional information is unrecoverable.

**Dependencies/consequences and stop:** freeze the action/sensor distinction
explicitly; do not grant one comparator extra data or select unsafe common
actions. Controller feedback is separate from release resolution; timed releases
do not automatically require PID/MPC. Source vehicle releases do not approve
integer traffic mass. If existing admissible rates already express the actions,
BP-U is not a distinct actuator escape; if sensing changes are necessary,
combine with an explicit BP-I decision rather than silently bundling them.

## BP-R — backtrack the comparison claim, with a bounded closure question

**Position:** RM7/RM8 exact matched-history necessity target. Keep a source-grounded
local rate/TST task, but explicitly study accuracy of declared model reductions
even when detailed reference initialization is known or reconstructible.

**Question and resulting chain:** for one admitted local task, can a credible
compact dynamic closure preserve the rate-sensitive transient exit-time response
of the richer source family? Specify the compact state, retained alternative,
public initialization/projection and common actions. The first product is a
bounded closure-sufficiency/evidence assessment, not the entire minimum-resolution
or useful-gradient research programme.

**Justification:** RM0003 shows that representing a physical mode can affect
control without providing irreducible unknown information. S12A005's transient/
mean-capacity distinction supplies a closure lead, though not a rate/TST result.
Known state and inadequate reduced dynamics can coexist; there is no logical
need for in-principle non-reconstructibility in a fidelity comparison.

**Pros:** directly removes the conflict of demanding newly hidden state from a
known complete deterministic initialization; preserves the underlying resolution/
gradient motivation; supports strong ablations and compact-memory competitors.

**Cons:** changes the precise scientific claim. Positive findings are relative
to qualified reductions and reference domains, not proof that an ideal aggregate-
history controller can never recover relevant information. A poor memoryless
baseline cannot establish necessity when a compact memory rival suffices.

**Dependencies/consequences and stop:** explicit user adoption of the claim
change and one admitted task/control response are prerequisites. Reject a
comparison supported only by mean-capacity or forward-flow differences with no
common-action relevance. A compact sufficient closure is a valid outcome; no
fixed lane/cohort/identity winner and no verified gradient are presumed.

## Fair comparison

All positions retain method-first source grounding, continuous conserved mass,
receiving/storage, complete all-stream accounting, strong relevant competitors,
and distinct forward/gradient-correctness/usefulness obligations. Minimal change
is assessed across the scientific contract, not counted by equations or code.

| Position | Primary contract reconsidered | Obstacle it addresses most directly | What still remains | Evidence strength for the first bounded review |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BP-M | Physical service law/task transfer | Fixed-service/queue-relocation control failure | History identification and compact sufficiency | Direct physical/rate lead; no joined complete-TST/info certificate |
| BP-I | Operational information and initial knowledge | Applicability of ideal-state/history sufficiency | Full-history residual, useful action and fair state estimation | Measurement distinctions and actual detector setting; source-specific initialization unestablished |
| BP-U | Rate-to-release action boundary | Averaging away potentially useful timing | Information sufficiency, sensing confounds and event gradients | Direct timing-task source; isolated matched benefit unestablished |
| BP-R | Claim/comparator obligation | In-principle reconstructibility excluding a representation study | Real control leverage and strongest closure/gradient validation | Direct conceptual justification and fidelity leads; changes the claim |

No entry is proven to yield a positive selective-information/gradient task.
BP-M is the smallest physical continuation, BP-I the most direct source-domain
check for the retained information claim, BP-U an action alternative, and BP-R
the cleanest logical reformulation if a relative fidelity claim is acceptable.
They are different positions, not four successive compulsory stages.

## Provisional recommendation and next bounded output

**Recommend BP-I first if the original hidden-information question remains the
priority.** Current evidence for an information-contract obstruction is stronger
than evidence that rate actuation itself is the problem. Grounding the controller
knowledge in a genuine task therefore has a better diagnostic justification than
assuming recovery or timing will cure it. This recommendation promises an admission
decision, not a positive ambiguity result.

After selection, the first pass should audit **one documented rate-meter setting's
measurement/initial-knowledge contract**, using the nearest existing source
frontier. It should output a source-grounded domain outside the current sufficiency
result, a sufficiency/overlap finding, or an explicit missing-evidence stop. It
should not solve a complete new observer, invent a favorable cold start, execute
traffic, or broaden into every freeway sensor/model. Any new sensing/task law is
presented for adoption before the corresponding analytic task work proceeds.

**If relative representation fidelity is already the desired claim, BP-R is the
better position.** Select its bounded closure question and an admitted physical
task explicitly. BP-M can then supply a missing control-leverage prerequisite;
it still should not be sold as evidence of irreducible hidden information.
BP-U is justified first only if the operational actuator mapping demonstrably
excludes the relevant timing actions. No fixed M → I → U → R fallback is approved.

The earlier coordinated multi-ramp extension remains deferred under its positive-
local-result prerequisite. A corridor re-selection would reopen that prerequisite
and task scope; it is not smuggled into any candidate here. Revisiting complete
TST to omit waiting is not an admissible backtracking position.

## Subsequent sequence discussion — BP-I first, BP-R conditionally

The user favors BP-I first because it targets the diagnosed obstacle with a
smaller amendment, then BP-R if BP-I yields a negative result. **This is a sound
planning order**, with two clarifications:

1. BP-I tests whether a genuine operational information contract supports the
   original question. It does not guarantee that the obstacle is bypassed, and
   must preserve available observations/history and fair initial knowledge.
2. BP-R changes the proof target/comparison claim to relative representation and
   control-sensitivity fidelity. It need not change the physical merge, rate
   actuator or complete-TST objective. It does not overturn an information-
   sufficiency result or make an action-irrelevant task useful.

Disposition must distinguish what the BP-I result says:

| BP-I outcome | Consequence for the proposed order |
|---|---|
| Genuine partial-information domain admitted, but useful hidden-state distinction not yet established | Continue its bounded history/action obligations; domain admission alone is not a completed positive research result |
| Relevant state is reconstructible/sufficient under the grounded full history | Close the scoped information result; BP-R is a justified subsequent claim/comparison alternative |
| Initial knowledge or source-domain support remains insufficient | Record an evidence-limited outcome, not an impossibility theorem; BP-R may be considered with a defensible physical task and declared initialization |
| No useful common rate-action effect survives complete accounting | First admit a genuine control-relevant task or explicitly change the relevant task/actuator contract; changing the claim alone cannot repair this failure |

BP-I was selected, planned, executed and closed as Stage 1. Its completed audit
supports a sensing distinction but leaves the full knowledge contract evidence-
limited. The user has now selected BP-R's changed claim and bounded comparison
admission for Stage 2. Any information/task amendment remains explicit. This
selection preserves the original findings; it does not authorize automatic later
stages or silently relax their acceptance requirements.
