## Why the arrangement is coherent

A estimates the mean effect of the insertion/acceleration mechanism; B uses a
calibrated capacity-drop trigger. As currently proposed they are distinct
approximations, with no established inclusion or dominance relation. A's
priority follows continuity with the current review, not a judgment that B is
methodologically inferior. B is a present companion, rather than a fallback
held until A produces a negative result.

Both draw on a common source/task envelope and may retain lane and spatial
queue information. Sharing that groundwork avoids repeated extraction and
inconsistent control/accounting assumptions. Their service laws and failure
attribution remain separate: evidence for one is not automatically a proof
about the other. Their coexistence is not a new blended traffic equation.

## Fairness and claims

- Common geometry, upstream permissions, physical population and complete TST
  remain mandatory. Both models evolve their own traffic and any declared memory.
- A's stationary-source support does not certify its changing-traffic embedding.
  Failure can indicate missing memory or another constitutive approximation;
  it does not prove that individual identity is necessary.
- B's empirical/phenomenological rationale is legitimate. A mismatched native
  trigger is a predictive-model issue to qualify. It may remain useful as a
  diagnostic companion without being certified as a mechanism-matched reduction.
- **No inactive-criterion waiver is adopted.** Merely reviewing B alongside A
  does not adopt the earlier sole-primary B proposal's acceptance amendment.
  Any later proposal to admit a failed limit under a broader definitive claim
  must identify that change and return to the user.
- Success of a compact model in a qualified domain is a valid result. Forward
  response fidelity, gradient correctness and gradient usefulness remain separate.
