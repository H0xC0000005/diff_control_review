## A01 — supplied Laval–Daganzo lane-changing journal

[Archived original](../../../archive/user_supplied/rm12_bp_r_stage3/Lane-changing%20in%20traffic%20streams.pdf)

[Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trb.2005.04.003)

Reread original PDF pages 4–6 via the existing local extraction. Equations
7–10 define desired advancing/movement amounts and sampled choice fractions;
12–13 prorate shared receiving according to oriented incoming demands.
Source IT treats each target independently subject to its original disaggregated
sending, and does not impose full diverge FIFO. These arguments alone do not
specify a general own-field physical-time lift with unspent T.A claims.

## W16N — Wright, Gomes, Horowitz and Kurzhanskiy, high-dimensional node models

[Primary author manuscript, arXiv:1601.01054](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.01054)

31 PDF pages. Read sections 2.1 and 3.1, especially requirement 7, equations
3.1–3.7 and remarks 3.1–3.2. Priorities govern relative receiving claims;
only their ratios matter. A restricted donor's physical demand can increase
to capacity, making current-demand-dependent priorities problematic for
invariance. The paper also distinguishes strict and partial FIFO. Its
general-node solution cannot be claimed equivalent to source IT merely by
sharing demand/supply inequalities. This supports P.A's fixed-weight family
and the need to qualify P.B, not a theorem choosing our sampled weights.

## W16D — Wright, Horowitz and Kurzhanskiy, dynamic node characterization

[Primary author manuscript, arXiv:1608.07623](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.07623)

Six PDF pages. Read merge cumulative states, demand-completion transitions,
general-node definition 3/input deadlines and section 4.3. The model interprets
node algorithms through accumulated transfers and events; it suggests using
varying demands/supplies for network evolution. It does not prove our
moving-restriction/source loop or authorize translating its snapshot-node
execution clock/deadlines into our physical clock. Do not import these as a
new nominal cap or confuse a request state with population.

## J12 — Jin, general-junction Riemann solver

[Primary author manuscript, arXiv:1204.6727](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.6727)

29 PDF pages. Read section 3.1, admissible initial/stationary/interior states,
and the stated fair-merge/FIFO-diverge scope. Boundary states of zero spatial
measure can make a discrete node rule compatible with wave solutions. This
provides a framework, but the theorem's FIFO coupling differs from native IT;
it cannot certify the new oriented fork/merge by substitution.

## What the present derivations add

The T.A early-depletion/stop wave case is exact original analytical work.
The priority-versus-live-demand comparison embeds two mainline donors, one
parallel through movement, a finite ramp, N.A and an old recipient restriction.
The P.B scalar equation is a reviewer-derived local completion using source
IT and standard boundary-wave admissibility, with positive fixed choice
fraction, one unconstrained parallel recipient and pending allowances. It is
not asserted as a published general non-FIFO theorem or a finite-CTM convergence
proof. Neither diagnostic is a reached policy or useful-gradient witness.
