## Control relevance and its limit

[RM8-P01, Sections 3–4 and Figure 6](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/f/3571/files/2023/03/merges.pdf)
compares regimes with ramp flow 500 veh/h but total discharge about 9,000 versus
7,800 veh/h. The authors associate the latter with queued, slower ramp entries.
This supports a mechanism involving entry conditions, beyond ramp flow alone.
Its demand/regime comparison is not our fixed-offered-demand upstream-permission
experiment and does not establish full TST improvement or compact-model failure.
The paper's freeway lane count was not inferred from those capacities.

The selected upstream permission can alter approach congestion, actual entry
times and entry speeds; these can affect constraints and eventual exits. This
is a credible dependency path, not a proved nondominance theorem. Complete
waiting costs and source-consistent clearance still matter.

## Why this is a formulation gap rather than a required experiment

A ready closure with unknown predictive accuracy is suitable for later testing.
An unspecified rule for evolving its speed, mode or moments is not. Likewise,
using the reference's future entry speeds to evaluate a compact capacity formula
would make the comparison dependent on reference truth. Declaring instantaneous
equilibrium, inventing recovery dynamics, or changing birth acceleration to
match an analytical formula would add an assumption requiring explicit review.
No such repair is adopted here.
