## DG-G1 — Which first reference domain?

**Problem statement:** Should the first diagnostic isolate a permanent physical
lane reduction or begin with an empirically calibrated temporary closure?

**Assumed model-chain function:** Native driving supplies reachable hidden lane
states and independent outcomes for shared desired-speed advice.

**Core gap:** The documentary review gives mechanism precedents, not validated
instances settling the approved root claim. No calibrated temporary-closure
dataset or installed reference has been established.

**Candidate A: controlled synthetic permanent 3→2 (recommended).** Use the
contract's homogeneous traffic, native merging and known fixed response gain.
**Pros:** Isolates continuing-lane balance; bounded setup and exact accounting;
tests information loss before expensive model construction.
**Cons:** Synthetic response and permanent geometry limit transfer; preparations
may fail to retain a useful matched contrast. It cannot substantiate temporary
closure or field compliance claims.

**Candidate B: calibrated temporary-closure reference.** Obtain suitable demand,
geometry and response data, calibrate without action-benefit tuning, and include
closure and post-reopening recovery in every scored rollout.
**Pros:** Stronger operational relevance and direct recovery evidence.
**Cons:** Additional data/access, calibration, engine and horizon contracts;
closure-event artifacts can obscure information loss. Requires a revised larger
plan, not a mechanical extension of A.

**Approved selection (2026-09-17):** A first. Keep B deferred, not rejected as a
scientific possibility; a later temporary-closure claim must reopen domain scope.

## DG-G2 — What establishes lost aggregate information?

**Problem statement:** Require identical declared aggregate/history inputs first,
or authorize a bounded approximate-match study now?

**Assumed model-chain function:** The observation channel determines what a
lane-blind controller can know before choosing common advice.

**Core gap:** Reachable states with equal strong history and useful hidden
contrast are not established. Similar averages alone do not rule out an adequate
aggregate response/history observer.

**Candidate A: exact-first with matching-feasibility stop (recommended).** Freeze
the full channel; search only the declared preparation bank; test actions only
after exact matching, with finer-grid and time-step audits.
**Pros:** Direct information-collision argument for the specified input channel;
no new macro implementation or unjustified residual threshold.
**Cons:** Exact float/history matching may be infeasible; a failed search neither
refutes the hypothesis nor proves aggregate sufficiency. Scope remains limited
to the declared initial open-loop information channel.

**Candidate B: bounded approximate matching with a pinned observer contract.**
First construct/validate a strong aggregate observer and an error/sensitivity
envelope; require decision differences to exceed what measured residual inputs
could explain. Preserve full history and publish every residual.
**Pros:** More accessible under native microscopic evolution; can test realistic
observation uncertainty without requiring bit-identical histories.
**Cons:** Observer-specific evidence, additional implementation and sensitivity
validation; unsupported tolerances risk manufacturing apparent information loss.
It needs a separate accepted error contract and fair comparison, not relaxed A.

**Approved selection (2026-09-17):** A, then stop if necessary. The next important
review-revealed choice is whether actual residuals justify planning B, a scenario
revision or deferral. Do not silently transfer to B.

## DG-R1 — Which information claim should the next methodological study pursue?

response prediction and a common-advice decision. A refinement can contribute

identify which rather than infer it from lane-dependent trajectories.

full-history-matched, decision-distinct pair. The review found observability and
abstraction qualifications, not measured matching failure. Hidden past lane-entry
forcing and richer-state oracle access need explicit interpretation. An exact
channel argument and a model-class performance comparison are not interchangeable.

**Candidate A: retain exact information-loss target; seek an analytical/
constructive certificate (recommended).** In a tractable source-grounded traffic
subcase, specify admissible preparations and known/hidden past inputs; establish
the actual declared equal observation/history channel and different useful
common-advice decisions. Preserve strong response/history and conservation.
If the required pair cannot be justified, report that rather than assume a
lane-dependent penalty or quietly shorten the history. This is a methodological
construction study, not implementation or adoption of a new candidate simulator.

**Pros:** Most faithful to the accepted conditional contribution; separates
information value from calibration/optimizer error; can clarify a compressed

**Cons:** Exact history reachability may remain unproved or fail; a conditional
model certificate does not establish field benefit, general SUMO behavior,

**Dependencies/consequences:** G2/G3 remain unchanged; specify the realistic
information availability boundary and compare the full action set needed by
the claim. Later empirical and independent-gradient evidence remain separate.

**Pros:** Can address practically useful finite representations without demanding

**Cons:** Changes the claim to model/observer adequacy; extra dynamics, estimation

**Pros:** No unsupported construction or favorable-case search.
**Cons:** Leaves the endorsed root question unsettled.
**Dependencies/consequences:** User deferral decision; no sufficiency/refutation claim.

advice split. The nine-pulse menu omits held100, so a restricted-menu distinction
cannot exclude that common continuous-space alternative.

candidate simulator. Retain known-empty preparation, the full observation/input

shared-advice/merge model contract.** First formulate the smallest continuous
mechanism capable of violating fixed-service/arrival-dominance premises: e.g.,
finite receiving-limited paired transfer coupled to advice-dependent approach
residence and endogenous response. State equations, units, donor/receiver

physical admissibility without assuming benefit. Preserve full aggregate history
and disclose unknown past lane inputs. Obtain user approval of that model
contract before using its action consequences as a certificate. An obstruction
is an admissible outcome.

**Pros:** Keeps work analytical; can isolate a minimal mechanism and make the
observation/service boundary inspectable before reference-run expense.
Deterministic conservative mass may avoid categorical shifts and event resets.
**Cons:** No complete source-admissible coupling is established. Adds a
substantive model choice/adaptation burden; convenient rates or penalties
chosen to force a split would be circular. A conditional proof would still not
validate native traffic, empirical response or gradients. More review may end
at another precise obstruction.
**Dependencies/consequences:** Requires a separately approved scientific model
contract; does not replace the approved native domain or silently alter G1/G2.
Changes to advice, history, hard-switch gradient treatment or Gap7 reopen the
smallest affected global contract. No source repair/smoothing/saltation exception.

**Pros:** Bounds evidence cost and preserves the useful qualifications without

**Cons:** Leaves the root conditional contribution untested; neither a negative

**Dependencies/consequences:** Explicit deferral decision; no implementation or
