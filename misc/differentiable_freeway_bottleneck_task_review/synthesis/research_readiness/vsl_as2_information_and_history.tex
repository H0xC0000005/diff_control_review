\paragraph{Core finding.}
Identical E and combined continuing traffic can conceal different A/B
loads even from a complete predecision longitudinal record. A common
low-speed preparation can keep early merging donor-limited, so it leaves
no trace of unused adjacent receiving. An extra initial adjacent-load
statistic removes the ambiguity in this homogeneous family. The result
concerns information, not different best advice or useful gradients.

\section{Accepted world and the information being compared}
Use the user-accepted
\href{vsl_as1_asymmetric_forward_contract.pdf}{AS1 initial-family/forward contract}.
E ends, A is its adjacent continuing lane, B the far continuing lane.
At time zero the cohort is uniform on $[0,\ell]$, the terminal and recovery
account are empty, and there are no later external arrivals. Put
\begin{equation}
 r=e_0+a_0,\quad \rho=e_0+a_0+b_0,\quad M=\ell\rho,
 \qquad C(v)=\frac{wk_jv}{w+v},\quad \kc(v)=\frac{wk_j}{w+v}.
 \label{eq:world}
\end{equation}
Keep all AS1 bounds, laws, coefficients and choke-completion TST.
The future action is one common constant $v\in[v_-,v_+]$ selected at zero;
only information available before that action may choose it.

All predictors receive the same geometry, physical coefficients, action
set, initialization-family description, disclosed loading recipe and common
past advice. These do not include the realized split between A and B.
The nested information roles are:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{p{24mm}p{122mm}}
\toprule
Role & Available information at the decision\\
\midrule
Level 1 & Every admitted lane-blind predecision longitudinal density, flow,
speed and cumulative-count channel, with all lawful deterministic inference.\\[4pt]
Level 2 & Level 1 plus initial E mass and combined A+B mass. Any permitted
E-resolved history is retained; the construction below even matches that
stronger record. The realized A/B split is not supplied separately.\\[4pt]
Level 3 & The same common record plus individual initial E, A and B masses.
No future traffic, queue or recovery trace is supplied.\\[4pt]
Compact peer & Level 2 plus one initial adjacent-lane mass or an equivalent
scalar. It has the same initial information as Level 3 in this family.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
Initial-dependent $r$ is not a shared physical parameter: with known $e_0$,
it discloses $a_0$. Actual A/B boundary traces, adjacent receiving or a pair
label likewise cannot be passed as common inputs in this contrast. If an
actually admitted channel reveals the split, its inference must be allowed;
it defines an informative case rather than an exception to suppress.

\section{The existing loading recipe reaches unequal A/B states}
\subsection{A general sufficient construction}
Select two AS1-admitted initial states with common $e_0=e>0$ and
$a_{0,i}+b_{0,i}=c$, but $a_{0,1}\ne a_{0,2}$. Thus
$\rho=e+c$ is common and $r_i=e+a_{0,i}$ differs. Use the already disclosed
predecision H1 loading recipe, with common past speed $\bar v>0$,
$T=\ell/\bar v$, and an empty road before $-T$:
\begin{equation}
 e^{\rm in}(t)=e\exp(-\bar vt/h),\quad
 a_i^{\rm in}(t)=r_i-e^{\rm in}(t),\quad
 b_i^{\rm in}(t)=b_{0,i},\qquad -T\leq t\leq0.
 \label{eq:loader}
\end{equation}
All inputs cease at zero. These are exogenous preparation traces; they do
not depend on which future advice is tested. The recipe is the same as the
accepted historical construction, now evaluated on the independent initial
family. It adds neither a preparation mechanism nor a new controller.

Sufficient loading conditions are
\begin{equation}
 e\exp(\ell/h)<\min_i r_i,\qquad
 \max_i r_i+(\gamma-1)e\exp(\ell/h)<\kc(\bar v),\qquad
 b_{0,i}\leq\kc(\bar v),\quad \ell<L_p.
 \label{eq:loadbounds}
\end{equation}
For $\gamma\geq1$, these ensure positive A input and free lane densities.
The donor test for H1 is $a+\gamma e\leq\kc(\bar v)$.
It holds strictly because $a_i+\gamma e=r_i+(\gamma-1)e$.

The occupied front is $x_f(t)=\bar v(t+T)$. In
$0<x<x_f(t)$ the homogeneous fields are
\begin{equation}
 e(x,t)=e\exp(-\bar vt/h),\quad
 a_i(x,t)=r_i-e(x,t),\quad b_i(x,t)=b_{0,i}.
 \label{eq:fields}
\end{equation}
They satisfy the same transport and lateral balances:
\begin{equation}
 e_t+\bar ve_x=-\bar ve/h,\quad
 (a_i)_t+\bar v(a_i)_x=\bar ve/h,\quad
 (b_i)_t+\bar v(b_i)_x=0.
 \label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
For example a characteristic entering at $t-x/\bar v$ carries
$e^{\rm in}(t-x/\bar v)e^{-x/h}=e\exp(-\bar vt/h)$.
The front travels freely into an empty road. It never reaches $L_p$ before
zero, so the terminal/recovery history is empty. At zero the required
uniform independent-lane initial state is reached, with total mass $M$.

\subsection{A nonempty example inside the accepted AS1 family}
Reuse AS1's shared scales and coefficients:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 v_-=u,\ v_+=2u,\ w=2u,\ k_j=3\kappa,\ \gamma=2,\quad
 R=39\kappa u/20,\ R_B=\kappa u,\\
 L_p=2\ell,\ b=\ell,\ c_0=11/10,\ \beta=1/100,\quad
 e=3\kappa/5,\quad a_{0,i}+b_{0,i}=11\kappa/20,\\
 (a_{0,1},b_{0,1})=(\kappa/5,7\kappa/20),\quad
 (a_{0,2},b_{0,2})=(7\kappa/20,\kappa/5).
 \end{gathered} \label{eq:pair}
\end{equation}
Use $\bar v=u$ and any $0<\ell/h<\log(4/3)$.
Then $r_1=4\kappa/5$, $r_2=19\kappa/20$,
$e\exp(\ell/h)<4\kappa/5$ and
\begin{equation}
 r_2+e\exp(\ell/h)<(19/20+4/5)\kappa
 =7\kappa/4<\kc(u)=2\kappa.
 \label{eq:examplebounds}
\end{equation}
The entire continuous AS1 split interval between these endpoints satisfies
the same loading bounds. All postdecision AS1 admission margins remain
unchanged. This is a sufficient matched-history subfamily, not a restriction
of the whole admitted task to those histories.

For exact rational checks one may set $\ell/h=\log(6/5)$. Earliest E input
is then $18\kappa/25$, the smallest A input is $2\kappa/25>0$,
and the worst donor work density is $167\kappa/100<2\kappa$.
At the decision, future advice $2u$ has the same AS1 initial offer
$6\kappa u/5$ in E, but receiving alternatives $13\kappa u/10$ and
$23\kappa u/20$. Thus identical pasts can precede the already proved
distinct initial receiving response. This local calculation is not an
action comparison.

\section{The complete observation record is identical}
\begin{proposition}
The constructed pair has identical lane-blind longitudinal fields and
counts at every cross-section for every predecision time. It also has
identical E and combined A+B histories, even if these richer histories
are available. Nevertheless its initial A/B split is different.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $I(x,t)=\mathbf1_{\{0<x<x_f(t)\}}$ for $-T\leq t\leq0$.
The common aggregate fields and cumulative longitudinal count are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 k_\Sigma(x,t)=\rho I(x,t),\quad
 Q_\Sigma(x,t)=\bar v\rho I(x,t),\\
 N_\Sigma(x,t)=\rho\pos{x_f(t)-x}.
 \end{gathered} \label{eq:aggregate}
\end{equation}
Speed is $\bar v$ on the occupied region; empty-region masks are the same.
E fields are $k_E=e(t)I$, $Q_E=\bar ve(t)I$. For a reached cross-section,
its cumulative E count is
\begin{equation}
 N_E(x,t)=\bar v\int_{-T+x/\bar v}^t e(s)\dd s;
 \quad N_E=0\ \hbox{before its first arrival}.
 \label{eq:Ecount}
\end{equation}
This is the integral of the \emph{local} E flow; it is not a count of
conserved E identity through upstream transfers. Since $e(t)$ is common,
all E-resolved channels in this expression are common. Continuing fields
are $k_{A+B}=(\rho-e(t))I$, $Q_{A+B}=\bar vk_{A+B}$ and
$N_{A+B}=N_\Sigma-N_E$, likewise common.

The represented early E-to-A transfer density source $\bar ve(t)/h$ is
also common, so its cumulative transferred mass is not a distinguisher
for this pair. This does not claim equality of unmodeled microscopic gap
or individual maneuver observations. Before $-T$ both roads are empty;
through zero both terminal/recovery histories are empty. At zero,
$E_0=\ell e$ and $M-E_0=\ell c$ agree, while A/B differ. Any deterministic
filter or inference from the complete matched record has identical inputs.
The result therefore also holds for any finite sensor/history subset of
that record, without shortening the history window.
\end{proof}

The common predecision population-time is $MT/2$, independent of future
advice. Excluding it from the decision-time TST does not remove an
action-dependent or pair-dependent term. Nothing from the post-action
trajectory has been included in the decision input.

\paragraph{What follows.}
Level 2 cannot identify the correct initial A/B split for every member of
this admitted pair. It may retain uncertainty or use a disclosed prior;
it is not required to assume equal A/B loads. No deterministic point
estimate can be correct in both states from identical inputs. This does
not imply different optimal actions: a common action could be optimal in
both, and AS3 must check that separately. The construction establishes
an exact conditional ambiguity, not universal nonobservability in traffic.

\section{The compact peer closes the initial-information gap}
Let $E_0,A_0,B_0$ denote initial masses, so $M=E_0+A_0+B_0$.
Given Level 2's common $M,\ell,E_0$ and one adjacent mass $A_0$,
\begin{equation}
 e_0=E_0/\ell,\qquad a_0=A_0/\ell,\qquad
 b_0=(M-E_0-A_0)/\ell.
 \label{eq:compact}
\end{equation}
An equivalent scalar such as $r$ also suffices. The AS1 forward law is
unique with neutral initial terminal/recovery history. The compact peer
and explicit per-lane initialization therefore predict the identical
complete trajectory and TST for every admitted advice. Their objective
derivatives are equal wherever those derivatives exist; this identity is
not a numerical-gradient validation or a new regularity theorem.

Predicted queues, speed marks and retained recovery histories still evolve.
Initial scalar sufficiency does not reduce that dynamic state to a single
meter reading. Nor does it establish that full per-lane spatial detail,
integer mass, identities or discrete order are necessary for this task.
Equal-information representation/optimizer comparisons remain separate
from the Level 2-to-Level 3 information comparison.

\section{When history does identify A, and the boundary of this result}
If an admitted record supplies exact E density and its time derivative
while early transfer is \emph{strictly receiving-limited} at known common
past speed $\bar v$, then
\begin{equation}
 \dot e=-\frac{C(\bar v)-\bar va}{\gamma h},\qquad
 a=\kc(\bar v)+\frac{\gamma h}{\bar v}\dot e,
 \quad r=e+a.
 \label{eq:infer}
\end{equation}
The body must be present and this is a density derivative, not the
derivative of whole-lane E mass during changing footprint. With the retained
free preparation law, $r$ is constant; its earlier inferred value gives
current $a_0=r-e_0$, then $b_0=\rho-r$. Such permitted inference is fully
available to Level 2.

Strict receiving activity is detectable from a transfer rate below the
donor offer, $0<-h\dot e<\bar ve$. On a donor-only history,
\begin{equation}
 \dot e=-\bar ve/h,\qquad
 r+(\gamma-1)e\leq\kc(\bar v),
 \label{eq:donorbound}
\end{equation}
which bounds $r$ but does not generally determine it. Both constructed
members satisfy the bound strictly. At donor/receiver equality the
derivative alone does not certify unique receiving activity. With only
initial E and no E history, \eqref{eq:infer} is not an available measurement;
the matched-history proof deliberately grants more, rather than relying on
that omission. Exact noiseless identification is not a claim of robust
estimation from noisy detector data.

Direct A/B counts, a known realized $r$, an identifying adjacent-receiving
measurement or other informative admitted data must also be honored. The
pair certificate applies only to the stated matched channels. There is no
new observation restriction: the positive result already matches the
existing complete lane-blind envelope and a richer E-resolved envelope.
