## Question, traffic example and paper role

Consider the same total traffic arriving at a permanent three-to-two lane drop.
In one preparation, little mass remains in the ending lane; in another, much
more must still merge. A common lower speed delays everybody's arrival but may
allow more merging before the narrow exit. The useful choice depends on whether
that reduction in unfinished merge work pays for the added travel time.

The task asks what information is sufficient to predict this balance and its
local speed sensitivity. It provides an elementary verification of the
information-to-control chain before the urban order/access study. It does not
claim that VSL needs discrete identity, FIFO order or full lane resolution.

The primary contribution target is useful control sensitivity: does the
retained information support a common-speed perturbation that improves complete
traffic time? Novelty of the precise verification is secondary. Novelty of the
VSL task, gradient optimization in general or the modeling stack is not claimed.
An information advantage over an aggregate peer and an optimizer advantage are
different findings; independent usefulness does not require gradients to beat
every search method.

## Physical setting and intervention

Start with S13's finite cohort, fixed preparation-zone length, explicit ending
lane and symmetric continuing-lane group. One constant common bounded speed
advice is the only decision. All lane geometry, traffic preparation, routes and
parameters remain fixed during differentiation. The analytical branch assumes
that advice sets the common realized longitudinal speed, with nonbinding
receiving during preparation. That assumption is explicitly conditional;
arbitrary compliance or congested speed response cannot use the same mapping
without a new qualification.

## Conservative state and causal dynamics

Continuous vehicle-equivalent mass transfers from the ending group to the
continuing group in equal amounts. In the inherited exponential member, longer
residence leaves less mandatory-merge mass at the choke. Each unit still needing
to merge consumes more receiving work than ordinary continuing traffic. Finite
receiving and homogeneous proportional batch allocation determine departures
and final clearance. The extra factor counts receiving work, not new vehicles.

Conservative lane transfer and finite receiving have literature foundations.
The constant transfer rate, synchronized cohort handoff and homogeneous
proportional clearance are S13's bounded analytical adaptations. Their traffic
validity and robustness must be audited before promoting an applied claim.

The transfer rate is per elapsed time; a source's distance-based desire to
change lane does not alone justify that response. Early receiving is assumed
nonbinding, so additional interaction delay from preparatory merging is not
modeled. There is no spatial density/storage/spillback update in this reduced
kernel. These limitations require regime or reduction evidence, not automatic
addition of a larger traffic model.
The shared continuing-group receiving assumption also needs justification:
the ending lane directly accesses its adjacent continuing lane, while common
speed alone does not ensure lane balance.

The primary-source recheck shows why the transfer clock is consequential:
Subraveti's lateral demand is a fraction of sending demand, whose free-flow
branch contains speed. A speed-scaled rate can cancel the longer traversal
time in a declared analytical diagnostic. This is not a prediction of the
whole native model or a selected new closure. An applied task needs an explicit
action-to-completed-transfer contract before its methods can be frozen.

## Objective and gradient meaning

Count all affected traffic time from the preparation entrance until every unit
exits, including action-induced travel and service waiting. Report total system
time in vehicle-equivalent seconds; mean cohort time is that total divided by
fixed cohort mass. No early horizon may reward uncompleted traffic. Any admitted
upstream waiting or downstream action effect must enter the same accounting.

The physical derivative describes the change in complete time caused by a
small feasible change in common speed, in metres per second. Traversal time is
an equivalent internal coordinate, so its derivative must be mapped back to
speed before comparisons. Fixed lane labels and routes are not differentiated.

## Information ladder and simpler rivals

Compare a full-history aggregate observer, the same observer plus initial
ending-lane mass, and the ending/continuing grouped model. Each predicts future
remaining mass independently for each action; no post-action reference state is
supplied. The scalar and grouped representations must agree under S13. The
aggregate-only contrast tests additional information; the scalar/group contrast
tests compression at equal information.

Screen established aggregate VSL/capacity-drop and lane-aware transfer models
against the exact claim. Separately compare analytical or bounded
one-dimensional search with gradient-based optimization if optimizer performance
is later claimed. A scalar action makes simple search particularly competitive.

Compare finite-difference gradient estimates as gradient methods where relevant;
direct search is also a serious optimizer rival. At equal information, the
compact scalar is a lawful sufficient augmentation in S13, not a baseline to
exclude as an engineering patch. Claims about noise, instability or local-optimum
failures need separate evidence; none has been established here.

## Verification, limits and connection to urban control

Verify conservation, receiving constraints, full-time accounting and compact
equivalence before derivative checks. Check analytical/AD/finite-difference
agreement in stable regimes, with feasible one-sided checks at bounds. Include
mechanism deletions and the smallest justified transfer/service alternatives.
Independent evaluation of identical perturbations is required for an external
usefulness claim; a richer evaluator remains unselected.

The common paper story is minimum information needed for a useful control
sensitivity. Urban retains its approved effective-service allocation, V0–V4
variants and discrete order/access question. VSL may conclude that one
continuous statistic suffices. Neither analytical openness nor internal loss
reduction proves calibrated traffic validity, empirical prevalence, gradient
transfer or novelty over the strongest simpler models.
