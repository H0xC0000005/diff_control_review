## Main finding

The study makes progress on **how lane information can be hidden**, but does not
yet establish **when that information changes useful common speed advice**.

Two conditional analytical results clarify the gap:

1. In a homogeneous free-flow branch, conservative lane transfers cancel from
   total mass. Different hidden lane-entry allocations can produce the same
   complete spatial aggregate/mean-speed/count history. A short, known-empty
   preparation can retain different ending-lane mass without deleting history
   or turning lane changing off. This is a continuous traffic-model subcase,
   **not** certification of the native reference or the near-drop preparation.
2. Attaching different fixed bottleneck capacities to those hidden states is
   insufficient. If higher advice only makes arrivals earlier and advice cannot
   change work-conserving service, the highest feasible advice is a common best
   choice—including source/approach waiting. Merely moving delay upstream cannot
   create useful slowing. This is a restricted-class obstruction, not a general
   claim that speed advice cannot help.

The missing link is therefore more specific now: **shared desired-speed advice
must change endogenous merging/receiving/service, or invalidate the simple
arrival-dominance premise, enough to outweigh its delay—and this must depend on
information not recoverable from the declared history.** Finite merge opportunity,
cooperative gap interactions and spillback are plausible mechanisms, not adopted
equations or proven benefits. Capacity drop is one possibility, not a universal
requirement. The reviewed lateral-control gains cannot be transferred directly
to this different actuator.

Read the [mathematical analysis PDF](../../synthesis/s05/rb_certificate_analysis.pdf)
for assumptions, constructions and proofs; its [LaTeX source](../../synthesis/s05/rb_certificate_analysis.tex)
is retained. The [source audit](../../extraction/rb_certificate_source_audit.md)

## What remains unproved

No complete source-grounded pair has been established with both the consequential
near-drop hidden contrast and different useful common advice. Nor has the study
proved that such a pair is impossible. Specifically:

- The homogeneous free-flow invariance need not survive lane-dependent actual
  speed, receiving constraints or native following/merging. Full history may then
  distinguish preparations. No matching failure was measured.
- The early contrast is reachable conditionally, but persistence to the last400m
  and a useful postdecision split remain separate obligations.
- A shared-advice response/merge closure cannot be inferred from an optimized
  lateral actuator or invented lane-dependent penalty. Published hard branches
  and unverified transfer budgets have not been repaired/adopted.
- Ending-lane spatial mass and continuing-lane imbalance remain candidate
  statistics, not sufficient/minimum states. Classes/order and all gradient
  claims remain deferred.
