# P-H selected: slow entries retain a service effect after completion

**Approved direction; CP-D1 closed, 2026-10-15.** The user agreed that the
point model must retain the slow-entry/recovery effect and instructed the
review to proceed with P-H. The primary qualification therefore follows
causal continuous service history. P-E remains a strong simpler capacity-drop
rival; it is no longer an undecided primary choice at CP-D1.

An ending-lane entry can finish crossing the measuring line while still
restricting service for traffic behind it. P-H must retain that influence
without counting the completed entry as another waiting vehicle. The terminal
still contains the physical approach queues; the point still determines
compulsory merging and local completion.

## What this approval fixes

| Decision | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **CP-D1 / G3** | Qualify a causal, continuous slow-entry/recovery service law driven by actual predicted E admissions. A mean capacity penalty alone is insufficient as the primary explanation. |
| **S3-M2** | Coupled point service and completion at the choke remain closed. Recovery influence may outlast completion; post-line vehicle-time stays outside the local objective. |
| **Terminal behaviour** | This remains compulsory merging at the point. It does not open last-minute lateral gap use inside the terminal. H1's earlier merging and its cutoff remain separate. |
| **Information and evidence** | Known empty initial service history; no future receiving oracle. Preserve aggregate/history, ending-scalar, search and capacity-drop rivals and the model → SUMO S-P → SUMO S-L ladder. |

The approval selects the mechanism and authorises its bounded qualification.
It does not approve an unspecified entry-speed mapping, recovery kernel,
overlap rule or numerical parameter. Those must be exposed in a concrete
candidate before freeze. The predecessor H1/T1 mathematical artifacts remain
unchanged; this is its narrowly reopened terminal successor.
