## What this means in traffic terms

Imagine the first E group reaches the lane end alongside A. In the current
smooth fluid loading, their combined arrival rate is below the undisturbed
shared service ceiling. Giving A priority still leaves enough service for
all of E. With the old instantaneous extra E work charge removed, that
ordinary allocation alone makes neither stream wait.

If we now say “E slows because it is queued,” we must first explain what
made E queue. If we say “the meter falls because E crosses slowly,” we must
explain what made the first crossing slow. Each statement describes a valid
possible feedback after activation; neither supplies the missing beginning.

The previous M1 reference supplied that beginning by assumption: every E
admission left a prescribed slow-entry response. M2 asks the update to depend
on the traffic actually discharged. It therefore cannot quietly inherit
M1's low representative insertion speed as if the terminal had predicted it.

There are two physical ways forward. A compulsory maneuver can itself
require yielding or speed adjustment even when average ordinary demand fits.
Alternatively, an ordinary bottleneck can first build a terminal queue, whose
slow discharge then supplies the recovery mechanism's input. Both can feed
the same shared meter, but they make different claims about why delay occurs.

## What the sources resolve

The 2011 Leclercq–Laval–Chiabaut conference model distinguishes congested and
uncongested entering traffic while keeping the main road sufficiently loaded
to encounter congestion. Its uncongested-entry case adopts the main-road
speed; it is not a derivation of slowing when both approaches are free.

[Primary conference text, §2 and §2.2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251530773_Capacity_Drops_at_Merges_an_endogenous_model)

The 2016 manuscript studies congested approaches and relates insertion
speed to entering flow through that traffic regime. The congested regime
is part of the interpretation; a low flow number by itself is insufficient
to infer a slow vehicle.

[Primary manuscript, introduction and §3.2](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_c424c1b3-7374-436b-a6a7-abdf69063aa6)

With the triangular fundamental diagram, the same subcapacity flow can
correspond to a free-flow state or a congested state with a lower speed.
Using the congested speed formula for every small discharge would mistake
light fast traffic for slow dense traffic. The LTM boundary histories and
traffic regime must be interpreted together; an equivalent speed obtained
from the meter ceiling is not automatically actual crossing speed.

[Primary LTM fundamental diagram and boundary demand, §2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.7080)

These sources support qualifying a congestion/recovery law. They do not
complete our finite transient departure process, establish a separate A
startup penalty, or select the first-disturbance mechanism for this task.

## The new choice: what first makes terminal passage slow?

**Historical candidate comparison; no longer an open either-or gate.** F/D
describe compatible behavior contexts. Their old recommendation and demand-
selection inference below are not the current review plan.

**Shared problem and model role.** The meter must receive a physically
interpreted disturbance, then carry its continuing effect. The unresolved
choice concerns component 6's entry/activation rule and its admissible demand
domain. Both candidates retain the same shared account, point location,
physical queues, local endpoint and continuous mass.

### F: the compulsory maneuver can create slowing before ordinary overload

Keep the inherited condition that the whole ordinary A/E stream fits within
the undisturbed shared budget. Qualify a point-entry law for the yielding,
alignment or speed adjustment needed to complete a compulsory merge. That
law supplies the initial speed/service effect; the shared account carries
the subsequent recovery restriction.

In the example, ordinary throughput could accommodate both approaching
streams, but an actual merge may still require a traffic-dependent adjustment.
The model must explain that adjustment. It must not simply assign a fixed
low speed to every E entry and call it predicted kinematics.

- **Pros:** preserves the clean interpretation that extra terminal delay
  originates in compulsory merging rather than ordinary aggregate overload.
  It changes one service component and need not introduce spatial cells.
- **Cons:** the inspected acceleration/capacity sources do not already give
  that free-approach entry law. A compact yielding/maneuver closure and its
  parameter meaning require further support. A capacity-recovery rule alone
  does not supply it.
- **Dependency/consequence:** Type 2 G3 entry semantics. The old demand domain
  stays fixed, but a new maneuver-origin restriction must be qualified. If
  immediate maneuver work and later recovery are both used, their distinct
  physical roles must prevent duplicate charging.

### D: ordinary terminal congestion creates the slow discharge

Permit a finite arrival pulse to exceed the ordinary shared budget. That
queue produces a genuinely congested departure state. Qualify how its
boundary kinematics and merging then change the shared meter during recovery.
Retain empty initial state: the queue is generated by this cohort, not inserted
as a hidden initial disturbance.

In the example, E/A arrivals first exceed what the point can pass. A terminal
queue forms through ordinary conservation. Slow entering/discharging traffic
then becomes an input to the additional merging/recovery effect. The task
would explicitly contain both ordinary congestion and the extra interaction.

- **Pros:** supplies a causal source of slow traffic and is closer to the
  congested-regime speed relations actually inspected. A compatible H1/storage
  domain exists without extra spatial state or initial information.
- **Cons:** relaxes the accepted no-ordinary-overload condition. Total delay
  and VSL benefit can then arise partly from an aggregate bottleneck, so
  strong aggregate baselines and attribution become particularly important.
  Overload alone establishes neither ending-information value nor a gradient
  advantage.
  The ordinary service ceiling also needs independent physical provenance;
  lowering it merely to force a queue would not qualify the task.
  Source regime compatibility still does not establish a complete finite
  M2 update or its transient accuracy.
- **Dependency/consequence:** explicitly reopen the narrow G3 demand-domain
  restriction before using this route. Preserve H1 only within the checked
  compatible domain; no upstream spillback coupling is silently added.

**Withdrawn recommendation (historical): D for the first source-based M2 qualification,
if ordinary congestion is accepted as part of the elementary task.** It
requires fewer unsupported assumptions about why an initially free E stream
must slow. Its demand-domain change is scientifically meaningful and needs
approval; it is not adopted here. If isolating extra delay under ordinary
underload remains essential, F is the consistent route. Neither recommendation
depends on obtaining an interior optimum or beating the aggregate rival.

Neither route is selected. M2's shared-account architecture stays approved.
