## What remains essential, and what can change

The anchor is the scientific question: whether omitted lane-loading
information matters to traffic service and useful VSL decisions, and whether
the resulting sensitivity supports decisions under a fair comparison. The
selected A/B information direction remains in scope. Changing that question
would need an explicit discussion; changing the traffic equations used to
study it is an expected possibility in this review.

The new task may use different traffic states, spatial resolution, lane
redistribution, merging rules or a different representation of capacity
drop. It need not reconstruct our old homogeneous preparation, forced-point
service or recovery meter component by component. Action, observation,
scenario and population-time accounting must be stated coherently for the
candidate; they need not copy every old convention. Old analytical results
would require re-establishment under the new laws.

What does carry over is the need for a complete and physically admissible
forward model, an explicit task and fair information/optimizer comparisons.
Similarity to the old model helps interpret the change, but does not excuse
an invalid update. Conversely, a valid candidate is not disqualified merely
because it replaces an old physical mechanism. Several citations can describe
one established modeling family or standard mathematical machinery; the
important burden is the number and significance of new assumptions and joins.

## Why trying allocation first is reasonable

Lane-change intention and realized movement perform different jobs. The
intention model says where traffic wants to go. The allocator decides which
requests can actually occur together given available traffic and space. If
ten units of traffic generate seven units of desire toward each neighbor,
the issue is sharing the ten consistently. That can be a bounded component
problem, rather than a reason to redesign the whole traffic model.

The next substantive review should therefore first seek an authoritative
source clarification or a small, established allocation component. Check
whether it jointly respects donor traffic, target receiving and the forward
movement left after lateral transfers, and whether its assumptions fit this
backbone. It may also expose an opportunity to simplify or replace another
source rule. Such a change should be compared through its traffic meaning
and explicit delta, not rejected for failing to reproduce the old stack.

Then judge the resulting whole candidate: does it answer the same RQs with
a complete model and a manageable set of assumptions? Backtrack to another
backbone if the allocator route needs several bespoke coupled changes,
conflicts with the intended information/control question, or is clearly less
credible than a source-native alternative. The review has not yet demonstrated
any of those outcomes.
