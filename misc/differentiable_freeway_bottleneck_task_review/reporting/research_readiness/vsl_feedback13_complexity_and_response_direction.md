## The concern is real, and your proposed response needs one correction

Your interpretation is sound: the traffic-model construction may now demand
more domain understanding and justification than is suitable for this
elementary CS-method verification. Philipp is questioning the scientific
burden carried by the benchmark: readers must decide whether its traffic
assumptions are credible before they can assess the information/gradient
finding, and may struggle to tell which conclusion survives a different
reasonable model.

Clearer prose and equation-level attribution help, but do not remove that
burden. The present model contains selected homogeneous-transfer, boundary-
speed, service-sharing and recovery-memory closures whose full composition
is not supplied or physically validated by a single source. Our earlier
review explicitly records this limit.

**We do not need to prove that this is the best possible compromise, or
that existing traffic models cannot answer the research questions.** The
relevant goal is to find a credible, sufficiently simple traffic setting
for answering those questions. An existing model may already provide that
setting even if its authors did not ask the same CS question. Its success
as a traffic model is a reason to consider using it.

Your closest-work summary is therefore useful as an account of what we
found and why particular ingredients were considered. It would not establish
that the final combination is necessary. Differences in task, observation,
objective or derivative evidence must not be turned into claims that the
source model is unsuitable as a benchmark.

## Brief direction for responding

The response should convey four points, without defending every earlier
modeling decision:

1. **Accept the scientific complexity concern.** The current formulation is
   conditionally specified and analyzed, but that does not establish it as
   the simplest or best-supported benchmark for the CS purpose.
2. **Summarize what the closest models already cover.** Aggregate VSL,
   lane-aware merging and traffic-control gradients each have strong
   precedents. Explain the narrow information/comparison gap without
   portraying those precedents as inadequate traffic models.
3. **Acknowledge the unresolved selection question.** We have not established
   whether one published model, retained substantially in its native form,
   can support the essential research questions with minimal adaptation.
   The review's component-by-component justification does not answer this.
4. **Propose a bounded model-reuse assessment.** Compare the strongest native
   aggregate-VSL and lane-resolved anchors already identified. Start with
   their existing traffic rules; ask what can be achieved by scenario,
   observation and comparison design, then identify the smallest genuinely
   necessary model change. Assess what research question each candidate
   can honestly answer and where a simpler information/control outcome
   would be scientifically sufficient.

The comparison should count **new constitutive assumptions and coupled
validation obligations**, not papers cited or state variables alone. Several
references may credit standard ingredients, so seven references do not mean
seven separate models. In the current report, Wei is comparison context
and Kong supplies event-sensitivity calculus; neither is another imported
traffic-model component. Nevertheless the study-defined joined rules create
a real burden. A published multilane model with more spatial cells could
be easier to justify than a smaller bespoke model, because its traffic law
can remain familiar and externally supported. That tradeoff has not yet
been settled here.

In particular, the record does not prove that retaining homogeneous
preparation and adding point-recovery detail is essential to the research
questions. Those earlier design preferences can be revisited through an
explicit methodological decision if a simpler research story warrants it.
Do not assume every physically plausible traffic effect must be represented
in the elementary verification task, or require a positive information gap
or interior VSL optimum as a model-selection condition.

The direction is therefore to test **one established model plus the smallest
justified change**, with an unchanged model remaining a valid outcome.
This is a proposed next question, not a claim that a particular one- or
two-source solution has already been found. The current formulation remains
an archived conditional reference while that possibility is assessed.

## What more complete supporting material would require

The existing records are sufficient to explain the concern, summarize the
closest work and state the unresolved native-model question. They can be
assembled into a fuller discussion note without another scientific review.

**A substantive answer to whether a simpler established-model route works
requires a separate bounded follow-up review in this VSL package.** Its
purpose is to assess whole-model reuse, an issue not settled by the earlier
component qualification. It would reuse the collected literature and ask:

1. Which of the strongest already identified native models can support the
   essential information/control/gradient questions, and under what task,
   action and observation contract?
2. Which published model can serve as the main forward-model backbone,
   with its state, equations and traffic meaning retained substantially?
   Why is that a defensible starting point for the research questions?
3. What can scenario and comparison design achieve with that model intact?
   What exact adaptation or component adoption, if any, is genuinely
   necessary, and can it come from a very limited set of additional works?
   Check compatibility and all new assumptions, not just citation count.
4. What assumptions, sensitivity limitations and independent validation
   burden remain, and what claims could the resulting task honestly make?

The output should be a short source-grounded comparison and recommendation,
with an explicit unchanged-source/minimal-change map and the unresolved
choices. An unchanged model, one small extension, or a reason to reconsider
the task/claim are all admissible outcomes. It should not reconstruct another
composite model in order to guarantee a positive information advantage.

This review would establish a defensible methodological route and its
limits. Numerical gradient usefulness and empirical traffic validity remain
later evidence. A short scope/shortlist/closure plan is the next preparation;
execution would stop at the planned gate or a consequential methodological
choice. The user's acceptance of the response ladder is recorded separately
from authorization to execute that review.
