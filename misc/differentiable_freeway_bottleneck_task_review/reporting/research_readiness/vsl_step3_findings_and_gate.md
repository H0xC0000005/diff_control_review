## What identical history means here

Imagine loading the preparation road before the decision. Both preparations
receive the same total entering traffic at the same common speed. Their
lane-specific inputs differ, but remain physically feasible. The loading
recipe accounts for early transfers so that both reach the required uniform
footprint and equal continuing-lane densities at the decision instant.
The terminal is still empty.

Throughout that loading history, total density, longitudinal flow, traffic
speed and cumulative counts agree at every cross-section. The construction
therefore does not make the aggregate peer forget earlier data or replace
it with a snapshot. A common deterministic estimator supplied with all those
records still receives identical inputs. It may use a prior or express
uncertainty, but the record alone cannot identify which ending allocation
is actually present.

This is a constructed, source-compatible initial reachability reference
under H1's declared law. It is not a claim that arbitrary traffic histories,
field sensors or another simulator have the same ambiguity. If an actual
observer also reports lane counts or lateral events, give those channels
and their lawful inference to the aggregate peer.

The added information is small: **current initial ending mass**. Under the
accepted continuing symmetry, it reconstructs the adjacent and other
continuing lanes. It then predicts early transfers, terminal arrivals and
waiting itself. No future receiving or post-action merge outcome is provided.
An explicit lane representation has no extra predictive power over that
scalar augmentation in this member.

## Why different merging is not automatically a different decision

In the lower-ending preparation, every early request fits the declared
receiving over the whole advice range. Its completed preparation per unit
distance is therefore unchanged by speed. Earlier travel remains beneficial,
including when a terminal queue forms, and fastest advice is best.

In the higher-ending preparation, faster advice can leave less normalized
receiving headroom. Some early requests are constrained, so more traffic
reaches the terminal still needing compulsory merging. This preparation
change is small in the reference construction; it does not automatically
outweigh faster travel.

The analytical check follows the entire cohort through the terminal tail.
When late merging uses enough extra receiving work, the small additional
unfinished mass produces a large additional time cost. Then decreasing
fastest advice helps the higher-ending preparation, while it harms the
lower-ending preparation. The shared aggregate history cannot select both
correct actions; the ending scalar can.

The qualification is essential. The proof varies a parameter already present
in the closed law and derives a sufficient range; it does not select a
numerical work factor, run an optimizer or claim that real lane drops occupy
that range. A separate sensitivity bound proves the opposite outcome for
late-work factors one through two in the history-matched reference family:
the saved waiting cannot repay everyone's longer travel, so both preparations
prefer fastest advice. The wider Step 2 compatibility family is not fully
classified. Common-action and negative-information regimes remain part of
the protocol.

## What the full gradient must include

The speed effect runs through both routes to complete time. It changes
early spare service and the class that reaches the lane end; it also changes
when every unit arrives and how the ordinary stream competes with unfinished
traffic. The final derivative must include full road travel and the complete
queue-clearance tail.

Freezing arrival times would erase a real part of that effect. Checking only
the final work or only peak waiting would change the objective. The supporting
note supplies exact complete-time references and a general sensitivity path
through physical initial positions and queue reflection.

The accepted member has a small event structure: an early transfer branch
may switch, arrivals have moving first/last times, and a forced queue may
form and empty. Its eligible weighted arrival rate decreases during the
pulse, so there is at most one positive ending-queue episode. The nominal
upstream receiving and terminal sending restrictions stay slack; leaving
that domain is an admission failure.

Later numerical verification must distinguish continuous-reference accuracy
from AD agreeing with its own discrete forward calculation. It needs forward
conservation/clearance first, then stable-event gradient checks and feasible
one-sided checks at advice bounds. No smoothing, frozen timing or removal
of valid forward events is approved. No AD or finite-difference traffic
calculation was run in this review.

## The independent-reference choice now matters

**Historical selection gate.** The subsequent user decision selects R-M/SUMO.
The R-C alternative and original recommendation below are preserved as the
decision record. The current open choice concerns terminal correspondence,
as explained in the linked continuation above.

**Problem statement.** Which traffic description should first judge the
predicted speed advice: actual individual gap/motion interactions under
a speed limit, or independently specified continuous lane-density and
spatial-service dynamics?

**Assumed model-chain function.** H1/T1 predicts a feasible common advice
perturbation from available initial information. The independent reference
receives that same physical intervention and reports complete cohort time.
It tests whether the predicted gain survives different traffic interactions.
It stays outside the predictor and supplies no future service oracle.

**Core gap.** The available sources do not jointly map the present initial
continuous footprint, pre-decision observations, ideal response, terminal
handoff/priority and work factors into either reference. Software capability
does not establish behavioral fitness. Selecting the reference determines
what independent usefulness will mean and which G2/G3 mappings must qualify.

The shared example is the two preparations above: equal total traffic and
aggregate history, different ending-lane occupancy. The question is whether
an advised slowdown saves enough compulsory delay to repay its longer travel.
The reference should answer that from its own traffic behavior.

### R-M — qualify a microscopic operational speed-limit reference

Use SUMO as the concrete candidate for this qualification direction.
Common advice changes the permitted speed on the declared lanes; native
vehicle motion, gaps and mandatory interactions determine realized travel
and departures. H1 remains the continuous predictor. The reference would
test its speed/preparation/service hypotheses against richer physical
interaction rather than impose the predicted trajectories.

The official documentation establishes speed-sign and native interaction
capabilities, with output/state tools. It also exposes why the bridge is
scientific work: continuous mass needs a valid vehicle realization; native
lane dynamics and acceleration affect history and response; the terminal
handoff and priority need an actual physical/network meaning.

**Follow-up clarification: prescribed intention is technically possible.**
SUMO can anticipate an ending lane natively. A Python/TraCI script can also
select a fixed share of drivers to request an early merge, while retaining
native safety-gap constraints. Such a share specifies who tries; it does
not fix who succeeds or their delay. The request schedule, competing native
motivations and terminal handoff still need a disclosed mapping. Generating
requests externally can preserve the independent service test; prescribing
completed transfers using H1's receiving law would not provide that test.
No exact request policy or reference admission is selected by this capability
check, and the approved primary law is unchanged.

[Verified early-merging and intention capabilities](../../extraction/research_readiness/vsl_step3_reference_and_evidence_audit.md#follow-up-capability-check-early-merging-and-prescribed-intention)

**Pros.** Directly tests operational advice against different physical
interaction, supports a strong reference-relative usefulness claim after
admission, and fits the paper's separation of prediction, checked gradient
and richer traffic evaluation.

**Cons.** Does not automatically reproduce the homogeneous footprint or
exact observer collision. Native histories, initialization, discrete
population realization, terminal permissions and complete-time accounting
require qualification. The native behavior does not calibrate a constant
late-work factor by inspection, and it may eliminate the analytical advantage.

**Dependencies and consequence.** G4 external-evidence meaning, with G2
observations/initialization and G3 intervention/task mapping. Qualify the
bridge before installation or traffic runs. Do not force realized speeds
or silently round the primary model's traffic to obtain agreement.

### R-C — qualify a continuous spatial lane-aware reference

Use the previously reviewed Nagalur Subraveti/Knoop/van Arem model as the
principal candidate. Its independent traffic description retains lane
density at successive road positions, constrained lateral transfers and
longitudinal propagation. Common advice would need a documented mapping
to its constitutive speed/flow behavior. These spatial states belong to the
reference; H1's primary homogeneous prediction remains unchanged.

Its continuous quantity makes the initial mass/profile bridge easier to
inspect. However, the source's fixed-diagram traffic estimation and field
lane-density tests do not supply the present common-VSL response or compulsory
point/handoff contract. An added advice-response rule would need its own
qualification; copying H1's rule would weaken the independence of that test.

**Pros.** Preserves continuous mass in the reference, offers an explicit
lane-density/service explanation and a source with lane-drop data tests,
and makes budget, profile and propagation differences inspectable without
individual gap realization.

**Cons.** Its response, terminal interaction and source budget branches need
qualification. Existing lane-density validation is not control-sensitivity
validation. Shared first-order/receiving ingredients make it especially
important to distinguish an independent behavior test from a reimplementation.

**Dependencies and consequence.** The same G4/G2/G3 evidence and mapping
contracts. Spatial reference state does not select the previously unchosen
spatial primary model or reopen H1 by itself. Do not repair native equations,
add speed kinetics or import reference state into the predictor silently.

[Reviewed primary continuous lane model](../../archive/full_text/FB0030.pdf)

**Provisional recommendation: R-M qualification first.** The accepted priority
is useful operational speed guidance, so a reference with its own physical
speed and maneuver response gives the clearer independent test. This is a
recommendation to qualify one bounded reference bridge, not an admission of
SUMO, a claim that it will preserve the positive result, or permission to
develop/run it. R-C is a serious alternative if continuous population and
inspectable spatial service are the preferred first evidence boundary.
