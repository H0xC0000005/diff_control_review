The big picture is now connected. **The same ending-lane traffic can need
different advice depending on how crowded its adjacent receiving lane is.**
The accepted model can represent that difference, an admitted predecision
history can conceal it, and a conditional complete-TST calculation shows
that it can change which finite advice adjustment helps. The gradient
reference follows the same causal chain through full clearance.

This is a qualified standalone task definition and analytical verification
framework. It does not establish a calibrated freeway result, measured
training difficulty, numerical gradient accuracy or an optimizer advantage.
The strong compact rival remains exactly sufficient once it receives the
same initial adjacent information as the per-lane predictor.

## Why the A/B split can change useful advice

Picture the accepted pair: E has 0.60 density units and A+B has 0.55 in
both. A/B is 0.20/0.35 in one preparation and 0.35/0.20 in the other.
Their complete admitted histories, including E's past, can agree under
AS2's common loading recipe. At the decision, however, E faces a busier
neighbor in the second preparation.

In the less crowded-A preparation, every early E offer fits at every advice
in the analytical example. Changing speed therefore leaves its residual E
profile unchanged at the same traveled distance. In the busier-A preparation,
the highest advice initially limits early merging by receiving. Lowering
advice can improve receiving headroom and reduce the mass left for forced
merging. This is the selected H1 headroom mechanism; extra elapsed time
alone does not generate extra attempts.

That reduction competes with the extra travel time from slowing everyone.
We checked the competition using complete TST, including all three lanes'
travel and the full waiting tail. First an exact fixed-work reference proves
the contrast. Then a uniform service/queue bound shows that the contrast
persists in a nonempty range of the selected W-F service and R-E/O-R recovery
law, with positive mismatch and finite positive recovery acceleration.

**In that conditional range, the same small feasible slowdown worsens the
less crowded-A case and improves the busier-A case.** A Level 2 controller
receiving identical histories must choose the same advice for both, so at
least one case has a positive gap from its own best achievable cost. This
statement uses infima and does not assume that every selected-law objective
is smooth or attains a minimum.

The qualification is substantial. The existence proof can require a large
forced-work factor, weak mismatch and fast recovery. It is not a positive
result for AS2's illustrative work coefficients, a recommended calibration,
or an independently supported physical parameter range. A stays free in
this proof; the earlier A-delay/active-renewal reference is a separate case.
No joint strong-delay/action/physical witness is asserted.

## What the gradient must carry

Advice affects more than the current merge rate. It changes preparation at
fixed progress, when every initial position becomes eligible for service,
which queues form or clear, and the speed marks deposited into recovery.
The recovery account also changes as its old mass is retired. All those
effects reach the travel-time objective.

The regular sensitivity reference now explicitly allows different fixed
initial A and B loads, including B's own moving travel clock. It retains
the current-state service roots, fixed physical mismatch coefficient and
history-aware event timing. Queue endpoints have zero queue-area value,
but their timing can still change later service and recovery. Freezing them
would compute a different derivative.

The reference is local to stable smooth event sequences with regular
cutoffs and isolated transverse queue events. Forward-admitted thresholds,
grazing or simultaneous events, kinks and cutoff encounters remain in the
task. A derivative that does not exist must be reported as such, alongside
the actual feasible cost change. No smoothing, altered queue law or deleted
boundary case is introduced to make differentiation easier.

The action proof controls complete **values and finite changes**. It does
not prove opposite infinitesimal gradients in the selected law. Later work
must separately verify continuous-reference correctness, numerical forward
convergence, finite differences, AD and useful feasible steps. Strong bounded
scalar search remains a serious reference for this one-dimensional action.

## Your learning interpretation

Your distinction is reasonable: information may be available but entangled
in other features, making extraction difficult for a learner. That is a
plausible learning or conditioning hypothesis. AS2's exact matched histories
establish a stronger structural ambiguity in their particular family; they
do not establish training difficulty when the information is recoverable.

For an informative history, an analytically reconstructed A statistic is
derived from the same inputs, and a model-aware or learned rival may use it.
A later learning claim must compare matched inputs and disclosed resources.
Neither extraction difficulty nor a richer representation's success alone
establishes an information impossibility or gradient advantage. This
clarification changes no model and opens no additional training task.
