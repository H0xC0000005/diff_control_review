## 1. Assessment of the proposed structure

Keep the five-part sequence. Three refinements make it more precise:

1. **Make the task and the information comparison one explicit contract.**
   Before model details, define the road, initial cohort, action, complete
   objective and what each predictor knows. In this task, independent A/B
   initialization and the E-plus-continuing-total rival are central, even
   though neither is one of the four physical mechanisms you plan to explain.
2. **Show current model structure before its S13 differences.** A reader
   should understand the complete present task without reconstructing the
   sequence of changes. A short old/new table can orient readers familiar
   with S13; the four current components then receive a paper-style account.
3. **State the global modeling assumptions explicitly, then distinguish
   deductions and evidence still required.** Give the backbone assumptions
   their own subsection in Part 2, rather than leaving the reader to infer
   them from the components. A declared work/recovery closure, an algebraic
   consequence of that closure, and numerical or physical validation have

The aim is a compact scientific definition, not a compressed review diary.
Stage IDs, discarded alternatives, long proof chains and solver procedures
remain in the archive or supporting material. Essential domain restrictions
and branch rules remain visible because they determine the actual model.

### Assumptions, admissible domain and claim-specific conditions

The report must distinguish three levels of conditions rather than treating
every restriction as one global hypothesis:

1. **Backbone assumptions** define the represented dynamics and task setting.
   Each component refers to the applicable MA IDs and exposes any further
   local constitutive choice. Its source attribution is a separate statement.
2. **Sufficient operating bounds** select the presently justified domain:
   unblocked preparation, feasible receiving/storage, a unique service root
   and complete clearance. Their physical meaning belongs in the main text;
   exact inequalities and deductions belong in the supporting definition.
   Such bounds are not empirical calibration or a proof of validity outside
   the admitted domain.
3. **Additional conditions for a particular result** attach to that result.
   The matched-history construction has its own loading conditions; the
   positive action certificate may need large forced-merge work, weak
   mismatch and fast recovery; the ordinary full derivative needs its
   stated regular event conditions. None is silently imposed on every
   forward-admitted case. Nonregular and common-action cases remain included.

For example, homogeneous preparation is a backbone assumption. Its
conservative storage/underload restrictions delimit the present forward
argument. Large work in the positive action proof is an extra condition for
that proof, not an assumption that useful lane information always exists.
The report must not assume different optimal actions, useful gradients or
training difficulty in order to establish those claims.

Use a compact dependency record: **assumption or task restriction → affected
component/claim → additional conditions → established conclusion and limit**.
The main text carries the assumptions and each conclusion's important
qualifiers; a supporting table supplies the full dependency map. Conservation,
budget positivity, clearance and compact-peer equality are deductions to be
checked, not substitutes for root assumptions. The stronger no-requeue result
likewise retains its pulse/law/domain conditions; a finite cohort alone does
not establish it. If this transcription exposes an unmade scientific choice,
reopen the smallest affected global contract rather than inventing an

## 3. Provenance rules: label the actual equation or modeling choice

Use three primary lineage descriptions, with a separate distinction between
a model assumption and a result derived under it:

| Description | What it commits the report to say |
|---|---|
| **Reused from the literature** | Identify the source equation/construction, its relevant assumptions and how notation maps. Use this label only when the object is retained in that meaning; a borrowed ingredient does not mean the whole source algorithm is adopted. |
| **Adapted from the literature** | Identify the source object, the exact change made here and why the task needs it. State which source guarantee or interpretation does not automatically transfer. |
| **Defined in this study** | State the constitutive closure or task assumption explicitly, its purpose and limits. Prefer this wording to “invented” or “novel,” which would imply a separate novelty claim. Literature motivation may accompany it without turning it into a borrowed formula. |
| **Derived under these assumptions** | Mark an algebraic identity, bound, well-posedness argument, information result or sensitivity calculation as a deduction. Distinguish it from a physical law we chose to assume and from empirical validation. |

“Inspired by” can explain motivation, but it is too loose to settle the
equation's provenance. A rule inspired by a source still needs to be identified
as an adaptation or a closure defined here. Conversely, the study's work is
not limited to new constitutive equations: the diagnostic task, fair information
comparison, analysis and gradient-evidence contract are methodological work.

The following examples are supported by the existing audits. They illustrate
how the eventual report should label equations; they are not a completed
equation-by-equation manuscript audit or a new literature review.

| Backbone or component | Provenance that should be visible |
|---|---|
| **FD and longitudinal backbone** | Standard triangular-diagram and cumulative-boundary ingredients have literature lineage. Choosing the particular advice-response family, fixed wave/jam parameters and restricted operating domain is a study assumption, not an empirical consequence of citing an FD. |
| **Pre-merge** | Continuous conservation, own-lane priority and residual receiving work have source support. The homogeneous same-location exchange, physical interaction-length scaling and chosen offer rule are H1 adaptations/closures; Carey’s native diagonal CTM algorithm is not adopted unchanged. |
| **Terminal/point representation** | LTM-style travel, cumulative stock and vacancy supply the inherited structure. The preparation cutoff, finite terminal handoff and restriction of residual E to the choke are declared task/model choices. |
| **Forced-merge dynamics** | Receiving work and allocation are literature-grounded ideas. The fixed speed-mismatch work formula, representative queued marks and selected current-state priority/cooperation are the chosen constitutive completion; the sources do not supply this complete joined node law. |
| **Recovery and shared constraint** | The isolated accelerated-insertion expression has Leclercq lineage. Its capped loss profile is derived/adapted; E-only deposition and recent-mass renewal/retirement are selected aggregate closures, not the source's native many-event capacity law. |
| **Information and gradient results** | Independent-lane initialization and observation roles are task choices; compact sufficiency, matched histories, conditional action value and regular sensitivities are own deductions. Numerical correctness/usefulness and applied physical validity remain unexecuted evidence. |

For each displayed core equation, the source record should let us answer:
what is taken from the source, what changed, why, and what is assumed or proved
here? A compact main-text table can give those answers, with exact source
locations and derivations in a supporting matrix. Numbering should be local
to the new report rather than making the reader navigate old gate IDs.

## 4. A repeatable writing pattern for each component

Use the same semantic sequence, with enough prose to explain the equation:

1. **Traffic purpose.** What physical issue would the task miss without this component?
2. **Representation and interface.** What traffic state or history is retained, what enters, and what flow/service leaves?
3. **Core formulation.** Give the governing equation group and explain its terms, active limits and what happens to unserved traffic.
4. **Assumptions and provenance, separately.** Refer to the global MA IDs, state any additional local closure or operating restriction, and identify reused/adapted/defined-here pieces. A citation does not discharge the assumption statement.
5. **Consequence.** Explain the causal effect relevant to information, advice or the objective, without treating it as a validated performance result.

This pattern need not become five visible subheadings repeated four times.
Connected prose can carry it. A formulation should read as a model definition;
time-step instructions, data structures and API details belong to a later
execution document unless they change scientific meaning.

For example, a service subsection could open along these lines:

> The ending and adjacent streams compete for one service budget at the
> choke. A queued ending stream entering faster adjacent traffic may consume
> more work per unit mass. We represent that dependence with a fixed-coefficient
> speed-mismatch term. The receiving-work interpretation draws on the lane-changing
> literature; the particular mismatch function and current-state sharing rule
> are defined in this study. Their physical parameter range is not established
> by the cited sources.

The corresponding formula can then make that statement exact. This gives
Philipp a clear sensitivity-bearing dependency and gives Wang the traffic
meaning and provenance before either has to decode the full notation.

Keep notation local and economical. Define a symbol at first use, avoid
reusing A/B for model roles as well as lanes, distinguish flow from work and
physical stock from eligible queue, and keep the renewal mass separate from
forced E mass. Refer to equivalent recovery speed as the boundary quantity
it is, not a literal vehicle trajectory. Do not use a singleton meter analogy
to conceal the retained speed/age history.

Use an illustrative figure only if it resolves a reading problem. A single
labelled lane-drop schematic may help distinguish preparation cutoff,
finite terminal and choke completion; a second figure is warranted only if
the service/history feedback remains hard to explain. No plots are needed
merely to decorate the report, and no conceptual sketch should resemble an
unexecuted traffic experiment's result.
