## Decision details: possible closure outcomes, all unselected

The alternatives below make each question's implications explicit. They are
possible ways to close a gap, not six prescribed repairs or a selection of
new models. Recommendations concern the evidence needed before model choice.

### MP1 — What is the physical meaning and opportunity scale of the transfer law?

**Problem statement.** What does the fixed coefficient represent, and why
should that process determine how much merging is completed over the section?
How does changing common speed affect the accumulated amount?

**Assumed model-chain function.** Motion through the preparation section and
the merge-demand process offer transfers that change remaining mandatory
merging. Their action response feeds the later objective and gradient.

**Core gap.** S13 fixes normalized completion per elapsed time. Carey supplies
spatial desire specifications; Subraveti makes lateral demand a fraction of
sending demand. Neither inspected formulation identifies that same time law.
The speed-proportional cancellation example is a conditional diagnostic, not
a prediction of either whole source model.

**Possible closure 1 — justify the constant time law in a restricted regime.**
Give evidence that the relevant normalized completion process is approximately
unchanged with speed across a stated range. Keep its demand/completion meaning
explicit. **Pros:** preserves the exact elementary reference and its compact
state. **Cons:** no such joint evidence is presently admitted; matching one
speed does not establish the response to changing speed. **Dependencies and
consequences:** MP2 must establish feasible realization; an unsupported version
remains conditional and cannot carry the applied claim.

**Possible closure 2 — retain a source-supported demand/opportunity relation.**
Identify the source's native relation to progress, available sending or traffic
state, with an explicit common-speed adaptation where needed. **Pros:** makes
the physical interpretation traceable and permits a negative preparation
result. **Cons:** neither a spatial desire nor a sending fraction by itself
closes actual completion or the VSL mapping. **Dependencies and consequences:**
MP2–MP3 may require different state; adopting the relation requires G3 approval.

**Provisional recommendation.** Establish the coefficient's physical meaning
and its action response before choosing a law. Do not resolve this by changing
units or assuming that a speed-dependent coefficient alone is sufficient.

### MP2 — What turns merge demand into feasible completed transfer?

**Problem statement.** How much ending-lane traffic is available to move, and
how much can its target accept while also serving continuing traffic?

**Assumed model-chain function.** A sending/receiving allocation determines
actual paired transfers. Conservation and feasibility apply to those transfers,
including competition for the target's receiving opportunity.

**Core gap.** S13 conserves mass exactly but takes preparation receiving to be
sufficient. The sources distinguish intentions from realization. A fixed desired
share of sending can coexist with restrictive service; it is not the same as
unconditionally completed mass per unit time.

**Possible closure 1 — justify nonbinding preparation constraints.** Establish
that the proposed transfers are feasible throughout the declared state/action
range. **Pros:** can preserve a small reduced law with fewer states. **Cons:**
the range is not established; heterogeneous receiving can invalidate the
restriction. **Dependencies and consequences:** must be checked against MP1,
MP3 and MP4, rather than inferred from total mass or common speed.

**Possible closure 2 — represent the consequential sending/receiving limits.**
Use a declared source allocation to distinguish desired from realized transfers.
**Pros:** covers restrictive lane service and exposes its information needs.
**Cons:** can add state and active branches; no source scheme is automatically
gradient-admissible. **Dependencies and consequences:** approval is needed for
changed laws/state; MP3 determines whether a compact representation suffices.

**Provisional recommendation.** Identify feasibility conditions explicitly.
Choose between a justified nonbinding restriction and represented limits only
after seeing their consequences; do not add an arbitrary cap as a substitute
for that qualification.

### MP3 — Is the continuing-lane aggregation valid for merge service?

**Problem statement.** Does combined continuing mass determine what the
adjacent target can receive, or can different lane allocations change service?

**Assumed model-chain function.** The state retained by the reduced model must
determine the receiving opportunities used by its transfer and exit rules.

**Core gap.** The ending lane directly accesses its adjacent continuing lane.
Swapping crowded and lightly occupied continuing lanes leaves the combined
mass unchanged but can change immediate receiving. S13 imposes symmetry;
common speed does not establish or preserve the needed equivalence.

**Possible closure 1 — justify a shared-group restriction.** State conditions
under which adjacent receiving is determined by the grouped state and remains
so during transfers. **Pros:** preserves the elementary two-group comparison.
**Cons:** the symmetry or equivalence may be restrictive or fail dynamically.
**Dependencies and consequences:** must agree with MP2's allocation and MP6's
operating domain; unsupported aggregation cannot be repaired by notation.

**Possible closure 2 — retain the consequential receiving distinction.** Add
only the continuous information needed to distinguish the target's service
opportunity. **Pros:** avoids losing a causal lane distinction and allows a fair
compact rival. **Cons:** its sufficiency is unproved and it may require more
state than one scalar. **Dependencies and consequences:** G3 governs state and
G2 its observation; no universal need for full lane or vehicle identity follows.

**Provisional recommendation.** Test what the grouped state determines before
fixing the revised state. A valid restriction and a compact augmentation are
both legitimate answers.

### MP4 — Are upstream preparation and late merging treated consistently?

**Problem statement.** Under what conditions does early merging use spare
receiving while unfinished merging at the exit incurs consequential cost?

**Assumed model-chain function.** Transfers consume receiving opportunity at
their location; travel and interaction effects contribute to complete traffic
time. Earlier completion must be evaluated with the costs it causes upstream.

**Core gap.** The source receiving cost is associated with lane-changing entry
wherever it occurs. S13 counts added travel but assumes no additional early
receiving delay. The intended joint early-slack/late-costly regime is not
established over the proposed actions.

**Possible closure 1 — justify that joint restricted regime.** Demonstrate
sufficient upstream receiving alongside consequential late merging using one
consistent set of conditions. **Pros:** preserves the transparent preparation
tradeoff. **Cons:** separately assuming early slack and late scarcity does not
prove they coexist. **Dependencies and consequences:** MP1–MP3 and MP6 must
support the same state/action range; the restriction stays visible in claims.

**Possible closure 2 — account for relevant interaction costs in both places.**
Retain source-consistent resource and departure effects of early and late
transfers. **Pros:** can distinguish saved delay from relocated delay.
**Cons:** may complicate the reduced dynamics or remove the proposed benefit.
**Dependencies and consequences:** MP5 must compute consistent departures;
traffic changes require G3 approval while G4's complete-time objective remains.

**Provisional recommendation.** Evaluate the joint cost conditions. Do not
require early merging to help or redefine the objective to preserve its benefit.

### MP5 — Does the exit reduction determine the relevant average time?

**Problem statement.** Can a simultaneous batch with constant receiving and
proportional service represent the arrival/departure chronology of the task?

**Assumed model-chain function.** Departures determine how long each part of
the cohort remains in the system, and therefore mean/total complete time.
Final clearance is only one feature of that trajectory.

**Core gap.** The audit supports S13's co-clearance calculation when all demand
is exposed together, receiving is constant, the allocation is proportional and
no independent sending constraint binds. Other arrivals or priorities can
change mean time at the same final work; the physical batch regime is unqualified.

**Possible closure 1 — retain a justified batch/service reduction.** Specify
why the cohort and allocation satisfy the audit's conditions. **Pros:** keeps
exact, easily checked accounting. **Cons:** a coherent conditional projection
does not establish physical arrivals or allocation. **Dependencies and
consequences:** MP2–MP4 and MP6 must support these conditions together.

**Possible closure 2 — retain the consequential departure chronology.** Use
the smallest source-supported arrival/service description that determines
complete time. **Pros:** permits meaningful priority and timing effects.
**Cons:** can add continuous state/time structure and exceed the elementary
role. **Dependencies and consequences:** G3 governs traffic chronology, G4
accounting and G2 any changed observer/matching meaning; no spatial simulator
is selected by listing this outcome.

**Provisional recommendation.** Distinguish resource-work consistency from
average-time validity. Preserve the accepted batch as conditional until its
conditions or a bounded alternative are justified.

### MP6 — Is there a jointly defensible applied operating regime?

**Problem statement.** Which source/adaptation argument supports the chosen
transfer, grouping, cost and service assumptions together under common advice?

**Assumed model-chain function.** Geometry, initial traffic, realized-speed
response and the admissible action range define where the proposed small
traffic contract and its sensitivity apply.

**Core gap.** The inspected sources support ingredients but no entire admitted
combination. Ideal advice-to-realized-speed response is also a restriction;
congested or heterogeneous responses cannot silently inherit it. The review
does not establish a specific synchronized-flow law.

**Possible closure 1 — state a conditional analytical domain.** Keep the
assumptions explicit and limit the evidence claim accordingly. **Pros:** valid
and transparent, with S13 already supplying one such example. **Cons:** does
not fulfill an independently supported applied-traffic claim. **Dependencies
and consequences:** choosing this as the primary task would require an explicit
G1/G4 role decision; it is not implied by failed source qualification.

**Possible closure 2 — justify a joint applied domain for the revised reduction.**
Map native source ingredients, adaptations and restrictions to one consistent
operating range. **Pros:** permits a falsifiable traffic interpretation and
identifies precisely where a compact model applies. **Cons:** the available
evidence may be insufficient, or the justified range may lack the proposed
benefit. **Dependencies and consequences:** MP1–MP5 remain provisional until
this consistency check; a major expansion returns to the elementary-role gate.

**Provisional recommendation.** Require joint justification for an applied
claim. Record an unresolved or conditional outcome honestly instead of selecting
laws to obtain a positive gradient result.
