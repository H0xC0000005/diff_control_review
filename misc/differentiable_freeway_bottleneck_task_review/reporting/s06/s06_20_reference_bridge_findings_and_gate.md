## Brief semantic findings

**There is a defensible mechanism basis, but not yet evidence that the proposed
extra information changes useful common-advice decisions.** The new review
narrows the mechanism, strengthens the aggregate alternative, and identifies
a preparation flaw before any experiment or invented model is warranted.

1. **Merging need not be represented as a fixed penalty.** Native lane-changing
   models couple urgency, safety gaps and speed adjustments. That supplies a
   plausible response/service path, not proof of beneficial slowing or hidden
   information. Native discrete behavior is reference methodology, not permission
   to import resets into our continuous-mass model. [Erdmann's primary description](https://elib.dlr.de/102254/1/Springer-SUMOs_Lane_changing_model.pdf).
2. **Slower traffic can redistribute lanes without reliably improving merging.**
   Field evidence shows competing utilization and gap effects; it does not provide
   a universal beneficial sign. [Knoop et al.'s primary paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224190648_Lane_distribution_of_traffic_near_merging_zones_influence_of_variable_speed_limits).
3. **A lane-drop precedent does not automatically transfer to this advice range.**
   A new Aimsun/CTM study uses strong regulatory restriction and discharge scoring;
   its low-limit profiles and prescribed aggregate capacity-drop closure do not
   certify our moderate voluntary response, all-cohort outcome or missing-information
   claim. [Zhang et al.](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/92568/1/Zhang_Optimization_Speed_Freeway.pdf).
4. **The strong aggregate alternative remains credible.** The archived advisory
   report uses aggregate feedback and reports improvements in a richer simulator.
   Another field study finds lane fractions largely track aggregate occupancy
   when occupancy is held comparable. Neither proves aggregate sufficiency, but
   both caution against equating lane effects with lost information.
   [Advisory report](../../archive/full_text/FB0005.pdf),
   [occupancy-conditioned field study](../../archive/full_text/FB0039.pdf).
5. **The prospective preparation times do not support the intended near-drop
   contrast well.** Under road-start entry and the intended100km/h ceiling,
   no entrant can reach the final400m at40 or60s. At80s the continuous-time
   ceiling allows at most three entrants, or one with ordinary zero-speed entry
   and the declared acceleration cap. This is an original conditional design
   screen, not measured matching failure. Departure details need explicit
   specification; no settings have been changed.

The [source audit](../../extraction/s06_reference_mechanism_source_audit.md)
contains methods, accounting qualifications, manuscript identities and the
timing screen's assumptions. The sources do not justify a minimum state,
capacity-drop switch, prescribed merge penalty or useful gradient.

## What can be retained for later modeling?

Retain a conservation-law propagation backbone and shared desired-response
semantics. If refinement earns a role, model finite lateral opportunity and
receiving/storage constraints with conservative donor/receiver transfers;
couple them to endogenous response rather than an arbitrary penalty. Ending-lane
mass, its spatial distribution and continuing-lane balance remain candidate
statistics. Gap/cooperation/history evidence means lane mass alone is not yet
shown sufficient. These are source-informed design directions, not adopted
equations or an approved differentiable implementation.

## S06-UG1 / DG-R3 — Where should consequential hidden contrast be prepared?

**Problem statement:** Should the next documentary study retain an immediate
near-drop hidden-state contrast and review a reachable preparation contract, or
allow an earlier upstream contrast whose later consequences must be established?

**Assumed model-chain function:** Reachable preparation supplies two states with
identical strong predecision observations but different retained information;
common advice then tests decision relevance through native merging and complete
waiting accounting. Preparation must work before a model or gradient is justified.

**Core gap:** The approved40/60/80s schedule is poorly aligned with its final400m
requirement. Moving later may produce interaction-rich states but also observable
speed/history differences; using earlier upstream contrast changes the spatial
evidence contract. Literature supplies neither joint certificate. This is a
scientific preparation choice, not an engine-installation problem.

**Candidate A — retain near-drop contrast; review a revised reachable preparation
contract (provisional recommendation).** Conduct a bounded documentary/analytical
reachability and observability study first. Specify road-entry position/speed,
travel/response bounds, preparation duration and the native anticipation scope;
derive admissible timing ranges before proposing a small preparation bank.
Keep exact full-history matching, advice response/range and outcome accounting.
Do not simply choose a later time and assert a match or benefit.

**Pros:** Preserves the intended local-refinement question; removes avoidably
empty snapshots; makes the tension between useful interactions and visible
histories explicit before any empirical/model work.

**Cons:** A later interacting preparation may not admit exact matching. Literature
may only establish bounds or a conditional obstruction, not a native certificate.
Further preparation review has a real evidence cost.

**Dependencies/consequences:** Reopen DG-G4's timing/preparation settings and
DG-C3-Q1 for a proposed revision, not G2's exact information claim. Freeze any
revised timing/bank/accounting contract only after user approval. No server checks,
experiments, held100 amendment or traffic-model construction are included.

**Candidate B — review earlier upstream hidden contrast with a longer downstream
consequence horizon.** Replace the immediate final400m contrast requirement with
a reachable upstream lane contrast at the decision time. Require that it persists
or causally changes later merging and common-advice outcomes, with the complete
predecision channel, identical known future inputs and all waiting retained.
The S05 free-flow construction is a conditional starting point, not native evidence.

**Pros:** Separates information hiding from later interaction; avoids requiring
the observation collision to occur where merging has already altered visible speed.
Can test whether an initially invisible difference acquires later decision value.

**Cons:** Persistence and advice relevance remain additional obligations. This
tests transport of hidden information, not necessarily a minimal near-drop state;
longer horizons and anticipation can erase the contrast. It is not an approved
fallback when A fails and does not establish lane mass as sufficient.

**Dependencies/consequences:** Explicitly amend the spatial preparation contract
under G1/G2/G3 and dependent C3/C4; retain exactness, disclosed hidden past inputs
and complete accounting. Documentary planning only; numerical work remains deferred.

**Candidate C — stop with the supported mechanism basis and defer the certificate.**
Archive the response/merge precedents and unresolved information-value link without
adopting a refinement or spending further effort on preparation.

**Pros:** Bounds cost; preserves defensible positive mechanism evidence without
turning it into an unsupported representation claim.

**Cons:** Leaves the accepted conditional contribution untested; no aggregate
sufficiency or task-wide impossibility conclusion follows.

**Dependencies/consequences:** Explicit deferral; no model, experiment or gradients.

**Provisional recommendation: A.** It changes the smallest affected preparation
contract and remains closest to the accepted local-resolution question. B is a
genuine alternative if the upstream information-propagation question is preferred,
not merely a procedural workaround. No candidate is selected by this report.
