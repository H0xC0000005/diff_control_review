## Objective

Determine how far published richer-reference evidence bridges the missing link:
common infrastructure desired-speed advice changes endogenous merging, receiving
or service enough to alter useful decisions, specifically using information a
strong aggregate response/history model cannot recover. Do not assume that
positive speed-control results establish the second, information-value claim.

## Fixed boundaries

- Same common link-level advice across lanes and compared resolutions; native
  drivers determine actual motion. Regulatory limits, lane-specific advice,
  direct CAV trajectories and commanded merging are boundary evidence only.
- Fixed/exogenous lane-capacity reduction remains the task anchor. On-ramp and
  general freeway evidence may inform mechanisms but is not lane-drop validation.
- Preserve spatial aggregate mass/density, mean response/speed, cumulative counts,
  storage, source waiting and necessary full histories. Do not use a weak
  memoryless model to manufacture a missing-information claim.
- Gap 7 remains fixed: no adopted control-dependent categorical mass shifts,
  discrete resets, smoothing, source repairs or invented penalty law.
- Literature/methodology only: no host/provider checks, configuration,
  installation, simulator/runner implementation, calibration or traffic runs.
  Empirical A, held100/cap504, model construction and gradients remain deferred.

## Bounded execution

1. Reuse the archived advisory field/microsimulation report and lane-distribution,
   lateral-control and lane-change studies already screened in this pack. Inspect
   primary methods/results where a stronger mechanism claim requires them.
2. Inspect a primary native lane-changing model description and bounded primary
   lane-distribution/merging evidence. Search three focused families: common
   speed advice with native merging; speed restriction and lane distribution;
   lane-drop speed control with aggregate versus richer-state observations.
   Reuse prior query/source logs before repeating discovery.
3. Extract separately: actuator; retained observations; native versus commanded
   lane changes; plausible service-changing path; measured outcome/accounting;
   causal attribution; history/matched-information test; derivative evidence.
   Classify evidence as direct, mechanism-only, boundary-only or unresolved.
4. Check whether evidence suggests that full history could reveal the supposed
   hidden distinction. A native mechanism is not an exact information collision;
   a speed-control benefit is not proof that a lane refinement is needed.
5. Synthesize a brief semantic findings report, with inspectable source audit,
   query/access records and a work/check report. Prefer a general supported
   coupling over an assumed merging penalty; negative or unresolved results
   remain admissible.

Online primary sources and existing verified full texts are sufficient for
methodology review; no installed SUMO is needed. Rolling documentation is not a
verified version-specific API or empirical finding. New sources are kept separate
from closed FB/source registers unless explicitly archived with provenance.
