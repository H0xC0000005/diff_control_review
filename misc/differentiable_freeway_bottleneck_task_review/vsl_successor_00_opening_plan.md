## Traffic question and intended contribution

At a lane drop, slower common speed advice costs travel time but may give
traffic in the ending lane more time to merge before it reaches the choke.
If unfinished merging consumes scarce receiving capacity, that preparation can
reduce subsequent delay. Two traffic preparations can therefore call for
different speed changes even when the declared aggregate observer sees the same
history. The first verification should establish which information is needed
to predict that tradeoff, then check whether the resulting sensitivity suggests
a worthwhile intervention.

The bounded task is:

> Determine whether a strong aggregate VSL predictor needs a merge-preparation
> statistic to predict useful common-speed perturbations at one lane drop, and
> whether the ending/continuing grouped model adds anything beyond that statistic.

S13 already proves a **conditional analytical possibility**, including an action
split and state-dependent analytical gradient. It does not establish the applied
answer. Its current equations can be reproduced by total mass plus one continuous
initial ending-lane mass, evolved autonomously under the same law. Equality with
that compact rival is an expected verification result, not a disappointing
outcome to engineer away.

VSL supplies the first, transparent information/gradient verification. The urban
task then asks the more specific question about discrete movement order and
finite access. Their common sequence is retained information → changed service
and exit timing → checked sensitivity → independently evaluated perturbation.
This connection does not amend the project's discrete-order hypothesis or make
VSL a second certificate of discrete-order necessity.

Feedback 2 makes the claim priority explicit: **useful, information-sensitive
control gradients first; novelty of the precise verification second.** Newness
of VSL, the traffic task, gradient optimization in general or the modeling
stack is not a contribution premise. The primary applied target is reliable
guidance for common-speed changes, checked under complete accounting in an
independently qualified traffic evaluator. S13 currently supports only its
conditional analytical counterpart.

An advantage over an observer with less traffic information is an information-
to-control finding, not by itself evidence that gradient-based optimization is
superior. Such superiority requires a same-model, equal-information optimizer
comparison. Useful gradients may coexist with an equally effective compact
model, analytical solution or direct search. Optimization instability, elaborate
repairs or lack of a discovered peer are neither required results nor substitutes
for usefulness evidence. The scientific checks still proceed forward-first:
claim priority does not replace their dependency order.

## Strong simpler competitors

The first comparison has three mandatory information roles under the same
admitted traffic law and common physical action:

| Role | Initial information and autonomous prediction | Scientific purpose |
|---|---|---|
| A: strong aggregate observer | Full declared aggregate density/flow/speed/count history and common parameters; any aggregate-derived state or shared inference rule must use that history alone | Test the consequence of information omitted by that observer, without weakening it to a density snapshot or fixed capacity |
| A+E: aggregate plus merge statistic | A plus initial ending-lane mass, or its equivalent fraction; predicts remaining merge mass for every proposed speed using its own dynamics | Test whether one continuous statistic repairs the omission |
| G: ending/continuing grouped state | The same initial local information as A+E, represented explicitly as two conservative groups | Test compression at equal information; must reproduce A+E in S13 |

The A versus A+E contrast deliberately adds initial information. Name it as an
information contrast, not a pure discretization improvement. A+E versus G tests
representation at equal information. Supplying post-action remaining mass from
the reference would invalidate both tests. If the statistic can be recovered
from admitted aggregate history in the concrete task, A must receive that
recovery too; a reduced snapshot does not establish full-history ambiguity.

Established aggregate VSL/history/capacity-drop and lane-aware models must also
screen the claim. Wei–Han–Wang's link model is the nearest first-order aggregate
candidate; Jin–Jin's lane-drop model and METANET VSL/MPC are serious context and
possible separate model/controller challengers. Carey and Subraveti supply
continuous lane/mandatory-transfer alternatives. Their different laws cannot
silently enter the matched ladder: compare a faithfully specified source model
as a separate model-form test, with every action/accounting adaptation exposed.
Do not combine their strongest features into an invented baseline.

Because the action is one scalar, S13's analytical optimum and a bounded
one-dimensional direct search are strong optimizer references. Correct/useful
gradients need not be necessary for finding this task's optimum. Optimizer speed
or superiority is a separate claim with separate matched budgets.

Initially observed merge mass is a legitimate compact augmentation, not an
unfair repair. If it closes the guidance gap, record the minimum information
finding. No present result establishes a peer's noisy/unstable optimization or
local-optimum failures; those are optional hypotheses requiring their own fair

## What the eventual verification must establish

First check conservation, finite receiving, complete population-time accounting
and exact A+E/G equivalence in the inherited member. Then check analytical
derivatives against AD and central directional differences in double precision
inside stable regimes. At action endpoints use feasible one-sided perturbations;
do not request an infeasible speed increase at the upper bound. Report active-set
changes separately and preserve boundary cases in forward evaluation.

Only afterward evaluate identical small speed perturbations in an independently
qualified richer model. Record predicted sign, magnitude/ranking and realized
complete-time change separately. A shared-equation reference checks transcription;
it does not establish traffic validity. SUMO is a candidate for documentary
fitness review, not selected software or an assumed derivative oracle.

Before later scientific runs, an approved protocol must freeze calibration and
held-out separation, admissible parameter domain, tolerances, perturbation
sizes, seeds if needed, common budgets and clearance-failure treatment. This
opening does not guess those numbers or borrow the urban numerical defaults.
No stochastic scenario generation or traffic experiment is performed now.

finding. Correct internal gradients failing independent perturbation evaluation
are a negative transfer finding. Failure of the transfer/service or reachability

an applied task claim. Broader laws, new kinetics, an altered observer/objective
or an expanded controller require the smallest affected global decision to be
reopened before work continues.
