# Response, lane and derivative evidence boundaries

Inspection date 2026-09-16. E3 means full text at stated locations, not verified
useful sensitivities. These records supplement the individual predecessor studies.

## FB0001 / FB0022 / FB0020: baseline and transfer qualifications

FB0001 archived October29-2018 manuscript of arXiv1310.2658, pages6–8,
equations5/9: discontinuous lane-drop discharge despite continuous density/FD.
Its idealized no-drop case constrains generic VSL-benefit claims; not evidence
that imperfect-advice/recovery/merge mechanisms never matter.

FB0022 official Wiley 2025 full HTML, sections2.1.1/3.2–3.3 and equation10:
entry-time speed histories and cumulative counts in homogeneous links; scalar
noncompliance, no LC/overtaking. Distance-triggered binary exit selection and
duration-triggered CD require derivative scrutiny. Section3.2 supplies a SUMO
forward comparison; section3.3 constant-limit US101/NGSIM drop assessment does
not validate a changing-advice/temporary-closure/gradient contract jointly.

FB0020 Zhang/Ioannou, DOI10.1080/23307706.2017.1402715, author full-web
introduction and sections3.1–3.3: macro–micro VSL discrepancy already recognized;
integrated RM/LC/VSL control and a hard capacity-drop threshold are not direct
matched-action evidence for retained response classes or lane state. Results
not deeply extracted or used to infer state-only gains in N2.

## FB0003 / FB0005: response does not identify cohort necessity

FB0003 final 2018 IFAC paper, sections 3/6/7, equations 4 and field FD figures:
scalar noncompliance modifies aggregate desired speed/FD. Density-dependent
response can occur without immutable population classes. FB0026 is its fuller
2019 extension, not independent evidence.

FB0005 official 2018 advisory field report: PDF page 39 (printed 21), Table 3.2,
simulation compliance 10/25/50% gives TTT changes -2.53/-3.3/-4.84%; -3.56% is
their average. Nearby prose misattributes approximately -3.5% to 50%. Section
8.3, printed 66, defines a rolling aggregate speed-discrepancy 'compliance' metric,
not an identified person-level membership fraction. Sections 8.5–9.1 describe
learning/context and before/after results, not a controlled resolution comparison.
Neither field metric nor nominal simulation fraction fixes B1 class weights.

## FB0024 / FB0004: deterministic transfer versus actuator confounding

FB0024 Chen/Cheng/Chang (2021), DOI 10.1061/JTEPBS.0000481,
[university full PDF](https://attap.umd.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JTEPBS.0000481.pdf),
methods/evaluation inspected. Equations 7–11 (PDF pages 3–4) model endogenous
density-imbalance lateral volumes limited by sending/receiving room, with paired
mass accounting and lane-group weighting. Units and group-width conventions
need transcription checks. On-ramp imbalance, not a complete mandatory-closure
law; field speed improvement does not establish action-ranking improvement.

FB0004 Guo et al. (2020), DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2020.2981658,
[author full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/340013753_Integrated_Variable_Speed_Limits_and_Lane-Changing_Control_for_Freeway_Lane-Drop_Bottlenecks),
sections II–III/equations 1–12 inspected. Lane/class subcells, cars/trucks in an
all-CAV population, dynamic passenger-car equivalence, lane-specific speeds AND
commanded lane-transfer volumes, GA/MPC with SUMO. Lateral paired flows are
transferable accounting ideas; joint-control gains are not evidence that our
shared link-advice action needs B2. 'Flow ratio' is not automatically random
sampling. Empty-traffic and FD mode conventions require later audit.

## FB0002 / RM0025 / SEO2026: derivative target must match the claim

FB0002, arXiv:1603.04785, selected sections 2.1–2.3/optimization results and
appendix: free-flow homogeneous LWR, road-wide maximum-speed function, L2
outflow tracking. Needle-variation derivatives include transported density-jump
terms; not random categorical shifts and not an independent microscopic check.
Global road actuation and free-flow restriction limit direct F1 transfer.

RM0025 reused in place from the ramp-metering archive, DOI
10.1007/s11081-026-10122-4, published August 20, 2026 as verified on
[Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11081-026-10122-4).
Section 4 includes class speed/routing/priority controls. Section 5's numerical
adjoint/finite-difference checks concern distribution/routing controls; do not
claim the paper demonstrated external VSL gradient transfer. General
piecewise/discrete-adjoint lineage is established, not our minimum-state result.
SEO2026/RM0024 remain inherited general derivative lineage; no new task-level
result or latest-version technical claim is adopted from them in N2.

## FB0033: recent work-zone evaluator calibration

Cao et al. (2026), DOI 10.3390/app16147091, published July 15, 2026,
verified via [publisher version notes](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/14/7091/notes).
Publisher-provided [full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/410108475_Three-Layer_Model_Calibration_for_SUMO_A_Study_on_Speed-Limit_Compliance_in_Chinese_Work_Zones)
sections 3.3/4.1/5.3–5.4/6 and data statement inspected. SUMO individual speed
factors drawn from an exogenous truncated-normal population; calibration adds
mean/spread to IDM/LC2013 parameters. Static 60 km/h work-zone limit, maxSpeed
120 km/h; realized speeds also depend on interactions. Table 10 compares
two/three-layer mean speed RMSEs 23.66/5.68 and 25.22/6.71 km/h over three seeds.
Section4.3 explicitly uses the same dataset for calibration/evaluation, without
independent validation. One site, fixed-limit forward fit, no dynamic-advice or
candidate-gradient test; data by request. Supports a concrete calibration strategy, not B1
necessity or permission for control-dependent class sampling.
